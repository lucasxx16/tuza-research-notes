ROUND 3 / D: A HUMAN REDUCIBILITY PROOF FOR THE COMPLETE FOUR-VERTEX CORE

Status: complete proof; adversarial reviewer C accepted the argument after
line-by-line review of both lemmas and both certificate constructions.
The argument was developed independently of the saved K4 certificates and
other new mainline routes. No census, certificate finder, or numerical
experiment is used. The source-paper definitions and the previously known
2K2 argument are the only mathematical inputs beyond elementary facts.

THEOREM.
Let u,v be adjacent vertices in a finite simple graph. Let
C=N(u) intersect N(v),
A=N(u) minus ({v} union C),
B=N(v) minus ({u} union C).
Suppose |C|=4, |A|=|B|=2, H=G[C]=K4, both links G[N(u)] and
G[N(v)] are connected and non-WKE, and
    deg_H(c)+deg_A(c)+deg_B(c)<=5  for every c in C.
Then {u,v} is reducible using six edge-disjoint triangles and twelve cover
edges. The assertion permits arbitrary A--B edges and arbitrary edges from
the displayed vertices to the rest of G.

Notation. A graph F is WKE if it has a matching M and vertex set Q with
|Q|<=|M| such that every edge of F with both endpoints outside Q belongs
to M. Equivalently, Q covers F-M. A certificate for {u,v} consists of an
edge-disjoint triangle set S and an edge set X satisfying |X|<=2|S|,
meeting every triangle containing u or v, and containing every edge of S
whose endpoints both avoid u,v. We write xyz for triangle {x,y,z}.

LEMMA 1: THE LINK CHARACTERIZATION.
Let F have vertices {z} union C union {a,a'}, where {z} union C induces
K5, neither za nor za' is present, and the remaining possible edges are
C--{a,a'} attachments and aa'. If F is connected, then F is non-WKE if
and only if every c in C has a neighbor in {a,a'}.

Proof. First suppose a core vertex c has no such neighbor. Put Q=C\{c},
so |Q|=3. The edges outside Q are zc and, if present, aa'. If aa' exists,
choose any two vertices x,y of Q; the matching {zc,aa',xy} has size three
and contains every edge outside Q. If aa' is absent, connectedness gives
an attachment a* x for some a* in {a,a'} and x in Q. Write y,w for the
other vertices of Q. The matching {zc,a*x,yw} again has size three and
contains every edge outside Q. Either matching with Q witnesses WKE.

Conversely, suppose every core vertex has an attachment and a WKE pair
(M,Q) exists. A matching on seven vertices has size at most three, so
|Q|<=3. As F-Q is contained in M, it has maximum degree at most one.
Consequently Q must remove at least three vertices of the K5: otherwise
three remaining vertices induce a triangle. Hence Q consists of exactly
three K5 vertices and contains no exclusive vertex. The two remaining
K5 vertices are adjacent and at least one is a core vertex c. Its edge
to an exclusive vertex is also outside Q, giving degree at least two in
F-Q, a contradiction. This proves the lemma.

Apply Lemma 1 to both links. Every c has at least one A-neighbor and at
least one B-neighbor. Since deg_H(c)=3, the degree budget gives
deg_A(c)+deg_B(c)<=2. Therefore each c has exactly one neighbor in A and
exactly one in B. Denote them by alpha(c) and beta(c). Thus alpha:C->A
and beta:C->B are two maps, with possibly empty fibers. Edges inside A
and B remain arbitrary. Connectedness requires a side edge if one fiber
is empty, but the constructions below do not need to use that fact.

LEMMA 2: ARRANGING TWO BINARY PARTITIONS.
Let alpha:C->A and beta:C->B, with |C|=4 and |A|=|B|=2. Exactly one of
the following alternatives applies:
(G) The vertices of C can be named x,y,z,w, and one vertex a- in A and
    one vertex b- in B can be chosen, so that
        alpha^{-1}(a-) is contained in {w,z},
        beta^{-1}(b-)  is contained in {w,y}.
(E) Both maps have two fibers of size two, and their partitions of C
    into fibers are identical, after exchanging the names in a side.

Proof. If both maps have fibers of size two and their partitions differ,
choose any fiber P of alpha and any fiber Q of beta. Their intersection
has size one: intersection zero or two would make the partitions equal.
Let w be the common vertex, z the other vertex of P, y the other vertex
of Q, and x the fourth vertex. This is (G).

If exactly one map has two fibers of size two, exchange the two maps
temporarily if needed so that it is alpha. Choose any size-two fiber P
of alpha and a fiber Q of beta of size at most one. If Q={q} and q is
in P, choose w=q, let z be the other vertex of P, and choose y outside
P. If Q={q} and q is outside P, choose w,z as the two vertices of P and
let y=q. If Q is empty, choose w,z as the two vertices of P and any y
outside P. In every case the remaining vertex is x, and the required
containments hold. Interchanging the maps interchanges the roles of y,z,
so this argument also handles the initially exchanged case.

If neither map has two fibers of size two, each has a fiber of size at
most one. Choose those fibers P,Q. If they are distinct nonempty
singletons, put z in P, y in Q, and choose w outside their union. If
they are the same singleton, put that vertex at w and choose distinct
y,z elsewhere. If exactly one is nonempty, put its vertex at w and
choose distinct y,z elsewhere. If both are empty, choose any distinct
w,y,z. Again the remaining vertex is x and (G) holds.

Finally, (G) is impossible for identical balanced partitions: its two
selected fibers would have to be exactly {w,z} and {w,y}, which intersect
in exactly one vertex; fibers of identical partitions are equal or
disjoint. Thus (G) and (E) are disjoint and exhaustive.

GENERIC SIX-TRIANGLE CONSTRUCTION (G).
Use the labels and choices in (G). Let a+ be the other vertex of A and
b+ the other vertex of B. Define

 S = { xyz, uvx, uyw, vzw, uz alpha(z), vy beta(y) },

 R = { xy,xz,yz,yw,zw,z alpha(z),y beta(y) },

 X = R union { uv,uw,vw,u a+,v b+ }.

All the listed triangles exist. The sole triangle avoiding the hubs is
xyz. At u, the other triangles use the disjoint spoke sets
{x}, {y,w}, {z,alpha(z)}; at v, they use {x}, {z,w}, {y,beta(y)}.
The central triangle uses uv only once. Their external edges are the
seven distinct edges of R: three from xyz and one from each of the four
one-hub triangles. No edge in R is reused. Hence S is an edge-disjoint
six-triangle packing. The five additional edges of X are distinct hub
edges, disjoint from R, so |X|=7+5=12=2|S|. R contains every external
packing edge by definition.

To verify coverage, every triangle containing both hubs is met by uv.
For a triangle through u but not v, its other two vertices are in C union
A. If both lie in C, their edge is in R except possibly xw, and that
exception is met by uw. If one is a+, its spoke u a+ meets the triangle.
If one is a-, its core neighbor must lie in {w,z}; at w the edge uw
meets the triangle, and at z its edge z a-=z alpha(z) belongs to R.
A triangle using both vertices of A is met by u a+. These possibilities
cover every triangle through u. For v, the same argument uses vw,
v b+, and the containment beta^{-1}(b-) subset {w,y}; an attachment at
y is the edge y beta(y) in R. Thus X meets every required triangle.

EXCEPTIONAL SIX-TRIANGLE CONSTRUCTION (E).
Write the common two-part partition as {x,y} union {z,w}, and name the
exclusive vertices so that
 alpha(x)=alpha(y)=a,  alpha(z)=alpha(w)=a',
 beta(x)=beta(y)=b,    beta(z)=beta(w)=b'.
Define a vertex d by
 d=a if aa' is present;  d=w if aa' is absent.
Thus a'd is an existing edge in either case. Put

 S = { axy, uvx, uyz, u a'd, vyb, vzw },

 R = { ax,ay,xy,yz,a'd,yb,zw },

 X = R union { uz,uw,vx,vy,vb' }.

All six triangles exist. The first uses only the external edges ax,ay,xy.
At u the hub triangles use disjoint spoke sets {x}, {y,z}, {a',d}:
d is either a or w, so the disjointness holds in both cases. At v they
use disjoint spoke sets {x}, {y,b}, {z,w}. The seven external edges in
R are pairwise distinct, uv occurs once, and u-spokes and v-spokes are
distinct. Thus S is edge-disjoint. Again |R|=7, the five added hub
edges are distinct from R, |X|=12=2|S|, and every external packing edge
is in X.

The four triangles containing both hubs are met, respectively, by
vx (for core vertex x), vy (for y), uz (for z), and uw (for w).
For triangles using one hub and two core vertices, R contains the core
edges xy,yz,zw. The remaining edges are xz,xw,yw. At u they are met by
the spokes uz,uw,uw, respectively; at v they are met by vx,vx,vy.
The A-attachments ax,ay are themselves in R; the A-attachments a'z,a'w
are met at u by uz,uw. If aa' is present, a'd=aa' is in R; if absent,
there is no triangle of that type. The B-attachments bx,by are met at
v by vx,vy, and the B-attachments b'z,b'w are met by vb'. A possible
edge bb' is also harmless because its triangle through v is met by vb'.
This exhausts all triangles containing a hub and proves coverage.

AMBIENT SAFETY AND CONCLUSION.
A triangle through u uses only vertices in N(u)={v} union C union A;
a triangle through v uses only vertices in N(v)={u} union C union B.
Thus edges between A and B cannot create additional hub triangles, nor
can edges to vertices outside the displayed neighborhood. Each recipe
uses only edges proved present and covers all possible hub triangles.
It contains in X every packing edge avoiding the hubs. Therefore either
recipe is a valid reducibility certificate in the ambient graph G.
Lemmas 1 and 2 show that one always applies, completing the theorem.

SCOPE.
This proves the complete K4-core case under the stated hypotheses,
including the configurations represented by all 21 saved K4 records.
The count 21 and those records are not used in the proof. The other
eight nonempty core types are outside this result; no overall template
minimality claim is made. A stronger observation established here is that
six triangles suffice for every admissible K4-core configuration, whether
or not the chosen saved certificate uses more.
