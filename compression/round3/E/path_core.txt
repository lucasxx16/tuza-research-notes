HUMAN STRUCTURAL PROOF FOR THE FOUR-VERTEX PATH CORE

Status: census-free complete P4-core proof, using a general attachment
support lemma for cores with a perfect matching. No saved graph,
certificate, side-mask list, or graph enumeration is a premise.

1. SETUP

There are adjacent hubs u,v, a four-vertex common set C, and two
exclusive pairs A={a0,a1}, B={b0,b1}. In a link write z for the
opposite hub and a0,a1 for that link's private vertices. The vertex
z is adjacent to every vertex of C and to neither private vertex.
The core is H, and only core-private edges and the private edge
a0a1 are possible in addition to H and the z-spokes.

A graph F is WKE if there are a matching M and a set Q of vertices
with |Q| <= |M| such that every edge with both endpoints outside Q
belongs to M. Equivalently, Q is a vertex cover of F-M.

The attachment support of one private pair is the set of core
vertices having at least one neighbor in that pair.

2. A PERFECT-MATCHING CORE FORCES THREE ATTACHED VERTICES

Lemma M. If H has a perfect matching, and the link is connected and
not WKE, its attachment support has size at least three.

Proof. Suppose the attachment support is contained in a two-element
set T={x,y} in C. If it has fewer than two elements, enlarge it
arbitrarily to such a T. Put Q={z,x,y}.

The vertices outside Q are the two core vertices C\T and a0,a1.
No core-private edge lies outside Q. Thus the only possible edges
outside Q are the core edge e joining the two vertices of C\T,
and the private edge a0a1. We construct a matching of size three
containing each of these two edges that is present.

Case 1: e and a0a1 are both present.
Take M={e,a0a1,zx}. Its edges are disjoint, and it contains all
edges outside Q.

Case 2: e is present and a0a1 is absent.
Connectivity implies that a private vertex has a core neighbor.
Such a neighbor lies in T; relabel T as necessary so that xa_i
is an edge. Take M={e,xa_i,zy}. These edges are disjoint and
contain the only edge e outside Q.

Case 3: e is absent and a0a1 is present.
Take the two edges of any perfect matching of H, together with
a0a1. This gives a three-edge matching containing the only edge
a0a1 outside Q.

Case 4: neither e nor a0a1 is present.
Connectivity gives a private-core edge ca_i. Let P be a perfect
matching of H, and let f be its edge not incident with c. Let d
be the fourth core vertex, outside {c} union V(f). Then
M={ca_i,f,zd} is a three-edge matching. There are no edges outside
Q, so the required containment is automatic.

In all four cases |Q|=|M|=3 and Q covers F-M, contrary to non-WKE.
This proves the lemma.

3. A DISTANCE-TWO ATTACHMENT CONSTRUCTION

Lemma D. Let H be precisely the path p-q-r-s. Suppose pa and rb
are present, with a in A and b in B. Then {u,v} is reducible by
a packing of five triangles and a cover of ten edges. No other
attachment restriction or link hypothesis is required.

Proof. Set

  S={uvs, uqr, upa, vpq, vrb},
  X={uv,ua0,ua1,vb0,vb1,pq,qr,rs,pa,rb}.

All five triangles are present. At u, the u-only triangles have
disjoint rim pairs {q,r} and {p,a}; at v they have disjoint rim
pairs {p,q} and {r,b}. None uses a spoke to s. Their four rim
edges qr,pa,pq,rb are distinct, so the five triangles are
edge-disjoint. Thus |S|=5, while all ten listed X-edges are distinct,
giving |X|=10=2|S|.

A triangle through both hubs meets uv. A triangle through just u
whose rim includes an A-vertex meets one of ua0,ua1. Otherwise its
rim edge lies in the core and is one of pq,qr,rs, all in X. The
same argument at v uses vb0,vb1. Therefore X meets every triangle
through either hub. The only packed edges avoiding both hubs are
qr,pa,pq,rb, all contained in X.

This verifies the ambient safety condition as well: every triangle
through a hub uses only vertices in its neighborhood. A-B edges
and edges leaving the local neighborhood cannot create another kind
of hub triangle. The displayed triangles remain present in every
ambient graph containing this local graph.

The same construction applies when the A and B attachment vertices
are q and s, or after exchanging the two hubs: relabel the path
in reverse order and/or exchange the two sides.

4. COMPLETE PATH-CORE THEOREM

Theorem. If H=P4 and both links are connected and non-WKE, the
pair {u,v} is reducible. The common-vertex degree budget is not
needed.

Proof. Write the path as p-q-r-s, and let S_A,S_B be the two
attachment supports. The core has the perfect matching {pq,rs},
so Lemma M gives |S_A| >= 3 and |S_B| >= 3.

Define the involution i on C by exchanging p with r and q with s.
Thus c and i(c) are exactly the pairs at distance two on the path.
If there were no c in S_A with i(c) in S_B, then

  i(S_A) is a subset of C\S_B.

The left set has at least three elements and the right set at most
one, an impossibility. Hence there are opposite-side attachment
vertices at distance two. Every such pair contains one endpoint
and one internal vertex of the path. Relabel the path and exchange
the hubs if necessary to obtain edges pa and rb, where a is in A
and b in B. Lemma D proves reducibility.

5. SCOPE

This settles the entire P4-core case (mask 13 in the existing bit
convention) by a human structural proof. The existing catalogue has
273 records of this core type, but neither that number nor the
catalogue's completeness is used above. Lemma D is the path
specialization of historical template T2, with its full certificate
verified directly here.

Lemma M also applies to other four-vertex cores with a perfect
matching. Its application alone does not settle their reducibility.
In particular, this document does not claim a human proof for C4
or any other remaining core.

A census-free proof for all admissible local graphs also requires
structural exclusion or reduction of core types omitted by the
upstream census, including the empty core and the single-edge core.
Those exclusions are not proved here and must not be inferred from
the saved catalogue's core-type list.
