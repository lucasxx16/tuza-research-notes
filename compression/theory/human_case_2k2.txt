ROUND 2 / E: A HUMAN PROOF FOR THE CORE 2K2
Date: 2026-10-03
This lemma was developed after reviewing D's completed independent
eleven-template draft. The resulting reduction is D's template T7.
No census, list of admissible side masks, or certificate lookup is used
in the proof. Scanning the supplied data suggested this core as a small
subcase, but the argument below covers every graph under its hypotheses.

Definitions. A graph F is weak Konig-Egervary (WKE) if there exist a
matching M and a set Q of vertices with |Q|<=|M| such that Q is a
vertex cover of F-M. Equivalently, every edge with both endpoints
outside Q belongs to M. This equivalence is immediate from the meaning
of vertex cover. Isolated vertices do not affect this definition.

Claim K1 (the seven-vertex link lemma).
Let F have vertices z,p0,p1,q0,q1,a0,a1. The core C={p0,p1,q0,q1}
induces exactly the two edges p0p1 and q0q1. The vertex z is adjacent
to all four core vertices and to neither a0 nor a1. The only other
possible edges are between C and {a0,a1}, and the edge a0a1.
If F is connected and not WKE, then:
(i) a0a1 is absent and each a_i has a neighbor in C;
(ii) the attachments from {a0,a1} meet both core edges, meaning at
     least one endpoint of each core edge is adjacent to an a_i;
(iii) some core edge xy has edges xa_i and ya_j for distinct i,j.
In (iii), the two core vertices and the two exclusive vertices support
a matching of size two between them.

Proof of (i).
If a0a1 were present, choose the matching
M={p0p1,q0q1,a0a1}
and the set Q={z,a0,a1}. Every edge outside Q is a core edge and
belongs to M, so Q covers F-M and |Q|=|M|=3. This contradicts
non-WKE. Hence a0a1 is absent. Each a_i has no neighbor z and now
no neighbor in {a0,a1}; connectivity therefore forces it to have a
neighbor in C.

Proof of (ii).
Suppose that no attachment meets one core edge, say p0p1.
Then all attachments meet q0q1. By (i), select an edge a_i q_j
and write q_(1-j) for the other endpoint. Put
M={p0p1, a_i q_j, z q_(1-j)},
Q={z,q0,q1}.
The three matching edges are present and pairwise vertex-disjoint.
Every edge outside Q is p0p1: the exclusives have no p-neighbors
and a0a1 is absent. Thus Q covers F-M and |Q|=|M|=3, a
contradiction. Exchanging the two core edges proves the assertion.

Proof of (iii).
Assume, to the contrary, that neither core edge supports the stated
two-edge matching to distinct exclusives.

First suppose each core edge has attachments at at most one of its
endpoints. For each core edge choose an endpoint that contains all
attachments at that edge; if there are no attachments there, choose
either endpoint. Call the chosen endpoints p* and q*. Then
Q={z,p*,q*}
is a vertex cover of all of F: z covers its incident edges, p*,q*
cover the two core edges, and every exclusive-core edge uses one of
the chosen endpoints. The exclusive edge is absent by (i).
There is a matching of size three: select any attachment a_i c,
let c' be the core mate of c, and let dd' be the other core edge.
Then M={a_i c, zc', dd'} is a matching of size three. Hence Q is
also a cover of F-M with |Q|=|M|, contradicting non-WKE.

It remains that one core edge has attachments at both endpoints.
Relabel it as p0p1. Failure of (iii) implies that all attachments
at these two endpoints come from the same exclusive, say a0:
otherwise one endpoint could be matched to a0 and the other to a1.
More explicitly, both endpoint-neighborhoods in {a0,a1} are nonempty;
if either contained both a0,a1, either choice for the other endpoint
would produce distinct representatives. Thus both are the same
singleton. Consequently a1 has no p-neighbor. By (i), a1 has a
neighbor on q0q1.

If a0 had both q0 and q1 as neighbors, a neighbor of a1 at either
endpoint together with a0 at the other would prove (iii), contrary
to assumption. Thus a0 has at most one q-neighbor. Choose an
endpoint r of q0q1 that contains all q-neighbors of a0, choosing
either endpoint if there are none, and let s be the other endpoint.
Put Q={z,a0,r}.
The vertices outside Q are p0,p1,s,a1. Their only possible edges
are p0p1 and a1s: the core has no edge between its two pairs,
a1 has no p-neighbor, and the exclusive edge is absent.
If a1s is present, take
M={p0p1,a1s,zr}.
If a1s is absent, then a1r is present by (i), and take
M={p0p1,a1r,zs}.
In either case M is a three-edge matching containing every edge
outside Q. Thus Q covers F-M and |Q|=|M|=3, the final contradiction.
This proves (iii) and Claim K1.

Claim K2 (unconditional human reducibility for the matching core).
Let G be a finite simple graph with adjacent vertices u,v, with
C=N(u) intersect N(v) of size four and exclusive neighborhoods
A=N(u) minus ({v} union C), B=N(v) minus ({u} union C), both of
size two. Suppose G[C] consists of two disjoint edges, and both
G[N(u)] and G[N(v)] are connected and not WKE. Then {u,v} is
reducible. No degree-budget assumption is required.

Proof. The link G[N(u)] has the form of Claim K1, with opposite
hub z=v, core C, and exclusives A. Claim K1(iii) supplies a core
edge whose two endpoints can be matched to distinct A-vertices.
Label this edge c1c2, and the exclusive vertices so that c1a1 and
c2a0 are present. Label the other core edge c0c3 for now without
choosing which endpoint is c0.

Apply Claim K1(ii) to the other link G[N(v)], whose opposite hub
is u and whose exclusives are B. Some endpoint of the other core
edge has a B-neighbor. Label that endpoint c0 and that neighbor
b1, and label the remaining B-vertex b0. We have obtained the
five distinct rim edges
R={c0c3,c1c2,c1a1,c2a0,c0b1}.
These are exactly the required present edges of D's template T7;
the four missing core cross-edges are absent because the core is 2K2.

For completeness, the witness can be written directly:
S={uc0c3, uc1a1, uc2a0, vc0b1, vc1c2},
X={uv,ua0,ua1,vb0,vb1} union R.

All five triangles exist. At u their rim pairs are
{c0,c3}, {c1,a1}, {c2,a0}, which are pairwise disjoint, so no
u-spoke is reused. At v their rim pairs are {c0,b1}, {c1,c2},
also disjoint. Their rim edges are the five distinct members of R,
so no edge is shared between different triangles. Thus |S|=5.

The set X has ten edges: five hub edges and five rim edges, so
|X|=10=2|S|. Every triangle containing both hubs uses uv. For a
triangle through u but not v, if an exclusive A-vertex occurs then
its u-spoke lies in X; otherwise its other two vertices belong to
C, and their edge is one of c0c3,c1c2, both in X. The same argument
applies at v using the B-spokes. These cases also prove coverage
directly in the ambient graph, because any triangle through u or v
uses only vertices in that hub's neighborhood. Added A--B edges or
edges leaving the local neighborhood have no effect.

Finally, each triangle of S has exactly one edge avoiding {u,v},
and these edges are precisely R subset X. Hence all external
packing edges are in X, proving the safety condition. This verifies
all three reducibility conditions and completes the proof.

Scope. This proves the complete 2K2-core subcase of the source's
codegree-four proposition by hand. It does not prove the other
eight nonempty core cases or a structural disjunction of all eleven
templates. According to the supplied catalogue, 17 records have this
core, but that count is not used anywhere in this proof.
