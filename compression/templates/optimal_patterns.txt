WHY NINE IS EXACT IN THIS DICTIONARY
The nine selected candidate IDs are [7, 78, 195, 214, 316, 329, 387, 428, 491]. Their relabelling orbits cover all 1,144 saved records. An assignment and literal valid witness are exported for every record. The lower-bound records have indices [189, 286, 294, 754, 828, 875, 1089, 1132, 1136] and keys [[7, 239, 239], [11, 445, 446], [12, 63, 255], [15, 431, 463], [30, 63, 207], [30, 255, 271], [31, 316, 316], [63, 60, 271], [63, 271, 271]]. Every one of the 499 dictionary candidates covers at most one of these nine records, as checked exactly against the saved incidence matrix. Any cover therefore needs at least nine candidates; the selected nine attain this bound. This counting proof does not use a numerical solver bound.

LOGICAL LIMIT
This is an exact optimum for the declared catalogue-derived finite dictionary and a universal proof for each chosen pattern. It is not an optimum over every possible signed template, and it does not supply the missing general human proof that A1+A2 force the nine-pattern disjunction.
