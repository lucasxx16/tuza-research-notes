DEGREE-SEVEN LINKS WITH MATCHING COMPLEMENTS

Status: three explicit universal reducibility constructions; a complete
local theorem for every degree-seven link obtained from K7 by deleting
a matching; and a conditional discharging theorem. No enumeration is
used. The full implication mad(G)<8 => tau(G)<=2nu(G) remains unproved.

1. SETUP AND PROVENANCE

All graphs are finite and simple. For adjacent vertices u,v, write
C=N(u) intersect N(v). A reducible pair {u,v} requires a set S of
edge-disjoint triangles and an edge set X such that |X|<=2|S|,
X meets every triangle through u or v, and X contains every S-edge
whose endpoints both avoid {u,v}.

The Fano construction used below is the seven-triangle construction
appearing in Gupta's proof for a complete codegree-six core. The
extensions and repair are proved explicitly. The round-one result
mad8/round1/E/mixed_fano_and_density.txt handled simplicial degree-seven
vertices; the theorem here also handles one, two, or three disjoint
missing edges in a degree-seven link.

None of the lemmas imposes a degree bound on common neighbors or
vertices outside the displayed configuration. Every cover argument
is made directly in the ambient graph.

2. A FIVE-CLIQUE IN THE COMMON NEIGHBORHOOD

Lemma A. Let u,v be adjacent, let d(u)+d(v)<=15, and suppose C
contains a clique W={c0,c1,c2,c3,c4}. Then {u,v} is reducible.

Proof. On the seven vertices {u,v} union W, take

  S={uvc0, uc1c2, uc3c4, vc1c3, vc2c4, c0c1c4, c0c2c3}.

These are the seven Fano triples. For direct checking their edge
sets, in order, are

  uv,uc0,vc0;
  uc1,uc2,c1c2;
  uc3,uc4,c3c4;
  vc1,vc3,c1c3;
  vc2,vc4,c2c4;
  c0c1,c1c4,c0c4;
  c0c2,c2c3,c0c3.

They are twenty-one distinct edges, so S is a packing of size seven.
Define

  X=E(G[W]) union {uv}
      union {ut:t in N(u) outside ({v} union W)}
      union {vt:t in N(v) outside ({u} union W)}.

The four displayed parts are edge-disjoint. There are ten edges
inside W, and exactly d(u)-6 and d(v)-6 spokes in the last two
parts. Hence

  |X|=10+1+(d(u)-6)+(d(v)-6)=d(u)+d(v)-1<=14=2|S|.

A triangle through both hubs meets uv. A triangle through u alone
with a vertex outside W meets a deleted u-spoke. Any remaining
triangle through u has its rim edge in G[W] and meets X. The same
argument applies at v. All packed edges avoiding the hubs lie
inside W, and therefore lie in X. This proves reducibility.

3. AN INDUCED FOUR-CYCLE AND ANOTHER COMMON VERTEX

Lemma B. Let u,v be adjacent with d(u)+d(v)<=15. Suppose four
common vertices x1,x2,x3,x4 induce the cycle x1-x2-x3-x4-x1,
and suppose r is another common vertex. Then {u,v} is reducible.

Proof. Put W={x1,x2,x3,x4} and take

  S={uvr, ux1x2, ux3x4, vx2x3, vx4x1},
  X=E(G[W]) union {uv}
      union {ut:t in N(u) outside ({v} union W)}
      union {vt:t in N(v) outside ({u} union W)}.

At u, the two cycle triangles use disjoint vertex pairs {x1,x2}
and {x3,x4}; at v they use disjoint pairs {x2,x3} and {x4,x1}.
Their four rim edges are distinct. The central triangle uvr uses
uv and the two spokes to r, none used by the other four triangles.
Thus S is edge-disjoint and |S|=5.

The cycle has exactly four edges, so

  |X|=4+1+(d(u)-5)+(d(v)-5)=d(u)+d(v)-5<=10=2|S|.

Any triangle through both hubs meets uv. A triangle through just
one hub with a rim vertex outside W meets its deleted spoke. If
both rim vertices lie in W, their edge is a cycle edge because
the cycle is induced; that edge lies in X. Finally the only packed
edges avoiding the hubs are the four cycle edges, all in X.
This verifies all three conditions in the ambient graph.

The induced condition is essential to this displayed certificate:
an added diagonal would support an uncovered hub triangle.

4. REPAIRING ONE MISSING EDGE IN THE FIVE-VERTEX CORE

Lemma C. Let u,v be adjacent with d(u)+d(v)<=15. Suppose W is a
five-vertex subset of C inducing K5 minus a single edge xy, and
suppose w is a neighbor of u outside {v} union W with wx present.
Then {u,v} is reducible. The vertex w may or may not be adjacent
to v, and all its other adjacencies are unrestricted.

Proof. Write W={x,y,p,q,r}. The following seven formal triples
are a Fano system on {u,v} union W:

  uxy, uvp, uqr, vxq, xpr, vyr, ypq.

Remove uxy, whose edge xy is absent, and insert uwx. Thus take

  S={uvp, uqr, vxq, xpr, vyr, ypq, uwx}.

All seven triangles now exist. The first six are edge-disjoint:
their edges are all edges of the seven-vertex clique except the
three edges ux,uy,xy. In particular ux is unused. The new triangle
uwx uses the unused edge ux and two edges incident to w, a vertex
outside those seven vertices. It is therefore disjoint from all
six triangles. Hence |S|=7.

Define

  X=E(G[W]) union {uv,wx}
      union {ut:t in N(u) outside ({v} union W)}
      union {vt:t in N(v) outside ({u} union W)}.

The displayed parts are edge-disjoint, including wx, which is a
rim edge between W and a vertex outside W. The spoke uw is among
the u-spokes. Since G[W] has nine edges,

  |X|=9+2+(d(u)-6)+(d(v)-6)=d(u)+d(v)-1<=14=2|S|.

Triangles through both hubs meet uv. A triangle through one hub
and a rim vertex outside W meets a deleted spoke. Otherwise its
rim edge is in G[W] and belongs to X. This covers all ambient hub
triangles. The edges of S avoiding the hubs are edges of G[W]
and wx, all in X. Thus the pair is reducible.

5. COMPLETE LOCAL THEOREM FOR MATCHING-COMPLEMENT LINKS

Theorem M. Let u have degree seven, and suppose the complement
of the induced link G[N(u)] is a matching. For every neighbor v
with d(v)<=8, the pair {u,v} is reducible.

Proof. Let m be the number of missing edges in the link. Since
the link has seven vertices and the missing edges are a matching,
0<=m<=3. Also d(u)+d(v)<=15.

Case 1: v is incident to no missing edge in the link.
Then v is adjacent to all six other vertices of N(u), so C consists
of those six vertices. Its graph is K6 minus a matching of size m.
If m<=1, omitting an endpoint of the possible missing edge leaves
a five-clique in C; apply Lemma A.
If m>=2, take the endpoints of two missing edges. On these four
vertices all other edges are present, so they induce a four-cycle.
There are two further common vertices, so Lemma B applies.

Case 2: v is incident to a missing edge vw in the link.
The vertex w is the only member of N(u)\{v} not adjacent to v,
so C=N(u)\{v,w} has size five. Its graph is K5 minus a matching
of size m-1. If m=1, apply Lemma A. If m=3, the four endpoints
of the two missing core edges induce a four-cycle, and the fifth
core vertex supplies r for Lemma B.

Finally suppose m=2. Write xy for the other missing edge. Then
C induces K5-xy. Because the missing edges form a matching and
w is already incident to vw, every edge from w to C is present;
in particular wx is present. Also w is a neighbor of u outside
{v} union C. Apply Lemma C with W=C.

The two cases exhaust every neighbor v and every possible matching.
This proves the theorem.

Corollary M1. In a graph with no reducible pair, every neighbor of
a degree-seven vertex whose link has matching complement has
degree at least nine.

This corollary requires no minimum-degree assumption elsewhere.
It includes the non-simplicial links K7 minus one, two, or three
disjoint edges, not only the complete link K7.

6. CONDITIONAL DISCHARGING BEYOND SIMPLICIAL LINKS

Lemma D. Let G be a nonempty graph of minimum degree at least
seven. Let L be its set of degree-seven vertices. Suppose that
every vertex of L has a link whose complement is a matching,
and every neighbor of L has degree at least nine. Then

  2|E(G)|/|V(G)| >= 8 + (3/4)|L|/|V(G)|.          (1)

Proof. The neighbor-degree assumption makes L independent and
ensures that no degree-eight vertex has a neighbor in L.

Let v be a vertex of degree d>=9 with a neighbor w in L. In the
seven-vertex link G[N(w)], the vertex v has at most one nonneighbor.
Thus at least five of the six vertices in N(w)\{v} are adjacent
to v. Each has degree at least nine by the neighbor-degree
assumption at w. Therefore at least five vertices of N(v) lie
outside L, giving

  |N(v) intersect L| <= d-5.                     (2)

Give each vertex its degree as initial charge. Each vertex in L
takes 1/4 from each of its seven neighbors. Transfers conserve
the sum of charges. A vertex in L ends with 7+7/4=35/4. A
degree-eight vertex neither sends nor receives charge, so retains
eight. A degree-d vertex, d>=9, with a neighbor in L ends with
at least

  d-(d-5)/4=(3d+5)/4>=8.

A degree-d vertex with no neighbor in L retains d>=9. Summing
the final charges gives (1). No assertion of sharpness is made.

Theorem T. Let G have minimum degree at least seven and average
degree below eight. If every degree-seven link has a complement
that is a matching, then G contains an explicit reducible pair
from Theorem M.

Proof. Suppose no degree-seven vertex has a neighbor of degree
at most eight. Then the hypotheses of Lemma D hold, giving
average degree at least eight, a contradiction. Hence some
degree-seven u has a neighbor v of degree at most eight; Theorem
M makes {u,v} reducible.

In particular Theorem T applies when mad(G)<8 with the same local
hypotheses. A subgraph-minimal counterexample to Tuza in that
maximum-average-degree class has no reducible set, so it cannot
simultaneously have minimum degree at least seven and matching
complements at all degree-seven links.

7. WHAT HAS AND HAS NOT BEEN CLOSED

The new conditional theorem extends the simplicial-link result of
round one to all four matching-complement types on seven vertices.
It gives a concrete donor restriction for degree-seven vertices
and uses only three human-checkable packing-and-cover recipes.
The special (7,8), codegree-six, K6-minus-two-disjoint-edges case
has a five-triangle/ten-edge witness from Lemma B, with no triangle
using the exclusive vertex of the degree-eight hub.

This does not complete Question 12.1. Degree-five and degree-six
vertices are still not eliminated. Even when minimum degree is
seven, the argument leaves degree-seven links having a vertex
with at least two nonneighbors in the link. No reduction or
discharging bound for every such link has been proved here.

Theorem T is a local forcing theorem. The matching-complement
property need not survive arbitrary edge deletions, so it is not
being used as an unsupported hereditary induction hypothesis.
All ambient coverage and external-packing-edge conditions are
verified separately in the three universal lemmas.
