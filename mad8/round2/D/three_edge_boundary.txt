MAD<8 / ROUND 2 / D
THREE SHARED EDGES, TRIANGLE SEPARATORS, AND 4-CONNECTIVITY

Status: reviewed research proof; see ../C/review_final_proofs.txt.
This is internal research review, not human peer review or formal verification.
No claim of novelty is made. No computation or external theorem is used.

SETUP AND NOTATION.
Let G_1,G_2 be subgraphs whose union is G. Their common vertex set is S,
their common edge set is F=E(G[S]), and G has no edge between
V(G_1)\S and V(G_2)\S. The sides contain all the edges of G induced on
their vertex sets. Thus every triangle of G is contained in at least one
side; a triangle wholly in S may belong to both.

For J subset F, let
 H_i(J)=G_i-(F\J),
 a_i=nu(H_i(empty))=nu(G_i-F),
 N=nu(G).
Here nu is maximum edge-disjoint triangle packing size, and tau is
minimum triangle edge-cover size. Assume Tuza's inequality holds for
every H_i(J), including G_i itself. This is the only hypothesis about
the sides used below.

Packings in H_1(J_1) and H_2(J_2) are edge-disjoint whenever J_1 and J_2
are disjoint: every edge common to the sides belongs to F. Consequently

 (*)  N >= nu(H_1(J_1))+nu(H_2(J_2)) whenever J_1 intersect J_2 is empty.

In particular N>=a_1+a_2, and nu(G_i)<=N-a_j for j distinct from i.
Adding a single edge increases the maximum triangle packing size by at
most one, because at most one member of a packing can use that edge.
Thus nu(H_i({e})) belongs to {a_i,a_i+1} for every e in F.

THEOREM 1 (a complete necessary obstruction at a three-edge boundary).
Suppose |F|<=3 and the side hypothesis above holds. If G violates
Tuza's inequality, then |F|=3, F contains no triangle, and after
interchanging the sides all the following hold:

 (1) N=a_1+a_2+1 and nu(G_i)=a_i+1 for both sides.

 (2) Restoring any nonempty subset of F on side 1 already gives its
     entire packing gain:
       nu(H_1(J))=a_1+1 for every nonempty J subset F.

 (3) Side 2 gains a triangle only when all three boundary edges return:
       nu(H_2(J))=a_2 for every proper J subsetneq F,
       nu(G_2)=a_2+1.
     Equivalently, nu(G_2-e)=a_2 for every e in F.

 (4) Side 1 is Tuza-tight, tau(G_1)=2nu(G_1), and every minimum triangle
     cover of G_1 avoids every edge of F. In fact every cover of G_1
     of size at most 2nu(G_1) avoids F.

 (5) The other side has slack: tau(G_2)<=2nu(G_2)-1.

These are necessary conditions for failure of this gluing argument,
not sufficient conditions for a counterexample.

Proof. Suppose tau(G)>2N. If N=a_1+a_2, inequality (*) shows
nu(G_i)<=a_i for both i, hence equality. Combining covers of the two
sides gives tau(G)<=2a_1+2a_2=2N, a contradiction.

If N>=a_1+a_2+2, choose all edges of F and covers of G_i-F. Every
triangle using a boundary edge is met by F, and every remaining triangle
is covered on its side. The resulting cover has size at most
  |F|+2a_1+2a_2<=3+2a_1+2a_2<=2N-1,
again a contradiction. Therefore N=a_1+a_2+1. If |F|<=2, the same
construction has size at most 2N, so necessarily |F|=3.

By (*), nu(G_i)<=a_i+1. If one side, say G_2, had nu(G_2)=a_2,
then the union of side covers would have size at most
2(a_1+1)+2a_2=2N. This is impossible. Thus nu(G_i)=a_i+1 for both
sides, establishing (1).

Call a boundary edge e gainful on side i if nu(H_i({e}))=a_i+1.
Suppose some e is gainful on neither side. Select the other two edges
F\{e}, together with covers of H_1({e}) and H_2({e}). A triangle
containing one of the selected boundary edges is met immediately; every
other triangle remains in at least one of the two H_i({e}) and is met
by its cover. The total size is at most
  2+2a_1+2a_2=2N,
a contradiction. Therefore every edge of F is gainful on at least one
side.

Different boundary edges cannot be gainful on opposite sides: their
singleton edge sets are disjoint, so (*) would give
N>=a_1+a_2+2. Choose an edge e gainful on some side and name that side
1. Each of the other two edges must then be gainful on side 1 and cannot
be gainful on side 2. The first edge e cannot be gainful on side 2
either, because either of those other two edges is gainful on side 1.
Thus every edge is gainful on side 1 and none is gainful on side 2.
Monotonicity together with nu(G_1)=a_1+1 proves (2).

For e in F, if nu(G_2-e)>=a_2+1, combine a packing of that size with
a packing of H_1({e}) of size a_1+1. Their permitted boundary sets are
disjoint, contradicting (*). Thus nu(G_2-e)<=a_2, while the reverse
inequality follows from G_2-F being a subgraph. Every proper J subset F
omits some e, so H_2(J) is a subgraph of G_2-e and has packing number
exactly a_2. This proves (3).

Now consider any cover X_1 of G_1 with |X_1|<=2nu(G_1)=2a_1+2.
If X_1 contains a boundary edge e, take a cover Y_2 of G_2-e with
|Y_2|<=2nu(G_2-e)=2a_2. Then X_1 union Y_2 covers G: triangles on
side 1 meet X_1, while triangles on side 2 either use e (already in
X_1) or survive in G_2-e (and meet Y_2). Its size is at most 2N,
a contradiction. Therefore every cover of G_1 within that budget avoids
F. The assumed side inequality supplies such a cover. If
tau(G_1)<=2nu(G_1)-1, adding any e in F to a minimum cover would produce
a cover within the budget that meets F. This is impossible. Hence
tau(G_1)=2nu(G_1), proving (4).

For any e in F, the set consisting of e and a cover of G_2-e gives
tau(G_2)<=1+2a_2=2nu(G_2)-1, proving (5).

Finally, F cannot contain a triangle, since every triangle cover of
G_1 would then have to contain an edge of F, contrary to (4). This
finishes the proof.

COROLLARY 2 (gluing across at most three vertices).
Under the same side hypothesis, if |S|<=3 then tau(G)<=2nu(G).

Proof. If |S|<=3, the induced edge set F has at most three edges. By
Theorem 1, failure would require exactly three edges and no triangle.
But the unique simple graph on at most three vertices with three edges
is a triangle. This contradiction proves the corollary.

COROLLARY 3 (4-connectivity of minimal counterexamples).
Let C be any class closed under arbitrary subgraphs. If C contains a
counterexample to Tuza, choose G in C minimizing |V(G)|+|E(G)| among
counterexamples. Then G is 4-connected. In particular this applies to
the class mad(G)<8.

Proof. Every proper subgraph of G belongs to C and satisfies Tuza by
minimality. Graphs on at most four vertices satisfy Tuza: if no triangle
is present there is nothing to cover; otherwise nu>=1, while any two
disjoint edges of K4 meet all its triangles, and intersecting this cover
with the edge set of the graph yields a cover of size at most two.
Thus |V(G)|>=5.

If G is disconnected, take two nonempty unions of its components as
the two sides and S=empty. Corollary 2 applies and contradicts G being
a counterexample. More generally, suppose S is a vertex separator of
size at most three. Partition the components of G-S into two nonempty
groups, and let G_i be induced by S together with the vertices in group
i. Both sides are proper subgraphs. Each H_i(J) is also a proper
subgraph of G, so all side inequalities assumed in Corollary 2 follow
from minimality. The corollary again proves Tuza for G, a contradiction.
There is therefore no separator of size at most three. Together with
|V(G)|>=5, this is 4-connectivity.

COROLLARY 4 (the remaining sparse four-vertex cuts are rigid).
For G as in Corollary 3, suppose a vertex separator S has size four and
G[S] has at most three edges. Then G[S] is either P4 or K_{1,3}.
Furthermore, for every partition of the components of G-S into two
nonempty sides, those sides have the packing and cover properties
(1)--(5) of Theorem 1, after interchanging their names if necessary.

Proof. Every required side inequality holds by minimality as before.
Theorem 1 forces exactly three shared edges and no triangle. A simple
triangle-free graph on four vertices with three edges is a tree: the
only possible cycle with at most three edges would be a triangle, and
an acyclic graph with four vertices and three edges is connected. The
only two trees on four vertices are P4 and K_{1,3}. The remaining
statements are exactly Theorem 1.

INTERPRETATION AND LIMITS.
This strengthens the round-one 3-connectivity reduction and identifies
the precise packing imbalance that would be needed at a separator
carrying only three edges. The exceptional four-vertex cuts force one
side to be Tuza-tight while every minimum cover avoids all three shared
edges; the other side has a maximum-packing gain that requires all three
edges jointly. No claim is made that this exceptional pattern occurs,
or that its exclusion would by itself prove the mad<8 statement.

The full question whether mad(G)<8 implies tau(G)<=2nu(G) remains open
in this work. No numerical search, census, certificate finder, or external
structural theorem is a premise of these proofs.
