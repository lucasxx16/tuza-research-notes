ADDING THE FOUR-VERTEX PATH COMPLEMENT AT DEGREE SEVEN

Status: a complete conditional local-forcing theorem for three link
families, with a stronger count obtained by selecting which neighbors
donate charge. The new family is K7 minus a four-vertex path. All
reducibility certificates used here were proved in earlier rounds;
the new work is the case analysis and the selected-incidence argument.
No numerical enumeration or new witness search is used.

1. THE THREE LINK TYPES AND THE IMPORTED REDUCTIONS

All graphs are finite and simple. Write L for the degree-seven vertices
and H for the vertices of degree at least nine. For u in L, consider
the complement of the induced graph G[N(u)]. The three allowed types
are:

  M: a matching, possibly empty;
  P: a P3 together with four isolated vertices;
  Q: a P4 together with three isolated vertices.

The names refer to the complement, not to the link itself. For type P,
the center of the missing P3 is the unique exceptional neighbor. For
type Q, the two internal vertices of the missing P4 are the exceptional
neighbors. Type M has no exceptional neighbors.

We use the following previously proved results, with their exact scope.

R1 (matching type). If d(u)=7 and u has type M, then every neighbor
v with d(v)<=8 forms a reducible pair with u. This is Theorem M of
mad8/round2/E/matching_complement_links.txt.

R2 (type P, nonexceptional neighbor). If d(u)=7 and u has type P,
then every nonexceptional neighbor v with d(v)<=8 forms a reducible
pair with u. This is Lemma P of
mad8/round3/E/p3_complement_links.txt.

R3 (repaired Fano). Suppose u,v are adjacent, d(u)+d(v)<=15, and
W is a five-vertex subset of their common neighborhood inducing K5
minus one edge xy. If w is a neighbor of u outside {v} union W and
wx is present, then {u,v} is reducible. The vertex w is allowed to
be a common neighbor of the hubs. Writing W={x,y,p,q,r}, a packing is

  S={uvp,uqr,vxq,xpr,vyr,ypq,uwx},

and a cover is

  X=E(G[W]) union {uv,wx}
      union {ut:t in N(u) outside ({v} union W)}
      union {vt:t in N(v) outside ({u} union W)}.

Its size is d(u)+d(v)-1<=14=2|S|. Full edge-disjointness, ambient
coverage, and external-packing-edge containment are proved in Lemma C
of mad8/round2/E/matching_complement_links.txt.

R4 (the dense four-core pair). Suppose u,v are adjacent degree-seven
vertices with common four-clique C={x,y,p,q}, private pairs {a,b} at u
and {c,d} at v, edges ab,cd, and a adjacent to every member of C.
Then {u,v} is reducible using

  S={uxy,upq,vxp,vyq,axq,ayp,uab,vcd},
  X=E(G[C union {a}]) union {ab,cd,uv,ub,vc,vd}.

This is the eight-triangle/sixteen-edge certificate proved in
mad8/round1/D/dense_pair_certificate.txt and reproduced with these
labels in mad8/round3/E/p3_complement_links.txt. Other private-core
attachments and edges between the private pairs are unrestricted.

These reductions impose no upper degree bound on common neighbors.
Each contains every external packing edge in its cover and covers
all ambient triangles through its two hubs. No new construction is
claimed for R1-R4.

2. THE FIVE NONEXCEPTIONAL POSITIONS IN A P4 COMPLEMENT

Lemma A. Let d(u)=7 and suppose the missing edges in G[N(u)] are

  ab,bc,cd,

where a,b,c,d form the complement path in that order. Write e,f,g
for the remaining three neighbors of u. If v is one of a,d,e,f,g
and d(v)<=8, then {u,v} is reducible.

Proof. First let v=e; the cases v=f,g are identical after relabeling.
Since e has no missing link edge, it is adjacent to every other
neighbor of u. The five-set

  W={a,c,d,f,g}

lies in N(u) intersect N(v) and induces K5 minus the edge cd:
the other two missing edges ab,bc use b, which is outside W.
Take w=b. This vertex is a neighbor of u outside {v} union W,
and bd is present because it is not a missing path edge. Apply
R3 with x=d and y=c. Here w is an additional common neighbor;
R3 explicitly permits this.

Next let v=a. Its only nonneighbor in the link is b. Thus

  N(u) intersect N(a)={c,d,e,f,g},

which induces K5 minus cd. Again w=b is a neighbor of u outside
that common five-set and bd is present. R3 applies with x=d,y=c.
The case v=d follows by reversing a-b-c-d.

Every case uses d(u)+d(v)<=15. These five possibilities exhaust
the nonexceptional neighbors, proving the lemma.

Consequently, if no such pair is reducible, a type-Q vertex has
five specified neighbors of degree at least nine. Its two internal
complement-path neighbors b,c are left unclassified by this lemma.

3. TWO EXCEPTIONAL DEGREE-SEVEN ENDPOINTS

Lemma B. Suppose u and v are adjacent degree-seven vertices, each
of type P or Q, and each is an exceptional neighbor of the other.
Then {u,v} is reducible by R4.

Proof. In either type, an exceptional neighbor is incident with
exactly two missing edges in the link. Hence u and v have exactly
four common neighbors C and two private vertices on each side.

Inspect the link at u. If it has type P with missing edges va,vb,
then C is a four-clique, ab is present, and both a,b are complete
to C.

If it has type Q, label its complement path a-v-b-t. Its two
private vertices are a,b, and C consists of t and the three isolated
complement vertices. Thus C is a four-clique. The edge ab is
present, since a and b are not consecutive on the complement path.
Also a is complete to C, since its sole missing link edge is av.

Thus in both cases the u-side supplies a private pair a,b with ab
present and a complete to C. Applying the same inspection to the
link at v shows that its two private vertices c,d are adjacent.
These are precisely the hypotheses of R4, which proves reducibility.

4. INDEPENDENCE AND THE DONATION INCIDENCES

Lemma C. Suppose every degree-seven vertex has type M, P, or Q,
and none of the pairs supplied by R1, R2, Lemma A, or Lemma B occurs.
Then L is independent. For each w in L, select the following neighbors:

  type M: all seven neighbors;
  type P: its six nonexceptional neighbors;
  type Q: its five nonexceptional neighbors.

Every selected neighbor v has degree at least nine, and v has degree
at least five inside the link G[N(w)].

Proof. If two degree-seven vertices are adjacent and one has type M,
R1 applies. If both have type P or Q and at least one is nonexceptional
for the other, R2 or Lemma A applies. The only other possibility is
that each is exceptional for the other; Lemma B then applies. Thus
the assumed absence of the listed configurations makes L independent.

Every selected neighbor of degree at most eight would give one of
R1, R2, or Lemma A. Therefore selected neighbors belong to H.

Finally a selected vertex is incident with at most one missing edge
in the corresponding seven-vertex link. In type M this holds for
every vertex; in types P and Q it holds for all nonexceptional
vertices. Hence its degree inside that link is at least 6-1=5.

The word selected refers to the ordered incidence from w to v. A
high-degree vertex may be exceptional for one degree-seven neighbor
and selected by another. Charge is sent only along selected incidences.

5. SELECTED-INCIDENCE DISCHARGING

Lemma D. Let G be a nonempty graph of minimum degree at least seven.
Let L and H be as above, and let L_M,L_P,L_Q partition L. Assume:

  (i) L is independent;
  (ii) every w in L_M,L_P,L_Q has respectively seven, six, or five
       selected neighbors in H;
  (iii) every selected neighbor v of w has degree at least five
        inside G[N(w)].

Writing n=|V(G)|, the following bound holds:

  2|E(G)|-8n >= [
      2|L_M|+|L_P|+|H|
      +4 sum over v in H of (d(v)-9)
    ]/5.                                                   (1)

Proof. Give each vertex its degree as initial charge. A vertex v in H
sends 1/5 to w in L precisely when w selected v. These transfers
conserve the total charge. A type-M receiver finishes with
7+7/5=42/5, a type-P receiver with 41/5, and a type-Q receiver
with eight. Vertices of degree eight send and receive nothing.

Let v in H have at least one selected recipient w. By (iii),
v has five neighbors inside G[N(w)]. They belong to N(v), and
none belongs to L by independence. This uses any one selected
incidence at v; no assumption is made about every link globally.
Consequently

  |N(v) intersect L|<=d(v)-5.

The number of selected recipients of v is at most this number.
Thus its final charge is at least

  d(v)-(d(v)-5)/5=(4d(v)+5)/5
      =8+[1+4(d(v)-9)]/5.

If v has no selected recipient, it retains d(v), which is at
least the same lower bound because d(v)>=9>=5. Summing the
receiver surpluses 2/5,1/5,0 and the donor surpluses gives (1).
No sharpness claim is made for this inequality.

6. COMPLETE CONDITIONAL FORCING THEOREM

Theorem. Let G have minimum degree at least seven and average
degree below eight. Suppose every degree-seven link has complement
equal to a matching, a P3 with four isolated vertices, or a P4
with three isolated vertices. Then G has a reducible pair provided
by R1, R2, Lemma A, or Lemma B.

Proof. Suppose none of those configurations occurs. Lemma C supplies
independence and the selected incidences. Lemma D then gives average
degree at least eight, contrary to the hypothesis.

The bound in fact leaves a positive margin whenever H is nonempty.
Under the absence of the listed reductions, if G has a degree-seven
vertex then it has selected neighbors in H, so H is nonempty. Thus
the same argument says: among graphs satisfying these local
hypotheses and containing none of the listed reducible pairs,
average degree at most eight forces the graph to be eight-regular.

The theorem applies in particular under mad(G)<8. In a subgraph-
minimal counterexample to Tuza in that class there is no reducible
vertex set, so if its minimum degree is at least seven, at least
one of its degree-seven links must lie outside these three families.

7. SCOPE AND THE FAMILY NOT INCLUDED

This extends the accepted matching-or-P3 conditional theorem by the
whole P4-complement family. It does not classify every three-edge
complement. The P3+K2 complement is specifically not included.
For example, with missing edges ab,bc,de, a neighbor v=d has
unique nonneighbor e, and its five-vertex common core with u
induces K5 minus the two incident edges ab,bc. The preceding
nonexceptional-neighbor argument does not cover that case. This
is a gap in that attempted extension, not a nonreducibility claim.

The three-edge star and the triangle complements are also outside
the stated theorem. No assertion is made that every exceptional
mixed (7,8) pair in type P or Q is reducible. The selected-incidence
argument avoids needing such a statement by charging only the
neighbors already forced to have degree at least nine.

The minimum-degree assumption is essential to the scope: the
degree-five and degree-six cases remain open in the main mad<8
route. The link-family condition need not survive arbitrary edge
deletions, so the theorem is a local forcing result at a minimal
counterexample, not an induction on an unproved hereditary class.

All common-neighbor degrees are unrestricted. No pointwise degree
budget from the earlier 7-regular problem is used. The external-
packing-edge requirement is part of every imported reduction, and
no census or numerical witness search is a premise here.
