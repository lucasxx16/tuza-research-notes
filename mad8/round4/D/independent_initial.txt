First new lemma. A graph with ordinary matching number at most two is
non-WKE only when its nonisolated part is K5 minus a matching. A short
proof follows from Tutte--Berge plus the reviewed <=5-vertex WKE facts.
If matching number is two, choose a Tutte--Berge set S. Its size is at
most two. If |S|=2, the remainder is independent, so S is a vertex
cover of size two. If |S|=1, deleting its vertex leaves either a single
3-vertex component or a single 2-vertex component, plus isolates. The
3-component case is WKE using one edge in it and a pendant edge at S
if available; otherwise the nontrivial part has at most four vertices.
The 2-component case is immediate. If |S|=0, the only possible components
larger than four have exactly five vertices; all remaining vertices then
are isolated. The reviewed five-vertex classification finishes the proof.

For a nontrivial cut of size m<=7 in the minimum counterexample, previous
4-connectivity and minimum degree five force both shores to have at least
four vertices, and hence the bipartite cut graph B has matching number
at least four (by Konig). Its compatibility graph H has an independent
set of size four. If H has a 3-edge matching, a vertex cover of size at
most three witnesses WKE. If its matching number is at most two and it
were non-WKE, five cut edges would induce K5 minus a matching in H.
Those five edges must form a star in B: a disjoint pair would force three
other edges to meet both, but a bipartite graph allows at most two.
Five star edges plus at most two other edges have matching number at
most three, contradiction. Thus H is WKE.

If B is C4-free, the reviewed cut lemma immediately reduces the cut.
If B contains C4, its component has at most six edges; a cyclic
seven-edge component has at most seven vertices and matching number at
most three, contrary to the needed total four. The possible C4 components
are exactly:
  A: a bare C4 (other components use at most three edges);
  B: C4 with one pendant edge (the other two edges must be isolated);
  C: C4 with two pendants at adjacent cycle vertices (one isolated edge);
  D: C4 with a pendant path of length two (one isolated edge).
Other six-edge attachments have matching number at most two and are
excluded. A is covered by the reviewed C4 certificate plus small WKE
components.

I have direct certificates for B--D, based on the number of present
internal rims. B has four possible rims: with <=2 use one crossing
triangle, and with >=3 there are two edge-disjoint crossing triangles;
cover all present rims. C has six possible rims split into two disjoint
3-rim sets, each supporting a three-triangle packing; >=5 present rims
force one set. With three or four rims there are two edge-disjoint
crossing triangles; with <=2 one suffices. Cover all rims.

For D there are five possible rims. If neither cycle diagonal is present,
all crossing triangles contain the middle edge of the pendant path;
one triangle plus that cut edge and its rim gives a two-edge cover.
With a diagonal present, <=4 rims are handled by one/two crossing
triangles. If all five rims exist, use the internal triangle on the
three vertices in one shore plus two crossing triangles; all five rim
edges form the cover, within the six-edge budget.
