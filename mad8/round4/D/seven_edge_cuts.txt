MAD<8 / ROUND 4 / D
NO NONTRIVIAL EDGE CUT BELOW EIGHT

Status: reviewed research proof; see C/review_D_full.txt.
This records internal research review, not journal peer review or formal verification.
No novelty claim is made. No computation is used.

THEOREM.
Let C be a class of finite simple graphs closed under arbitrary subgraphs.
If C contains a counterexample to tau(G)<=2nu(G), choose such a G with
|V(G)|+|E(G)| minimum. Every edge cut of G of size at most seven
separates off a single vertex. Thus every such cut is the set of incident
edges of a vertex of degree five, six, or seven.

An edge cut means the complete bipartition cut delta(U), where U is a
nonempty proper vertex set. A nontrivial cut means both shores contain
at least two vertices. The theorem does not assert 8-edge-connectivity,
since vertices of degrees five through seven are not excluded.

DEPENDENCIES AND NOTATION.
We use the reviewed round-two 4-connectivity theorem in
mad8/round2/D/three_edge_boundary.txt, and the reviewed round-three
minimum-degree-five and cut-reduction results in
mad8/round3/D/small_edge_cuts.txt. The latter already excludes nontrivial
cuts of size at most six and proves the small WKE facts recalled below.
The only additional classical theorem used as a black box is the
Tutte--Berge formula, stated explicitly in Section 1. A verified
author-hosted statement is A. Schrijver, "Tutte-Berge formula", page 1,
Theorem (1): https://homepages.cwi.nl/~lex/files/tutteb.pdf.
See also the source record mad8/round4/A/tutte_berge_source.txt.

Write nu for maximum edge-disjoint triangle packing size and tau for
minimum triangle edge-cover size. Write mu(H) for the ordinary matching
number of a graph H. A WKE witness in H is a matching M and vertex set Q
with |Q|<=|M| such that Q covers H-M, or equivalently H-Q is contained
in M. Disjoint unions of WKE graphs are WKE.

The previously proved small classification says that every graph on at
most four vertices is WKE, and the only non-WKE graphs on five vertices
are K5 with a matching of size zero, one, or two deleted.

1. A WKE CLASSIFICATION AT MATCHING NUMBER TWO.

LEMMA 1.
If mu(H)<=2, then H is non-WKE if and only if its nonisolated part is
K5 minus a matching. All other vertices, if any, are isolated.

Proof. If mu(H)<=1, the nonempty edges pairwise intersect. Their nontrivial
part is a star or a triangle, and each is WKE (one matching edge and a
suitable one-vertex cover after its deletion). The edgeless case is WKE.
Thus suppose mu(H)=2 and put n=|V(H)|.

The Tutte--Berge formula states
  2mu(H)=n-max_{S subset V(H)} (o(H-S)-|S|),
where o denotes the number of odd-order connected components, including
isolated vertices. Choose S attaining that maximum. Then
  o(H-S)-|S|=n-4.
Since o(H-S)<=n-|S|, we have |S|<=2. The total excess of component
orders over their odd-component count is
  |V(H-S)|-o(H-S)=4-2|S|.
An odd component of order t contributes t-1 to this excess; an even
component of order t contributes t. All contributions are nonnegative
even integers.

If |S|=2, the excess is zero, so every component of H-S is an isolated
vertex. Hence S is a vertex cover of size two. With any maximum matching
of size two, this is a WKE witness.

If |S|=1, write S={x}. The excess is two. Apart from isolated vertices,
H-x has either a single component of order two or a single component
of order three. In the order-two case, let ab be its edge. Then M={ab}
and Q={x} witness WKE: every remaining edge is incident to x.

In the order-three case, let T be that component and select an edge ab
in T; let c be its third vertex. If x has a neighbor y that is isolated
in H-x, take M={xy,ab} and Q={x,c}. The only possible edge outside Q
is ab, which belongs to M, so this is a WKE witness. If no such y
exists, all nonisolated vertices of H lie in {x} union T, a set of at
most four vertices. The reviewed small classification gives WKE.

If |S|=0, the component excess is four. Every component has order at
most five. If a five-vertex component occurs, it has odd order and uses
the entire excess; every other component is then an isolated vertex.
The five-vertex classification says that H is WKE unless that component
is K5 minus a matching. If no five-vertex component occurs, every
component has order at most four and is WKE, so their union is WKE.

Conversely, isolated vertices cannot help a WKE witness on a non-WKE
component: removing them from Q does not affect coverage or increase
its size, and no matching edge uses them. Therefore a K5-minus-matching
component with only isolated vertices outside it remains non-WKE. This
proves the lemma, including disconnected graphs and all isolated cases.

2. COMPATIBILITY ON AT MOST SEVEN CUT EDGES.

LEMMA 2.
Let B be a simple bipartite graph with m<=7 edges and mu(B)>=4. Let H
have vertex set E(B), where adjacent vertices of H must correspond to
incident edges of B. Then H is WKE.

Proof. A matching P of B of size at least four is an independent vertex
set in H. Thus Q=E(B)\P is a vertex cover of H of size at most three.
If mu(H)=3, use any three-edge matching of H together with Q; Q covers
all of H and hence H-M. This is a WKE witness. A larger matching in H
is impossible because H has at most seven vertices.

If mu(H)<=2 and H were non-WKE, Lemma 1 would give five vertices
inducing K5 minus a matching, with any other vertices isolated. Let D
be their corresponding five edges in B. Suppose two edges e=x1y1
and f=x2y2 in D are disjoint, with x1,x2 and y1,y2 in the respective
bipartition classes. The vertices e,f are nonadjacent in H. Each vertex
of K5 minus a matching has at most one nonneighbor, so each of the
other three edges in D must be adjacent to both e and f in H, and
therefore meet both in B. Only x1y2 and x2y1 can do that in a simple
bipartite graph. Three distinct such edges are impossible.

Thus the five edges in D pairwise intersect. Pairwise-intersecting edges
of a bipartite graph all share one endpoint: after choosing xy and xz,
an edge meeting both without containing x would have to be yz, which
is not bipartite with y,z in the same class. Hence D is a five-edge
star. A matching of B uses at most one edge of D and at most m-5 other
edges, giving mu(B)<=1+(m-5)<=3. This contradicts the hypothesis and
proves the lemma.

3. THE CUT CERTIFICATE FRAMEWORK.

For a cut E0=delta(U), its cut graph B consists of the cut edges and
their endpoints, bipartitioned by the two shores. Every crossing triangle
uses two cut edges and one internal rim edge. The compatibility graph H
has vertex set E0, joining the two cut edges of each crossing triangle.
It is a graph of the kind in Lemma 2.

A certificate consists of edge-disjoint triangles S and an edge set X
such that |X|<=2|S|, X meets every triangle using E0, and every edge
of S outside E0 lies in X. It is valid even when S and X are empty,
provided there are no crossing triangles. For
  G'=G-(E0 union X),
a packing in G' is disjoint from S, while a cover of G' together with X
covers G. Thus Tuza for G' implies Tuza for G. A minimum counterexample
in a subgraph-closed class has no such nonempty reducible E0.

We recall the reviewed C4-free cut lemma. If B is C4-free and H is WKE,
choose a matching M and Q covering H-M with |Q|<=|M|. Pack the crossing
triangles represented by M, and cover their rim edges together with the
cut edges represented by Q. Matching M prevents repeated cut edges.
Two different crossing triangles with the same rim would form a C4
in B, so the rims are distinct as well. The result is a valid certificate.

Certificates may also be combined over components of B. Every crossing
triangle is confined to one component, because its two cut edges share
a vertex. All vertices used in the constructions below belong to their
component of B, so packings from different components are vertex-disjoint
and their covers have total size at most the sum of the separate sizes.
This also permits a triangle wholly inside a shore, provided all its
edges are included in its component's cover.

4. THE ONLY FOUR-CYCLE COMPONENTS TO HANDLE.

Suppose G has a nontrivial cut E0 of size seven. Both shores contain at
least four vertices: a shore of size two or three has at least
5s-s(s-1), namely eight or nine, outgoing edges because delta(G)>=5.
A vertex cover of B of size at most three would leave vertices on both
shores and disconnect G after deleting that cover, violating reviewed
4-connectivity. The bipartite matching-cover equality, proved in the
round-three file, therefore gives mu(B)>=4. Lemma 2 shows that H is WKE.
If B is C4-free, its cut is reducible, so assume B has a C4 component K.

Write r=|E(K)|. If r=7, K is connected and cyclic and has at most seven
vertices, hence matching number at most three; with no other cut edges
this contradicts mu(B)>=4. Thus 4<=r<=6.

If r=4, K is the bare C4 and there are three edges in other components.
If r=5, the extra edge introduces one new vertex as a pendant leaf:
there is no extra edge available among the four vertices of K2,2.
The component then has matching number two, so the remaining two cut
edges must be separate isolated-edge components to reach matching four.

If r=6, only one cut edge remains outside K, so mu(K)>=3. Since K is
cyclic and connected, it has at most six vertices. Therefore it has
exactly six vertices and a perfect matching. Relative to its C4, its
two other edges add two vertices as attached trees. They form either a
pendant path of length two, or two pendant leaves. In the latter case
the leaves must attach at distinct adjacent cycle vertices: the same
vertex could not match both leaves, while opposite cycle vertices leave
two vertices in the same bipartition class after matching the leaves.
Conversely adjacent attachments admit a perfect matching. The seventh
cut edge is an isolated-edge component.

Hence the list is exhaustive:
 A. bare C4;
 B. C4 with one pendant edge;
 C. C4 with pendants at two adjacent cycle vertices;
 D. C4 with a pendant path of length two.
No numerical enumeration is used in this classification.

5. LABELED CERTIFICATES FOR THE FOUR SHAPES.

Common labels. The cycle has vertices x1,x2 on one shore and y1,y2
on the other. Its four cut edges are
 a=x1y1, b=x1y2, c=x2y1, d=x2y2.
Its possible internal rims are p=x1x2 and q=y1y2. In shapes B--D
add a cut edge e=x1y3, with y3 on the y-shore. This adds possible rims
r=y1y3 and s=y2y3. Shape C adds f=x3y1, giving possible rims
t=x1x3 and w=x2x3. Shape D instead adds f=x3y3, giving rim t=x1x3.

The following table lists every possible crossing triangle through its
two cut edges; it exists precisely when the indicated rim is present:

 rim     cut-edge pairs of its triangles
 p       (a,c), (b,d)
 q       (a,b), (c,d)
 r       (a,e)
 s       (b,e)
 t       (a,f) in C; (e,f) in D
 w       (c,f) in C only.

Writing (a,e), for example, denotes the unique triangle containing cut
edges a,e and its specified rim r, rather than a two-edge subgraph.
The table follows directly by listing pairs of incident cut edges.
It is complete even if other internal edges of G exist, because an
internal edge outside this list cannot be the rim of a crossing triangle.

In the cases where X is chosen to be all present rims, coverage and the
external-edge safety condition are automatic. We need only exhibit a
packing with size at least half the number k of those rims. Cut-edge
pairs listed together below are disjoint, and their rims are distinct,
so the corresponding crossing triangles are edge-disjoint.

A. BARE C4.
Let X be the subset of {p,q} that is present. If neither is present,
take S=empty. Otherwise choose any one crossing triangle using a present
rim. Then |X|<=2=2|S|. This is the reviewed C4 certificate.

B. ONE PENDANT EDGE.
There are four possible rims p,q,r,s. If k=0, use the empty certificate.
If 1<=k<=2, choose any one crossing triangle and cover all present rims.
If k>=3, choose two triangles as follows. When p and r are present,
use pairs (a,e),(b,d), with rims r,p. When p is present but r is absent,
we have s present and use (b,e),(a,c), with rims s,p. When p is absent,
all of q,r,s are present and we use (c,d),(a,e), with rims q,r.
Their two triangles are edge-disjoint, and all present rims give
|X|=k<=4=2|S|.

C. TWO ADJACENT PENDANTS.
There are six possible rims p,q,r,s,t,w. The cases k=0 and 1<=k<=2
are handled by an empty or one-triangle certificate as above.

If 3<=k<=4, two edge-disjoint triangles with distinct rims exist. If
p or q is present, some one of r,s,t,w is present, and its cut-edge
pair is disjoint from a pair with rim p or q. More explicitly:
 - with rim p: r pairs with (b,d), s with (a,c),
              t with (b,d), w with (b,d);
 - with rim q: r,s,t pair with (c,d), and w with (a,b).
In each line the first triangle is the unique pair for the listed
extra rim in the table. If neither p nor q is present, at least three
of r,s,t,w are present, so either both r,w or both s,t are present.
Use (a,e),(c,f) in the first case or (b,e),(a,f) in the second.
Covering all k rims costs at most four for these two triangles.

If k>=5, at least one of the disjoint triples of rim names
  {p,r,w}, {q,s,t}
is wholly present: missing an edge from each would leave at most four
rims. The first triple gives the three triangles
  (a,e), (c,f), (b,d),
with respective rims r,w,p. The second gives
  (b,e), (a,f), (c,d),
with respective rims s,t,q. Each packing uses the six distinct cut edges
once and distinct rims. Cover all k<=6 rims; this costs at most 2|S|.

D. A PENDANT PATH OF LENGTH TWO.
There are five possible rims p,q,r,s,t.

If neither p nor q is present, all crossing triangles contain cut edge
e: their possible pairs are (a,e),(b,e),(e,f). If there is no such
triangle use S=X=empty. Otherwise choose any one, with rim z, and use
X={e,z}. This two-edge cover meets every crossing triangle and contains
the selected triangle's only noncut edge.

Now suppose at least one of p,q is present. For k<=2 use any one
crossing triangle and all present rims as X. For 3<=k<=4, at least one
extra rim r,s,t is present. An r-triangle (a,e) is disjoint from
(b,d) with rim p or (c,d) with rim q. An s-triangle (b,e) is disjoint
from (a,c) with rim p or (c,d) with rim q. A t-triangle (e,f) is
disjoint from every core pair in the p and q rows. Thus two triangles
with distinct rims exist, and covering all k<=4 rims fits the budget.

Finally, if k=5, all five rims exist. Use the internal triangle
  y1 y2 y3
and the two crossing triangles
  x1 x2 y1,  x1 x3 y3.
The internal triangle uses rims q,r,s. The crossing triangles use
cut-edge pairs (a,c),(e,f) and rims p,t. All three triangles are
edge-disjoint. Their five noncut edges are exactly {p,q,r,s,t}; take
X to be those five edges. It meets every crossing triangle and has
|X|=5<=6=2|S|, including every external packing edge.

All four shapes therefore have complete certificates under every
pattern of internal edge presence. These are proved symbolic recipes,
not an assertion of unchecked pattern enumeration.

6. ASSEMBLY AND CONCLUSION.

In shape A, the other components of B together have at most three cut
edges; each has a C4-free cut graph and a compatibility graph on at
most three vertices, hence is WKE and admits the reviewed cut certificate.
Combine these with the bare-C4 certificate. In shapes B--D, all other
components are isolated edges and create no crossing triangles. Their
empty certificates combine with the displayed nonempty component's
certificate. In every case Section 3 gives a reduction of the entire
nonempty cut E0, contradicting minimality of G.

Thus no nontrivial cut of size seven exists. The reviewed round-three
theorem already excludes smaller nontrivial cuts and gives delta(G)>=5.
Every cut of size at most seven therefore isolates a vertex, whose
degree equals its size and belongs to {5,6,7}. This proves the theorem.

LIMITS.
This is a structural restriction on hypothetical minimum counterexamples,
not a proof that mad(G)<8 implies Tuza. It leaves the degree-five/six/seven
vertices themselves and graphs with no small nontrivial cuts. The
matching-number-two lemma depends on the classical Tutte--Berge formula;
the cut constructions and their exhaustiveness are proved explicitly.
No numerical search, broad census, certificate finder, or external
computation was used. Research stops at this theorem for review.
