MAD<8 / ROUND 3 / D / INDEPENDENT INITIAL DRAFT

Chosen route: small edge cuts, independent of the round-three mainline.
No E round-three material was inspected. No numerical work was run.

Candidate theorem: a minimum counterexample in a subgraph-closed class
(in particular mad<8) has minimum degree at least five and every edge
cut of size at most six isolates a single vertex. This strengthens the
round-two 4-connectivity result by ruling out nontrivial small edge cuts.

Local cut certificate. Let B be the bipartite graph formed by the edges
of a cut E0. Form a compatibility graph H whose vertices are cut edges,
with two vertices adjacent when those two cut edges belong to a triangle.
Every crossing triangle uses two cut edges and one internal rim edge.
If B has no C4, distinct crossing triangles have distinct rim edges:
two different centers using the same rim would make a C4 in B.

If H is WKE, choose a matching M and Q with |Q|<=|M| covering H-M.
Use the triangles corresponding to M as S, and put in X all their rim
edges together with the cut edges represented by Q. This is an edge-set
reduction: matching M prevents repeated cut edges; C4-freeness prevents
repeated rim edges; X meets every crossing triangle; external packing
edges are exactly the selected rims; |X|<=2|S|.

Needed elementary WKE facts: every graph on at most four vertices is
WKE. The only non-WKE graphs on exactly five vertices are K5 minus a
matching. Proof sketch for the latter: if matching number<=1 it is a
star/triangle plus isolates. Otherwise nu=2. If some vertex x has two
nonneighbors y,z, the triple {x,y,z} has at most one edge. If it is
independent, its complement is a two-vertex cover. If yz is its only
edge, extend yz to a two-edge matching if possible, with the other two
vertices as Q. If no extension exists, {y,z} itself covers all edges
and any two-edge matching suffices. Conversely K5 minus a matching
leaves at least two adjacent edges after deleting at most two vertices.

A cut with at most four edges is C4-free, except for a C4 itself. In
the latter case, the two possible internal diagonals cover all crossing
triangles; use one triangle if either diagonal is present (cover size
at most two), or the empty packing/cover if neither is present. Thus
every nonempty cut of size at most four is reducible and delta>=5.

For a nontrivial cut E0 of size m<=6 in a 4-connected minimum
counterexample, both shores have at least four vertices: a shore of
size 2 or 3 would have at least 5s-s(s-1)>=8 outgoing edges. A vertex
cover of B with at most three vertices would disconnect those shores
after deletion. Thus B has vertex-cover number at least four, and by
bipartite Konig it has matching number at least four. Hence
m-nu(B)<=2.

In a connected component with r cut edges, the matching number is at
most floor((r+1)/2), since it has at most r+1 vertices. Thus r>=6
would contribute matching deficiency at least three, impossible. Every
component has at most five edges; a five-edge component must be a
six-vertex tree with a perfect matching, while a cyclic component can
only be C4. If C4 occurs, its deficiency two forces all other components
to be isolated edges, so the explicit C4 certificate handles the cut.

Otherwise B is a forest. A compatibility graph on the five edges of a
five-edge component cannot be K5 minus a matching unless B is a star:
if two cut edges are disjoint, each has already used its sole allowed
nonneighbor, so the remaining three edges would all have to meet both.
In a bipartite graph there are at most two distinct edges meeting both
members of a disjoint pair. A star cannot have a perfect matching of
size three. Thus all compatibility components are WKE, and their union
is WKE. The local cut certificate gives a reduction, contradicting
minimality.
