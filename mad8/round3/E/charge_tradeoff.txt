GENERAL CHARGE CRITERION: FIVE HIGH-DEGREE NEIGHBORS CAN SUFFICE

Status: a purely structural counting lemma. It weakens the six-neighbor
requirement of Lemma D in p3_complement_links.txt to five when the
degree-seven links have minimum degree at least four. It does not
establish these structural hypotheses for any additional link family.
The original six-neighbor surplus bound is retained unchanged.

1. GENERAL CRITERION

Theorem. Let G be a nonempty finite simple graph of minimum degree
at least seven. Let L be its degree-seven vertices and H its vertices
of degree at least nine. Let h>=1 and r>=0 be integers with h+r>=9.
Suppose:

  (i) L is independent;
  (ii) each w in L has at least h neighbors in H;
  (iii) each link G[N(w)], w in L, has minimum degree at least r.

Then G has average degree at least eight.

Proof. If L is empty, every vertex has degree at least eight and the
conclusion is immediate. Otherwise, (ii) implies h<=7 and (iii)
implies r<=6. Thus the meaningful parameter range is

  1<=h<=7, 0<=r<=6, h+r>=9;

in particular h>=3.

Give each vertex its degree as initial charge. Each vertex in H
sends 1/h to each of its neighbors in L. Degree-eight vertices do
not send or receive charge. Transfers preserve the total charge.

A vertex in L receives at least h(1/h)=1, so finishes with charge
at least eight. A degree-eight vertex retains eight.

Consider v in H having a neighbor w in L. By (iii), v has at least
r neighbors inside G[N(w)]. These vertices lie in N(v), and by
(i) none belongs to L. Therefore

  |N(v) intersect L| <= d(v)-r.

The final charge at v is at least

  d(v)-(d(v)-r)/h = ((h-1)d(v)+r)/h.

Since h>=1, the last expression is nondecreasing in d(v). As
d(v)>=9, it is at least

  9-(9-r)/h = 8+(h+r-9)/h >=8.

A vertex of H with no neighbor in L sends nothing and retains
degree at least nine. Every vertex ends with at least eight,
proving the average-degree bound.

2. THE FIVE-NEIGHBOR SPECIALIZATION

Corollary. Suppose G has minimum degree at least seven, its set L
of degree-seven vertices is independent, every vertex of L has
at least five neighbors of degree at least nine, and every
degree-seven link has minimum degree at least four. Then G has
average degree at least eight.

Proof. Use h=5 and r=4. A degree-seven receiver gains at least
one unit. A degree-d donor, d>=9, loses at most (d-4)/5 and
therefore retains at least

  d-(d-4)/5=(4d+4)/5>=8.

Thus two of the seven neighbors of a receiver may have degree
eight; those neighbors are not charged. This is a conditional
counting statement, not a proof that any unclassified link type
has five high-degree neighbors or that L is independent there.

3. EXACT SURPLUS FORMULA IN THE MEANINGFUL PARAMETER RANGE

Assume 1<=h<=7 and 0<=r<=6, and retain (i)-(iii). Write

  q(w)=|N(w) intersect H| for w in L,
  n=|V(G)|.

The same transfer gives the more explicit bound

  2|E(G)|-8n >= (1/h) [
      sum over w in L of (q(w)-h)
      +(h+r-9)|H|
      +(h-1) sum over v in H of (d(v)-9)
    ].                                                     (1)

Proof. A vertex w in L has final charge exactly

  7+q(w)/h = 8+(q(w)-h)/h.

For v in H, the bound |N(v) intersect L|<=d(v)-r holds when v
has an L-neighbor by the preceding argument. It also holds when
it has none, since r<=6<d(v). Therefore its final charge minus
eight is at least

  [(h-1)(d(v)-9)+(h+r-9)]/h.

Degree-eight vertices have zero surplus. Summing these estimates
proves (1).

For h=5,r=4, this becomes

  2|E(G)|-8n >= [sum_L(q(w)-5)+4 sum_H(d(v)-9)]/5.

For h=6,r=4, it implies the earlier bound

  2|E(G)|/n >=8+|H|/(6n),

and retains additional nonnegative terms. Thus the stronger
six-neighbor hypothesis has a useful margin even though five
neighbors suffice to reach the threshold eight.

4. WHAT IS EXACT ABOUT THE CONSTANT TRANSFER TEST

For 1<=h<=7 and 0<=r<=6, suppose one uses a constant transfer t
from every H-vertex to each adjacent L-vertex, relying only on
the lower bound h at receivers and the upper bound d-r at donors.
The receiver estimate reaches eight precisely when

  7+ht>=8, or t>=1/h.

The worst donor estimate at degree nine reaches eight precisely
when

  9-(9-r)t>=8, or t<=1/(9-r).

If both inequalities hold, then t<=1/3<1, so the donor lower
bound d-(d-r)t is increasing in d and degree nine is indeed the
worst allowed donor degree. Hence these two scalar estimates
can certify the threshold eight with a constant t exactly when

  1/h <= t <= 1/(9-r),

an interval which is nonempty exactly when h+r>=9.

This is an exact statement about the stated worst-case arithmetic
test. It is not a claim that every extreme numerical pattern is
realized by a graph, nor that no other discharging rule or structural
argument could work when h+r<9. No graph-class optimality or new
classification is inferred from this interval calculation.

5. RELATION TO THE CURRENT MAINLINE

The matching-or-P3 theorem in p3_complement_links.txt already
forces six high-degree neighbors and minimum link degree four,
assuming the stated reducible configurations are absent. Its
stronger quantitative conclusion therefore remains valid.

The present refinement gives a smaller sufficient target for a
future structural argument: independence of the degree-seven
vertices, five high-degree neighbors each, and link minimum
degree four would already force average degree at least eight.
The degree-five/six gap and unclassified degree-seven links remain.
No new numerical experiment or link-family classification was used.
