THE FIRST NONMATCHING COMPLEMENT: A THREE-VERTEX PATH

Status: a complete conditional forcing theorem extending the matching-
complement family, using previously proved universal certificates;
and a precise unresolved mixed-degree position. No enumeration is used.
The full maximum-average-degree-below-eight question remains open.

1. SETUP AND ACCEPTED INPUTS

All graphs are finite and simple. The link of a vertex u is the induced
graph G[N(u)]. Say that a degree-seven vertex has matching type if the
complement of its link is a matching (possibly empty). Say it has path
type if that complement is a P3 and four isolated vertices: exactly two
missing edges sharing an endpoint.

For a path-type vertex u, write the missing link edges as va,vb. Call v
its exceptional neighbor. The other four neighbors of u will be denoted
C. Then C is a four-clique, the pair a,b is adjacent and complete to C,
and v is adjacent to all of C but to neither a nor b.

A reducible pair {u,v} has an edge-disjoint triangle set S and an edge
set X satisfying |X|<=2|S|, meeting every triangle through either hub,
and containing every packed edge avoiding both hubs.

We use two proved results from mad8/round2/E/matching_complement_links.txt:

  A. If u,v are adjacent, d(u)+d(v)<=15, and their common neighborhood
     contains a five-clique, then {u,v} is reducible by a seven-triangle
     Fano construction. The cover consists of the ten clique edges,
     uv, and all hub spokes outside those seven vertices; its size is
     d(u)+d(v)-1<=14.

  M. If u has degree seven and matching type, every neighbor v of
     degree at most eight forms a reducible pair with u.

The exact certificates and exhaustive matching-complement cases are
proved in that file. Their applicability does not require bounded
degrees at common neighbors or any WKE hypothesis.

We also use the eight-triangle certificate already proved in
mad8/round1/D/dense_pair_certificate.txt. Lemma Q below is exactly
that proposition with labels a1=a, a0=b, b0=c, b1=d and core order
x,y,p,q. It is reproduced for a self-contained verification, not
claimed as a new construction. The new step is its application to
the mutually exceptional degree-seven endpoints and the resulting
conditional discharging theorem.

2. ALL NONEXCEPTIONAL NEIGHBORS ALREADY REDUCE

Lemma P. Let u have degree seven and path type, with exceptional
neighbor v. If t is another neighbor of u and d(t)<=8, then {u,t}
is reducible.

Proof. Write the two missing link edges as va,vb.

If t is one of the four vertices of C, then t is adjacent to all six
other neighbors of u. In particular u and t have as common neighbors
the five-clique {a,b} union (C\{t}). All edges of this five-set are
present because neither of the two missing link edges lies in it.

If t=a, the common neighborhood of u and a contains the five-clique
{b} union C. The case t=b is symmetric.

These cases exhaust all t other than v. Since d(u)+d(t)<=15, result A
applies and proves the lemma.

Thus in a graph with no such reducible pair, all six nonexceptional
neighbors of a path-type degree-seven vertex have degree at least nine.
This alone leaves the exceptional neighbor free to have degree eight.

3. AN EXPLICIT PAIR WITH TWO PRIVATE PAIRS

Lemma Q. Let u,v be adjacent vertices of degree seven, with exactly
four common neighbors C={x,y,p,q}, and let

  N(u)\({v} union C)={a,b},
  N(v)\({u} union C)={c,d}.

Suppose C is a clique, a is adjacent to every vertex of C, and both
ab and cd are edges. Then {u,v} is reducible. No further attachment
condition is needed at b,c,d, and edges between the two private pairs
are unrestricted.

Proof. Define

  S={uxy, upq, vxp, vyq, axq, ayp, uab, vcd},

  X=E(G[C union {a}]) union {ab,cd,uv,ub,vc,vd}.

All eight triangles exist. For edge-disjointness, the first six use
the following edges:

  ux,uy,xy;
  up,uq,pq;
  vx,vp,xp;
  vy,vq,yq;
  ax,aq,xq;
  ay,ap,yp.

The six distinct core edges are xy,pq,xp,yq,xq,yp. The four a-core
edges and the eight displayed hub-core spokes are also distinct.
The last two triangles add ua,ub,ab and vc,vd,cd, six new edges.
Hence S is edge-disjoint and |S|=8.

The graph on C union {a} is K5, contributing ten edges to X. None of
the six remaining listed edges lies inside this K5, and those six
are pairwise distinct. Therefore |X|=16=2|S|.

Any triangle through both hubs meets uv. A triangle through u alone
that contains b meets ub. Otherwise its other two vertices lie in
C union {a}, and their edge is in X. A triangle through v alone
that contains c or d meets vc or vd; otherwise its rim edge belongs
to C and lies in X. This exhausts every ambient hub triangle because
the displayed neighborhoods contain all neighbors of the two hubs.

Finally, every packed edge avoiding {u,v} is either inside C union
{a}, or is ab or cd. All such edges lie in X. Thus all three
reducibility conditions hold. Edges between the private pairs or
edges leaving the local graph do not affect this verification.

Corollary Q1. Suppose u and v both have degree seven and path type,
and each is the exceptional neighbor of the other. Then {u,v} is
reducible.

Proof. The path-type structure at u gives a four-clique C, its
private pair a,b adjacent and complete to C, and v missing both
members of that private pair. The path-type structure at v gives
its two private vertices c,d adjacent. These are precisely the
hypotheses of Lemma Q.

4. THE DEGREE-SEVEN VERTICES BECOME INDEPENDENT

Lemma I. Suppose G has minimum degree at least seven and every
degree-seven vertex has matching type or path type. If no pair
supplied by results M, P, and Q1 is reducible,
then the set L of degree-seven vertices is independent. Every vertex
of L has at least six neighbors of degree at least nine and at most
one neighbor of degree eight.

Proof. Suppose u,v are adjacent vertices in L. If either has matching
type, result M gives a reducible pair. Otherwise both have path type.
If either is nonexceptional for the other, Lemma P gives a reducible
pair. The only remaining possibility is that each is exceptional
for the other, where Corollary Q1 applies. This contradiction proves
independence.

A matching-type vertex has only neighbors of degree at least nine,
by result M. At a path-type vertex, Lemma P forces its six
nonexceptional neighbors to have degree at least nine. Independence
prevents its exceptional neighbor from having degree seven. Under
minimum degree at least seven, that remaining neighbor has degree
eight or at least nine, as claimed.

The last sentence uses the minimum-degree assumption. Without it,
the independence claim and the six high-degree neighbors would
still hold, but the exceptional neighbor could have degree at most six.

5. ALLOWING ONE DEGREE-EIGHT NEIGHBOR WITHOUT CHARGING IT

Lemma D. Let G be a nonempty graph of minimum degree at least seven.
Let L be the degree-seven vertices and let H be the vertices of
degree at least nine. Suppose:

  (i) L is independent;
  (ii) every vertex of L has at least six neighbors in H;
  (iii) every degree-seven link has minimum degree at least four.

Then

  2|E(G)|/|V(G)| >= 8 + |H|/(6|V(G)|).             (1)

Proof. If v in H has a neighbor w in L, condition (iii) says that
v has at least four neighbors within G[N(w)]. Those four vertices
belong to N(v) and, by (i), are outside L. Consequently

  |N(v) intersect L| <= d(v)-4.                    (2)

Give each vertex its degree as initial charge. Each vertex in H
sends 1/6 to each adjacent vertex in L. Vertices of degree eight
send and receive nothing. Transfers preserve the total charge.

By (ii), each vertex of L receives at least 6(1/6)=1 and finishes
with charge at least eight. A degree-eight vertex retains eight.
For v in H with a neighbor in L, inequality (2) gives final charge
at least

  d(v)-(d(v)-4)/6=(5d(v)+4)/6>=49/6=8+1/6.

If v has no neighbor in L, it retains d(v)>=9>49/6. Summing final
charges proves (1).

This lemma is stated separately because its three hypotheses are
the actual discharging requirements. The degree-eight vertices are
never asked to donate charge above their initial value.

6. COMPLETE CONDITIONAL FORCING THEOREM

Theorem T. Let G have minimum degree at least seven and average
degree below eight. Suppose the complement of every degree-seven
link is either a matching, or a P3 with four isolated vertices.
Then G has a reducible pair supplied by result M, Lemma P, or
Corollary Q1.

Proof. Suppose none of these pairs occurs. By Lemma I, L is
independent and each of its vertices has at least six neighbors
of degree at least nine. A matching-type link has minimum degree
at least five, while a path-type link has minimum degree four.
Thus all hypotheses of Lemma D hold. It gives average degree at
least eight, a contradiction.

This applies in particular under mad(G)<8, with the same local
hypotheses. Consequently a subgraph-minimal counterexample in that
maximum-average-degree class, if it has minimum degree at least
seven, must contain a degree-seven link whose complement is neither
a matching nor a P3 with isolated vertices.

7. EXACT SCOPE AND REMAINING POSITION

The theorem extends round two by one complete nonmatching family,
the two-adjacent-missing-edges link. It does not assert that every
neighbor of degree at most eight of such a degree-seven vertex
forms a reducible pair. Its exceptional (7,8) position is still
unresolved here: common core K4, an adjacent u-private pair complete
to that core, and three v-private vertices with unrestricted
attachments. The discharging proof tolerates that one degree-eight
neighbor instead of using an unproved reduction for it.

The reused certificate in Lemma Q uses two degree-seven endpoints;
it is not transferred to a degree-eight endpoint without accounting
for the additional hub triangles. All common-neighbor degrees are
unrestricted, and no old 7-regular attachment budget is invoked.

Degree-five and degree-six vertices remain an independent gap in
the full mad<8 problem. More general degree-seven complements,
including three-edge stars, longer paths and triangles, are not
covered unless a separate argument yields the hypotheses of
Lemma D or an already proved reducible configuration.

The link-family assumption is not hereditary under arbitrary edge
deletions. Theorem T is a local forcing result used at a minimal
counterexample, not an unsupported induction on that link family.
No numerical scan, fixed-catalogue coverage claim, or original
certificate finder is part of the proof.
