ACCEPT the proposed mutual-center (7,7) pair certificate. Write C={x,y,p,q}=K4, u-private a,b with a complete to C and ab present, v-private c,d with cd present. The displayed eight triangles uxy, upq, vxp, vyq, axq, ayp, uab, vcd are present and edge-disjoint: their six core rim edges partition E(C), the four a-core edges are distinct, and hub spokes do not repeat. X=E(C∪{a})∪{ab,cd,uv,ub,vc,vd} has 10+6=16 actual edges. Every ambient triangle through u or v meets uv, ub, vc/vd, or a rim edge in E(C∪{a}); all packed edges avoiding both hubs, including ab and cd, lie in X. Extra attachments of b,c,d do not defeat coverage. This proves only the mutual-center (7,7) case, not a P3-center (7,8) pair.

ACCEPT the conditional discharging consequence. If δ≥7, every degree-7 link complement is a matching or P3, and no reducible pair exists, then degree-7 vertices L are independent: matching cases use round-two Theorem M; P3 noncenter cases use K5; two P3 centers use the certificate above. Each P3 vertex has at most one possible degree-8 neighbor (its center), so it has at least six degree-9+ neighbors. A degree-d≥9 donor adjacent to w∈L has at least four neighbors among N(w)\{v} because every matching/P3-complement link has minimum degree at least four. Independence of L makes these four outside L, hence d_L(v)≤d−4. Transferring 1/6 from each 9+ neighbor to L gives final charges ≥8 for degree 7, exactly 8 for degree 8, and ≥(5d+4)/6≥49/6 for degree d≥9. Therefore average degree ≥8+n_{≥9}/(6n). The theorem is conditional; degree 5/6 and other degree-7 links remain unresolved.
