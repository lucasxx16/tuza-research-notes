MAD<8 / D / ROUND 1
LOCAL-DENSITY OBSTRUCTION AND THE RELATIVE-CERTIFICATE BOTTLENECK

Status: Reviewed research proof; see C/review_full_candidates.txt.
These statements do not resolve whether mad(G)<8 implies tau(G)<=2nu(G).
They identify a precise limitation on extending the degree-seven argument.

Definitions. For adjacent hubs u,v of degree seven, put
C=N(u) intersect N(v), A=N(u) minus ({v} union C),
B=N(v) minus ({u} union C), and c=|C|.
The reduced local patch P has vertices {u,v} union C union A union B,
all its edges incident to u or v, arbitrary edges within C, within A and
within B, and arbitrary A--C and B--C edges. The patch omits A--B edges.
Thus |A|=|B|=6-c. Omitting A--B edges changes neither hub's link.
Every triangle containing u or v lies in P, even if A--B edges are
present in an ambient graph.

PROPOSITION 1 (uniform local density bounds).
Every such patch satisfies
  mad(P)<=37/5  when c=4,
  mad(P)<=22/3  when c=5,
  mad(P)<=7     when c=6.
Each bound is attained by the patch having every allowed edge.

Proof. Since maximum average degree is monotone under taking subgraphs,
it suffices to analyze the maximal patch J_c. Every allowed edge is
present, so its edge set is that of two complete graphs K7 on
{u} union C union A and {v} union C union B, overlapping in C,
together with uv. Hence
 |V(J_c)|=14-c,  |E(J_c)|=43-binomial(c,2).

For c=4, J_4 has ten vertices and 37 edges. A core vertex has degree
nine, a hub has degree seven, and a private vertex has degree six.
Thus every induced subgraph on nine vertices has at most 37-6=31
edges, and average degree at most 62/9<37/5. Every subgraph on at most
eight vertices has average degree at most seven, because the complete
graph on eight vertices has degree seven. The full graph has average
degree 74/10=37/5. It follows that mad(J_4)=37/5.

For c=5, J_5 has nine vertices and 33 edges, so its average degree is
22/3. Every proper vertex subset has at most eight vertices and average
degree at most seven. Deleting edges cannot increase average degree on
a fixed vertex set. Therefore mad(J_5)=22/3.

For c=6, there are no private vertices and J_6=K8, whose maximum average
degree is seven. This proves every assertion.

PROPOSITION 2 (the old pointwise budget fails inside mad<8).
The graph J_4 has two adjacent degree-seven vertices u,v of codegree four,
both of whose links are connected and non-WKE, but every core vertex c
has
 deg_{J_4[C]}(c)+|N(c) intersect A|+|N(c) intersect B|=3+2+2=7.
In particular the upper bound five used by the degree-seven proof fails,
although mad(J_4)=37/5<8.

Proof. The degree and density calculations are in Proposition 1. Each
hub's link has seven vertices, consisting of four core vertices and
three others (the opposite hub and two private vertices). All four core
vertices are universal in the link. In particular the link is connected.

Suppose its link F were WKE, with a matching M and Q satisfying
|Q|<=|M| and F-Q contained in M. On seven vertices, |M|<=3. Therefore
|Q|<=3, and at least one of the four universal core vertices remains
outside Q. There are at least four vertices outside Q, so that universal
vertex has degree at least three in F-Q. This contradicts F-Q being a
subgraph of a matching. Thus both links are non-WKE. The displayed
pointwise degree sum follows directly from maximality of the patch.

COROLLARY 3 (no local pruning from mad<8 alone).
Every codegree-four local triple consisting of a core and two side
patterns, including every triple with connected non-WKE links, is realized
by a graph of maximum average degree at most 37/5: take its reduced local
patch itself. Hence the hypothesis mad(G)<8 alone excludes none of these
local triples. The same statement holds at codegrees five and six with
the respective bounds in Proposition 1.

More generally, the same no-pruning conclusion holds for mad(G)<t for
every real t>37/5 at codegree four. At t=37/5 the strict hypothesis
excludes the maximal patch, so the strict inequality on t matters.

This corollary concerns realization inside the class, not realization
inside a MINIMAL COUNTEREXAMPLE. Additional irreducibility, robustness,
or global discharging constraints may still rule out configurations.
Proposition 2 is not a counterexample to Tuza's conjecture.

PROPOSITION 4 (ordinary Tuza already holds for the reduced patches).
Every degree-seven pair patch with c<=5 has treewidth at most six. In
particular every patch at codegree four or five satisfies tau(P)<=2nu(P)
by the theorem of Botler, Fernandes, and Gutierrez. The codegree-six
patch also satisfies Tuza, by the maximum-degree-seven theorem in the
source manuscript, or the appropriate known small complete-graph case.

Proof of the treewidth statement. Use a three-node path of bags
 L={u} union C union A,
 M={u,v} union C,
 R={v} union C union B,
in that order. The outer bags have size seven and the middle bag has
size c+2<=7. Every allowed edge is contained in one bag: uv is in M,
the left-side and u-edges are in L or M, and the right-side and v-edges
are in R or M. Every vertex occurs on a connected set of bags: core
vertices occur throughout, u in L,M, v in M,R, A only in L, and B only
in R. This is a tree decomposition of width at most six.

The invoked theorem is Theorem 3.4 of Botler--Fernandes--Gutierrez,
"On Tuza's conjecture for triangulations and graphs with small treewidth":
https://arxiv.org/html/2002.07925v2
(arXiv:2002.07925v2, 16 July 2020; primary-source Theorem 3.4 checked).
The tree decomposition and density bounds above are independent arguments.

WHY THIS DOES NOT YIELD A PAIR REDUCTION.
An ordinary Tuza witness only supplies a triangle packing S and a cover X
with |X|<=2|S| and X meeting all local triangles. For removal of {u,v},
one also needs X to contain EVERY packing edge with both endpoints
outside {u,v}. An ordinary cover need hit only one edge of each packed
triangle; it may leave external packing edges available for a recursive
packing outside the two hubs. The union of the two packings may then
reuse an edge. The treewidth theorem does not assert this boundary
condition. Thus proving ordinary Tuza on larger collections of these
patches cannot by itself replace the local relative-certificate theorem.

The precise possible next target is a boundary strengthening:
  Given a reduced degree-seven pair patch whose links are connected
  and non-WKE, find a pair-reduction certificate without the old
  pointwise core budget, or characterize the patches where this fails.
This remains unproved here and would still not settle mad<8 by itself,
because a low-degree vertex in a mad<8 graph can have higher-degree
neighbors, so pairs of two degree-seven hubs need not suffice.
