MAD<8 / D / ROUND 1
AN EDGE-GLUING LEMMA AND A MINIMAL-COUNTEREXAMPLE CONSEQUENCE

Status: Reviewed research proof; see C/review_full_candidates.txt.
This is an elementary structural lemma. No novelty claim is made.

PROPOSITION (gluing along one edge).
Suppose G=G_1 union G_2, with V(G_1) intersect V(G_2)={x,y},
E(G_1) intersect E(G_2)={e}, where e=xy. There are no other edges in G.
If Tuza's inequality holds for each G_i and each G_i-e, then it holds
for G. If e belongs to every maximum triangle packing of at least one
G_i, then in fact tau(G)<=2nu(G)-1.

Proof. Every triangle of G is contained in one side: a triangle using
a vertex of G_1\{x,y} and a vertex of G_2\{x,y} would need a nonexistent
edge between these vertices. Thus packings on the two sides can conflict
only at e.

Put a_i=nu(G_i-e) and epsilon_i=nu(G_i)-a_i. Then epsilon_i is either
zero or one. Indeed deleting e removes at most one member of a triangle
packing, while a packing in G_i-e is also one in G_i. Moreover epsilon_i=1
if and only if every maximum packing of G_i uses e.

The exact packing identity is
 nu(G)=a_1+a_2+max(epsilon_1,epsilon_2).
For the upper bound, a packing uses e in at most one side; its contribution
from that side is at most a_i+epsilon_i, and the other side contributes
at most a_j. If it avoids e in both sides, it has at most a_1+a_2 members.
For the lower bound, combine maximum packings avoiding e on both sides
if both epsilons vanish. Otherwise use a maximum packing on a side with
epsilon_i=1 and a maximum packing avoiding e on the other side. These
are edge-disjoint and attain the stated value.

If both epsilons vanish, the union of minimum covers of G_1 and G_2 is
a cover of G, so
 tau(G)<=tau(G_1)+tau(G_2)<=2a_1+2a_2=2nu(G).

If at least one epsilon equals one, choose e itself and minimum covers
of G_1-e and G_2-e. This covers every triangle, and gives
 tau(G)<=1+tau(G_1-e)+tau(G_2-e)
       <=1+2a_1+2a_2=2nu(G)-1.
This proves both conclusions.

REMARK (more than two sides).
Exactly the same proof applies to any finite collection of sides sharing
only the same edge e. The packing formula becomes
 nu(G)=sum_i nu(G_i-e)+max_i(nu(G_i)-nu(G_i-e)).
Tuza for all G_i and G_i-e again implies Tuza for their union, with one
unit of slack if any side requires e in every maximum packing.

COROLLARY (a minimal mad<8 counterexample is 3-connected).
If a graph satisfying mad(G)<8 violates Tuza's inequality, choose one
with |V(G)|+|E(G)| minimum. Then it has at least four vertices and is
3-connected.

Proof. The property mad<8 is hereditary under subgraphs. Every proper
subgraph of the chosen graph therefore satisfies Tuza by minimality.
Graphs on at most three vertices satisfy Tuza directly, so |V(G)|>=4.

A disconnected graph splits into proper components, with tau and nu
both additive, and hence cannot be a minimal counterexample. If a cut
vertex exists, split G into two proper subgraphs meeting only there.
No triangle crosses the split and their edge sets are disjoint, so tau
and nu are again additive. This is impossible.

Suppose {x,y} is a two-vertex separator. Split the components of
G-{x,y} into two nonempty groups and let G_1,G_2 be the induced graphs
on each group together with x,y. Both are proper subgraphs. If xy is
absent, their edge sets are disjoint and every triangle lies in one side,
so additivity proves Tuza for G. If xy is present, G_i and G_i-xy are
all proper subgraphs satisfying Tuza, and the proposition proves Tuza
for G. This contradiction rules out every two-vertex separator and
completes the proof.

The same minimal-counterexample conclusion holds for any class closed
under arbitrary subgraphs (a monotone class), not just mad<8, whenever a counterexample is chosen with
minimum |V|+|E| within that class. This does not establish that any
counterexample exists.

SCOPE.
This lemma supplies a rigorous simplification of a hypothetical minimal
counterexample and a closure rule for subclasses already satisfying
Tuza. It does not settle graphs with separators of order at least three,
or the mad<8 question as a whole. No computation or external theorem is
used in the proof.
