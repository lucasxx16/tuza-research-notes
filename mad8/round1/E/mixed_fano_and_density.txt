A MIXED-DEGREE FANO REDUCTION AND A SHARP CONDITIONAL DENSITY BOUND

Status: explicit universal reducibility lemma; a complete conditional
forcing theorem; and a concrete obstruction to using only the old
low-degree discharging rules. This is partial progress toward
mad(G)<8, not a proof of that conjectural implication.

1. DEFINITIONS AND ORIGIN OF THE CONSTRUCTION

A nonempty vertex set V0 is reducible if there are an edge-disjoint
triangle set S and edge set X such that |X|<=2|S|, X meets every
triangle containing a vertex of V0, and every S-edge whose endpoints
both avoid V0 belongs to X.

A vertex is simplicial if its open neighborhood induces a clique.
The maximum average degree mad(G) is the maximum of 2|E(J)|/|V(J)|
over nonempty subgraphs J of G.

Gupta's Fano witness for the complete codegree-six core uses seven
triangles and thirteen cover edges inside K8. The one-edge slack
permits the extension below. The seven-triangle construction is
reproduced and verified here; novelty of the underlying Fano witness
is not claimed. The new use is its extension beyond two degree-seven
endpoints and the resulting conditional density theorem.

Source: Gupta, "Tuza's conjecture for graphs of maximum degree at
most seven," Section 7, Lemma "a Fano witness for K6" (label
lem:fano6 in the upstream source).

2. UNIVERSAL MIXED-DEGREE REDUCTION

Lemma F. Let K be an eight-vertex clique in an arbitrary finite
simple graph G, and let u,v be distinct vertices of K. Suppose

  |N(u) outside K| + |N(v) outside K| <= 1.

Then {u,v} is reducible. No degree bound is imposed on the other
six clique vertices or on vertices outside K.

Proof. Label K\{u,v}={c0,c1,c2,c3,c4,z}. Define

  S={uvc0, uc1c2, uc3c4, vc1c3, vc2c4, c0c1c4, c0c2c3}.

The three edges of these triangles, in order, are

  uv,uc0,vc0;
  uc1,uc2,c1c2;
  uc3,uc4,c3c4;
  vc1,vc3,c1c3;
  vc2,vc4,c2c4;
  c0c1,c1c4,c0c4;
  c0c2,c2c3,c0c3.

All twenty-one displayed edges are distinct, so S is edge-disjoint
and has seven triangles. Put

  X0=E(G[{c0,c1,c2,c3,c4}]) union {uv,uz,vz},
  Y={uw:w outside K and uw is an edge}
      union {vw:w outside K and vw is an edge},
  X=X0 union Y.

The first set has ten clique edges and three hub edges, so |X0|=13.
The hypothesis gives |Y|<=1; Y is disjoint from X0. Therefore
|X|<=14=2|S|.

Every triangle through u that uses a vertex outside K contains an
edge of Y. Inside K, a triangle through both u and v meets uv.
A triangle through u and z meets uz. Any other triangle through
u has its remaining vertices in {c0,...,c4}, and therefore meets
their edge in X0. The same proof applies to v, using vz. Thus X
covers all ambient triangles through either selected vertex.

Every S-edge avoiding {u,v} is an edge among c0,...,c4 and hence
belongs to X0. This proves the final reducibility condition.

Corollary F1. If u is a simplicial vertex of degree seven and
v is a neighbor of degree at most eight, {u,v} is reducible.

Proof. K=N[u] is an eight-clique. The vertex u has no neighbors
outside K. The vertex v has its seven other clique vertices as
neighbors, so at most one neighbor outside K. Apply Lemma F.

Consequently, in a graph with no reducible pair, every neighbor
of a simplicial degree-seven vertex has degree at least nine.

3. A SHARP CONDITIONAL DENSITY LEMMA

Lemma D. Let G be a nonempty graph of minimum degree at least
seven. Suppose every degree-seven vertex is simplicial and every
neighbor of a degree-seven vertex has degree at least nine.
Write n=|V(G)| and n8 for the number of degree-eight vertices.
Then

  2|E(G)|/n >= 8 + (2/5)(n-n8)/n.                 (1)

In particular the average degree is at least eight. If G has no
degree-eight vertices, its average degree is at least 42/5.

Proof. Let L be the degree-seven vertices. The neighbor-degree
assumption makes L independent, and no degree-eight vertex has
a neighbor in L.

Consider a vertex v of degree d>=9 that has a neighbor w in L.
Because w is simplicial, the six vertices of N(w)\{v} are all
adjacent to v. Each of them has degree at least nine, by the
neighbor-degree assumption at w. Thus N(v) contains at least
six vertices outside L, which gives

  |N(v) intersect L| <= d-6.                     (2)

Give each vertex its degree as initial charge. Each vertex of L
takes 1/5 from each of its seven neighbors. Transfers conserve
the total charge. A vertex in L ends with

  7+7/5 = 42/5.

A degree-eight vertex sends and receives nothing, so ends with
eight. A degree-d vertex, d>=9, with a neighbor in L loses at
most (d-6)/5 by (2), and therefore ends with at least

  d-(d-6)/5 = (4d+6)/5 >= 42/5.

A degree-d vertex with no neighbor in L retains d>=9>42/5.
Summing final charges yields

  2|E(G)| >= 8 n8 + (42/5)(n-n8),

which is exactly (1).

Sharpness. Let G be the join of a seven-clique and an independent
three-set. Its three independent vertices are simplicial of degree
seven; its seven clique vertices have degree nine. It meets all
the hypotheses, has ten vertices and forty-two edges, and has
average degree 42/5. Thus the constant in the no-degree-eight
case cannot be improved under these hypotheses. No claim that
this example has no other reducible configuration is made.

The term involving n8 is also necessary: any eight-regular graph
has n=n8 and meets the hypotheses vacuously, with average degree
eight. Disjoint unions of such graphs and the sharp ten-vertex
example attain equality in (1).

4. COMPLETE CONDITIONAL FORCING THEOREM

Theorem. Let G have minimum degree at least seven and average
degree below eight. If every degree-seven vertex is simplicial,
then G contains a reducible pair of the form in Corollary F1.

Proof. If no such pair exists, Corollary F1 implies that every
neighbor of a degree-seven vertex has degree at least nine.
Lemma D then gives average degree at least eight, a contradiction.

In particular this theorem applies under mad(G)<8 with the stated
minimum-degree and simpliciality assumptions. It is a local forcing
theorem, not an induction proof for all graphs in that subclass:
the hypotheses need not survive arbitrary edge deletions. Its
application to a subgraph-minimal counterexample is legitimate
whenever that counterexample has the stated local properties.

5. A SMALL OBSTRUCTION TO AN UNMODIFIED DEGREE-SEVEN ROUTE

Let G0=K9-ab, and call the other seven vertices C. The two vertices
a,b have degree seven and are not adjacent; each vertex in C has
degree eight. Hence G0 has no adjacent degree-seven pair.

Its maximum average degree is exactly 70/9<8. Indeed G0 itself
has nine vertices and thirty-five edges. Any subgraph on at most
eight vertices has average degree at most seven, and a subgraph
on nine vertices has at most thirty-five edges.

All its links are connected and non-WKE. The links at a,b are K7.
For any matching M in K7, |M|<=3; deleting at most three vertices
leaves a clique on at least four vertices, whose edges cannot all
lie in a matching. Thus no vertex set Q with |Q|<=|M| covers
K7-M. At each c in C the link is K8 minus one edge. Here a
matching has size at most four, and deleting at most four vertices
leaves at least four vertices inducing at least five edges. Those
edges likewise cannot all belong to a matching. This proves the
non-WKE assertions directly. Connectedness is immediate, and all
links have at least five vertices, so G0 is robust.

There are no vertices of degree at most six. Therefore all the
low-degree transfers in Puleo's Lemma 2.8 are zero, and all of
the necessary constraints concerning such vertices in his Lemma
2.7 hold vacuously. Nevertheless the average degree is below eight.
Thus those constraints plus connected non-WKE links cannot force
an adjacent degree-seven pair or average degree at least eight.

The order nine is minimal for this specific obstruction with
minimum degree seven and at least one degree-seven vertex but no
adjacent degree-seven pair: on at most seven vertices minimum
degree seven is impossible, and on eight vertices it forces K8.

This graph is not a counterexample to Tuza. Choose u=a and any
v in C. Then K=N[a] is an eight-clique, u has no external neighbor,
and v has the unique external neighbor b. Lemma F gives a literal
seven-triangle packing and fourteen-edge cover reducing {a,v}.
This is a mixed (7,8) pair, exactly the kind the regular-only
argument does not address.

6. LIMITS AND NEXT OBSTRUCTION

The proved reduction tolerates arbitrarily high degrees at the
six common clique vertices, so it does not import the old common-
vertex degree budget. It does not settle incomplete six-vertex
cores, other codegrees for a (7,8) pair, or non-simplicial degree-
seven vertices. Degree-five and degree-six deficits also remain.

The complete mad<8 question is therefore still open in these notes.
No graph census, witness finder, or floating-point optimization is
a premise of any result in this file.
