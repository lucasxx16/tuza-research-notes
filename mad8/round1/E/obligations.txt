MAXIMUM AVERAGE DEGREE BELOW EIGHT: EXACT PROOF OBLIGATIONS

Target: mad(G)<8 implies tau(G)<=2 nu(G), as asked in Gupta's
Question 12.1 (label q:mad8 in the upstream source).

1. MINIMALITY THAT IS ACTUALLY AVAILABLE

If the target fails, choose a counterexample minimizing |V|+|E|.
Every proper subgraph still has maximum average degree below eight
and satisfies Tuza's inequality by minimality. Thus the standard
reducible-vertex-set and reducible-edge-set extension lemmas imply
that this graph has neither kind of nonempty reducible set.

The graph is connected, because packing and covering numbers add
over components. It has no isolated vertices. Every edge lies in
a triangle: removing an edge lying in no triangle leaves both
packing and covering numbers unchanged, contradicting minimality.

Puleo's local lemmas then give robustness, minimum degree at least
five, and no WKE link component. A link at a vertex of degree at
most nine is connected, because two components of order at least
five require at least ten vertices. Vertices of degree at most six
are independent. These consequences do not bound the degrees of
neighbors of a degree-seven vertex.

Primary source: Puleo, arXiv:1308.2211v3, Sections 2, 4 and 5,
especially Definition 2.1, Lemmas 2.2 and 2.6, Lemma 4.4,
Corollary 4.9, and Proposition 5.2:
https://arxiv.org/html/1308.2211v3

2. WHAT A COMPLETE LOCAL/DISCHARGING ROUTE MUST SUPPLY

(a) Valid reductions must meet all three conditions: an edge-disjoint
    triangle packing S; a cover X of every triangle touching the
    proposed deleted vertex set; and containment in X of every
    S-edge outside that set, with |X|<=2|S|.

(b) A forcing argument must apply to arbitrary robust irreducible
    graphs, not only regular graphs. It must force a reduction or
    a subgraph of average degree at least eight.

(c) Degrees five and six remain possible under the known necessary
    conditions. Proving a statement only for minimum degree seven
    does not finish the target.

(d) At degree seven, high-codegree neighbors may have arbitrarily
    large total degree. For an adjacent pair with codegree c, the
    exclusive-neighborhood sizes are d(u)-1-c and d(v)-1-c.
    The old equality |A|=|B|=6-c requires both endpoint degrees
    to equal seven. The old common-vertex attachment budget also
    requires an actual degree bound on the common vertex; it is
    not a consequence of mad(G)<8.

(e) A discharging proof targeting eight must control both the new
    deficit at degree seven and the old deficits at degrees five
    and six. A degree-eight donor has no initial surplus above
    eight, so its obligations must be excluded or compensated.

3. THE EXISTING RULE DOES NOT AUTOMATICALLY EXTEND

Puleo's Lemma 2.8 transfers 2/3 to a degree-five vertex from each
subsuming neighbor, 1/6 to a thin degree-six vertex from every
neighbor, and 1/4 to a non-thin degree-six vertex from each
subsuming neighbor. Sections 5 and 7 justify final charge at
least seven. These rates do not address degree-seven deficits
relative to a target of eight.

The graph K9 minus one edge, proved in mixed_fano_and_density.txt
to have mad=70/9<8, has no vertices of degree at most six. Hence
all these transfers are zero, and its two degree-seven vertices
remain below eight. It satisfies the listed low-degree necessary
conditions and has connected non-WKE links everywhere. Thus
those particular necessary conditions alone do not force average
degree at least eight. The graph is not a Tuza counterexample;
an explicit new mixed-degree reduction is provided in that file.

4. ROUND RESULT AND THE PRECISE REMAINING GAP

The companion file proves an explicit reducible pair in an
eight-clique whenever its two selected vertices have at most one
external incident edge in total. In particular it handles a
simplicial degree-seven vertex next to a vertex of degree at most
eight. It also proves a sharp conditional density bound and hence
forces such a pair whenever minimum degree is seven, all degree-
seven vertices are simplicial, and average degree is below eight.

This does not prove the target. The remaining cases include
graphs with degree-five or degree-six vertices and graphs with
non-simplicial degree-seven vertices. No claim that those cases
reduce to the proved conditional theorem is made.

No census, original certificate finder, or new numerical graph
enumeration is used in these arguments. The seven-vertex link
census is not needed for the round result.
