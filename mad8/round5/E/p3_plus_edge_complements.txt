ADDING THE P3+K2 COMPLEMENT AT DEGREE SEVEN

Status: two explicit universal reducibility certificates, a complete
case analysis for the P3+K2 complement, and an enlarged conditional
forcing theorem. The proof is symbolic and does not depend on a
solver or graph census. The full mad(G)<8 implication remains open.

1. DEFINITIONS AND PREVIOUS RESULTS

All graphs are finite and simple. A reducible pair {u,v} has a set S
of edge-disjoint triangles and an edge set X with |X|<=2|S|, such
that X meets every triangle through u or v and contains every S-edge
whose endpoints both avoid {u,v}.

The complement types below concern the seven-vertex induced link
G[N(u)] of a degree-seven vertex u. Isolated complement vertices are
included so that each complement has seven vertices.

  M: a matching, possibly empty;
  P: a P3 and four isolated vertices;
  Q: a P4 and three isolated vertices;
  R: a P3, a disjoint K2, and two isolated vertices.

Type M has no exceptional neighbor. The P3 center is exceptional
for types P and R; the two internal P4 vertices are exceptional
for type Q. All other neighbors are called selected neighbors.
There are respectively seven, six, five, and six selected neighbors.

The accepted inputs are the following.

(A) At a type-M degree-seven vertex, every neighbor of degree at
    most eight gives a reducible pair. See Theorem M in
    mad8/round2/E/matching_complement_links.txt.

(B) At a type-P or type-Q degree-seven vertex, every selected
    neighbor of degree at most eight gives a reducible pair.
    See mad8/round3/E/p3_complement_links.txt and
    mad8/round4/E/p4_complement_links.txt.

(C) Adjacent degree-seven vertices of types P/Q that are exceptional
    for each other form a reducible pair. Their common core is K4,
    and the certificate is the one in
    mad8/round1/D/dense_pair_certificate.txt. Its exact application
    across P and Q is proved in the round-four file.

(D) The repaired K5-minus-edge lemma: for adjacent u,v with
    d(u)+d(v)<=15, suppose a common five-set W induces K5-xy.
    If a neighbor w of u lies outside {v} union W and wx is
    present, then {u,v} is reducible. The vertex w may also be a
    neighbor of v. This is Lemma C in the round-two file.

These inputs include ambient triangle coverage and the external-
packing-edge requirement. No degree bound on common neighbors is
part of them.

2. REPAIRING TWO INCIDENT MISSING CORE EDGES

Lemma F. Let u,v be adjacent, with d(u)+d(v)<=15. Suppose a
five-set W={a,b,c,p,q} in N(u) intersect N(v) induces precisely
K5 minus the two edges ab,bc. Suppose w is a neighbor of u
outside {v} union W, and wa,wc are present. Then {u,v} is
reducible. No other adjacency of w is required.

Proof. Take

  S={uva,apq,ubp,vbq,ucq,vcp,wac}.

For direct verification, the edge lists of these seven triangles are

  uv,ua,va;
  ap,aq,pq;
  ub,up,bp;
  vb,vq,bq;
  uc,uq,cq;
  vc,vp,cp;
  wa,wc,ac.

All twenty-one edges exist and are distinct. Equivalently, start
with the Fano triples

  abc, uva, apq, ubp, vbq, ucq, vcp

on {u,v} union W. The two missing edges occur only in abc.
Remove that triple, which also frees ac, and insert wac. Thus
S is an edge-disjoint packing of seven triangles.

Define

  X=E(G[W]) union {wa,wc,uv}
      union {ut:t in N(u) outside ({v} union W)}
      union {vt:t in N(v) outside ({u} union W)}.

The displayed parts are edge-disjoint. Since W has eight edges,

  |X|=8+3+(d(u)-6)+(d(v)-6)
      =d(u)+d(v)-1<=14=2|S|.

A triangle through both hubs meets uv. A triangle through just
one hub with a rim vertex outside W meets its deleted hub spoke.
Otherwise its rim edge lies in G[W] and belongs to X. This covers
all ambient hub triangles. The packed edges avoiding both hubs
are exactly E(G[W]) union {wa,wc}, all in X.

This verifies every reducibility condition. In particular, the
new outside triangle wac is paid for by including all three of
its edges in X; ac was already among the eight core edges.

3. A PAIR WITH COMMON CORE K4 MINUS ONE EDGE

Lemma K. Let u,v be adjacent degree-seven vertices with common
neighborhood C={x,y,p,q}, inducing K4 minus xy. Let their private
pairs be A={a,b} at u and B={c,d} at v. Suppose

  ab, cd, dp, dq

are present. Then {u,v} is reducible by seven triangles and a
fourteen-edge cover. No other private-core attachment is required.
Edges between the two private pairs are unrestricted.

Provenance. This smaller pattern was discovered in the bounded
local search recorded in
mad8/round5/B/mutual_centers_witness.json, then independently
checked by literal reconstruction. The universal proof below
does not rely on a solver status. An earlier symbolic eight-
triangle construction, with different attachment hypotheses,
is retained in alternate_k4_minus_edge_certificate.txt.

Proof. Set

  S={uxq,uyp,uab,vxp,vyq,vcd,dpq},
  R=E(G[C]) union {ab,cd,dp,dq},
  X=R union {uv,ua,ub,vc,vd}.

All seven triangles exist. At u the three hub triangles use
disjoint rim pairs {x,q}, {y,p}, {a,b}; at v they use disjoint
pairs {x,p}, {y,q}, {c,d}. Their four core rims are xq,yp,xp,yq.
The triangle dpq uses the fifth core edge pq and the two new
edges dp,dq. The private edges ab,cd are used once each.
Hence all triangles are edge-disjoint and |S|=7.

The rim set R has 5+4=9 edges, and the five additional hub
edges are distinct from each other and from R. Thus
|X|=14=2|S|.

Every triangle through both hubs meets uv. A triangle through
u and either private vertex meets ua or ub. Otherwise its rim
is a core edge and belongs to X. The same argument at v uses
vc,vd and the core edges. These cases exhaust all ambient
hub triangles because both full neighborhoods are specified.

The external packing edges are exactly R, so the safety
condition is satisfied. Arbitrary edges between A and B cannot
create another hub triangle; edges leaving the displayed local
vertex set cannot affect triangles through the hubs either.

The listed hypotheses are exactly the optional edges needed
by the displayed packing and cover. Mutual type-R centers
supply stronger adjacencies, but the sufficient lemma does
not require them. No minimum-packing or optimality claim is made.

4. EVERY SELECTED NEIGHBOR IN TYPE R REDUCES

Lemma R. Let d(u)=7 and suppose the complement of G[N(u)] has
edges

  ab,bc,de,

with f,g its other two vertices. The unique exceptional neighbor
is b. If v is any of a,c,d,e,f,g and d(v)<=8, then {u,v} is
reducible.

Proof. There are three kinds of selected neighbor.

First let v=f; the case v=g is symmetric. The vertex f is
adjacent to all six other vertices of N(u). The common five-set

  W={a,c,d,e,g}

induces K5 minus de. The vertex w=b is a neighbor of u outside
{v} union W and bd is present. Apply input (D), the repaired
K5-minus-edge lemma. Here w is also a common neighbor, which
that lemma allows.

Next let v=a; the case v=c is symmetric. Its only nonneighbor
inside N(u) is b, and the common core is

  W={c,d,e,f,g},

inducing K5 minus de. Again w=b and bd is present, so input (D)
applies.

Finally let v=d; the case v=e is symmetric. Its only nonneighbor
inside N(u) is e. Thus its common core with u is

  W={a,b,c,f,g},

inducing K5 minus ab,bc. Take w=e. This vertex is a neighbor
of u outside {v} union W and is adjacent to both a and c.
Lemma F applies, with f,g playing the roles of p,q.

All applications use d(u)+d(v)<=15. The six selected neighbors
have now all been covered.

5. MUTUAL EXCEPTIONS AND INDEPENDENCE

Lemma I. Suppose every degree-seven vertex has type M, P, Q,
or R. If none of the pairs supplied by the preceding reductions
occurs, then the set L of degree-seven vertices is independent.
Every selected neighbor of a vertex in L has degree at least nine.

Proof. Suppose u,v are adjacent degree-seven vertices. If one
has type M, input (A) supplies a reduction. Otherwise both have
type P, Q, or R. If at least one is selected for the other,
input (B) or Lemma R supplies a reduction.

It remains that each is exceptional for the other. Each has
exactly two nonneighbors at the opposite hub's position in its
link, so the common neighborhood has four vertices and each
side has two private vertices.

For types P and Q the common four-vertex core is K4, as proved
in the round-four case analysis. For type R it is K4 minus the
isolated missing edge de: the two missing P3 edges use the
exceptional hub and its private vertices, and disappear from
the common core. Therefore a mutual-exception pair with one
endpoint of type R and the other of type P or Q is impossible:
the same induced common core cannot be both K4 and K4 minus
one edge.

When neither endpoint has type R, input (C) applies. When both
have type R, the common core is K4 minus one edge. Each private
pair is adjacent and complete to the common core: in the
complement, the private vertices are the two P3 leaves, each
incident only to its missing edge to the opposite hub. Hence
all hypotheses of Lemma K hold after relabeling, so it supplies
a reduction.

These cases exhaust all possible L-L edges and prove independence.
Finally any selected neighbor of degree at most eight would give
one of (A), (B), or Lemma R. Thus selected neighbors have degree
at least nine.

6. THE SELECTED-INCIDENCE DENSITY BOUND

Let H be the vertices of degree at least nine, and let L_M,L_P,
L_Q,L_R be the four subsets of L. Suppose G has minimum degree
at least seven and none of the reductions in Lemma I occurs.
Then, with n=|V(G)|,

  2|E(G)|-8n >= [
      2|L_M|+|L_P|+|L_R|+|H|
      +4 sum over v in H of (d(v)-9)
    ]/5.                                                   (1)

Proof. At a type-M vertex select all seven neighbors; at types P
and R select the six nonexceptional neighbors; at type Q select
the five nonexceptional neighbors. By Lemma I they all belong
to H, and L is independent.

Every selected vertex has at most one missing edge in the
corresponding seven-vertex link, so it has degree at least five
inside that link. If a donor v in H has any selected recipient
w, choose one. At least five vertices of N(w) are adjacent to
v; none belongs to L because L is independent. Thus N(v)
contains at least five vertices outside L, and

  |N(v) intersect L|<=d(v)-5.

Give each vertex its degree as initial charge. A vertex of H
sends 1/5 to precisely the degree-seven vertices that selected
it. Receivers of types M,P,Q,R finish with respectively

  42/5, 41/5, 8, 41/5.

A donor with a selected recipient sends to at most d(v)-5
vertices and therefore retains at least

  d(v)-(d(v)-5)/5
      =8+[1+4(d(v)-9)]/5.

A donor with no selected recipient keeps d(v), which is at
least that same lower bound. Degree-eight vertices neither
send nor receive charge. Summing these estimates proves (1).
No sharpness claim is made.

7. CONDITIONAL FORCING THEOREM AND ITS LIMITS

Theorem. Let G have minimum degree at least seven and average
degree below eight. Suppose every degree-seven link has
complement of type M, P, Q, or R. Then G has a reducible pair
given by one of the proved constructions.

Proof. If none of those pairs occurs, inequality (1) gives
average degree at least eight, a contradiction.

The same estimate shows that, in this family and in the absence
of the listed reducible pairs, average degree at most eight
forces the graph to be eight-regular. Indeed if H is nonempty
the right side of (1) is positive. If L is nonempty, it has
selected neighbors and therefore H is nonempty. Thus both
sets must be empty, leaving only degree-eight vertices.

Consequently a subgraph-minimal counterexample to Tuza with
mad(G)<8, if it has minimum degree at least seven, must have
a degree-seven link outside these four complement families.
This is a conditional local forcing theorem, not a completed
proof of the mad<8 question.

The degree-five and degree-six cases remain open in this route.
The three-edge star and triangle complements, and more general
complements, have not been settled here. No assertion is made
that every exceptional mixed (7,8) pair in types P,Q,R is
reducible; those pairs are not needed by the selected-incidence
argument.

The link-family condition is not hereditary under arbitrary edge
deletions. It is used at a minimal counterexample after the
standard reducibility framework, rather than as an unsupported
induction hypothesis. Common-neighbor degrees are unrestricted;
the old pointwise degree budget from the 7-regular setting is
not assumed.

Lemma F was derived symbolically, and the bounded search also
found and literally verified a relabeling of that pattern. The
smaller Lemma K pattern was found by the bounded search and
independently checked, then promoted to the universal proof
given above. An alternate symbolic certificate is preserved
separately. No solver status, failed search, or catalogue
completeness claim is a premise of these proofs.
