NEW EXACT COVER PROFILE.
Round two proved that a failed gluing across three shared edges F forces
one side H to satisfy tau(H)=2nu(H), with every minimum cover avoiding F.
Put k=nu(H), H(J)=H-(F\J), for J subset F. Its packing profile is
 nu(H(empty))=k-1 and nu(H(J))=k for every nonempty J.
All H(J) satisfy Tuza by the minimal-counterexample setup.

I claim that its cover profile is forced exactly:
 tau(H(J))=2k-2+min(|J|,2) for every J subset F.
Proof: for nonempty A subset F, any cover of H-A together with A covers
H and meets F. Since no minimum cover of H meets F, this gives
tau(H-A)+|A|>=2k+1. In particular tau(H-F)>=2k-2; Tuza and nu(H-F)=k-1
give equality. Adding one edge increases tau by at most one. Thus for
|J|=1 the upper bound is 2k-1, matching the preceding lower bound with
|F\J|=2. For |J|=2 the lower bound with one edge deleted is 2k and
monotonicity gives the same upper bound. For J=F the value is 2k.

Consequences: H-F is also Tuza-tight. Each graph retaining exactly two
boundary edges is Tuza-tight too. Restoring one edge raises both packing
and cover by one; restoring a second raises only the cover; restoring
the third raises neither. This is much more rigid than a lone tight side.

EQUALITY EXTENDABILITY LEMMA.
If tau(H)=2nu(H) and Tuza holds for every subgraph H-E(T), where T is a
triangle, then EVERY triangle T belongs to some maximum packing.
Indeed let nu(H)=k. Combining T with a packing of H-E(T) gives its packing
number at most k-1. If it were at most k-2, then covering H-E(T) and
adding the three edges of T would give tau(H)<=2(k-2)+3=2k-1, a
contradiction. Hence nu(H-E(T))=k-1 and T extends to a maximum packing.
