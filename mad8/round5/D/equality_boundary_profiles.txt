EQUALITY, EDGE DELETION, AND THE THREE-EDGE SEPARATOR OBSTRUCTION

Status: reviewed research proof; see C/review_D_full.txt.
This is internal research review, not journal peer review or formal verification.
No novelty claim is made. No numerical result is a premise below.

PURPOSE AND NOTATION.
The reviewed round-two separator theorem in
mad8/round2/D/three_edge_boundary.txt leaves one necessary obstruction
when the shared edge set F has size three and is triangle-free. One
side H must be Tuza-tight, tau(H)=2nu(H), while every minimum cover of
H avoids F. Its packing number drops by one on deleting all three
edges, but not on retaining any nonempty subset. This file derives an
exact cover profile, simplifies the hypotheses needed to recognize it,
and proves packing-extension properties of equality graphs.

For any graph H, nu(H) is its maximum edge-disjoint triangle packing size
and tau(H) its minimum triangle edge-cover size. H-D means edge deletion,
retaining all vertices. All applications of Tuza to an edge-deleted
graph are explicitly hypotheses; the full conjecture is not assumed.

1. RECOGNIZING AN EDGE IN NO MINIMUM COVER.

LEMMA 1.
For any e in E(H), the following are equivalent:
 (a) no minimum triangle cover of H contains e;
 (b) tau(H-e)=tau(H).

Proof. Monotonicity and adding e to a cover give
tau(H)-1<=tau(H-e)<=tau(H). If a minimum cover of H contains e,
removing e from that cover covers H-e and yields tau(H-e)<=tau(H)-1.
Conversely, if tau(H-e)=tau(H)-1, a minimum cover of H-e together
with e is a minimum cover of H containing e. These implications prove
the equivalence. An edge in no triangle trivially has this property;
such edges are excluded in the proposed numerical search.

2. THE DELETION BOUND AT EQUALITY.

THEOREM 2.
Suppose tau(H)=2nu(H)=2k and D is any subset of E(H) such that
tau(H-D)<=2nu(H-D). Then
  nu(H-D)>=k-floor(|D|/2).
If D contains even one edge that belongs to no minimum cover of H,
the stronger bound holds:
  nu(H-D)>=k-floor((|D|-1)/2).
The stronger assertion is used only for nonempty D.

Proof. Let Y be a minimum cover of H-D. The disjoint union D union Y
covers H: triangles meeting D are handled by D, and the others remain
in H-D. Therefore
  2k<=|D|+tau(H-D)<=|D|+2nu(H-D).
Integer rounding proves the first bound.

If D contains an edge absent from every minimum cover of H, the cover
D union Y cannot be a minimum cover of H, since it contains that edge.
Its cardinality is an integer, so
  2k+1<=|D|+tau(H-D)<=|D|+2nu(H-D).
Rearranging and rounding gives the stronger bound.

COROLLARY 3 (edge avoidance and packing extension).
Under tau(H)=2nu(H)=2k, with Tuza assumed for the particular deleted
graphs that occur in each assertion:

 (i) Every edge can be avoided by some maximum triangle packing of H.
     Here Tuza is needed for H-e.

 (ii) Any two distinct edges, at least one of which belongs to no minimum
      cover of H, can be avoided simultaneously by a maximum packing.
      Here Tuza is needed for deletion of those two edges.

 (iii) Every triangle T belongs to some maximum packing.
       Here Tuza is needed for H-E(T).

 (iv) Every packing of two triangles that uses an edge belonging to no
      minimum cover of H extends to a maximum packing of H.
      Here Tuza is needed for deletion of the six edges of those triangles.

Proof. In (i), the first bound of Theorem 2 with |D|=1 gives
nu(H-e)>=k, hence equality by monotonicity. In (ii), the stronger bound
with |D|=2 similarly gives nu(H-D)=k.

For (iii), delete the three edges of T. The first bound gives
nu(H-E(T))>=k-1. The reverse inequality follows because T can be
combined with any packing in H-E(T). Equality follows, and such a
maximum packing together with T has size k.

For (iv), let P be the two-triangle packing and D=E(P), so |D|=6.
The stronger bound gives nu(H-D)>=k-2. Conversely any packing of H-D
can be combined with P, giving nu(H-D)<=k-2. Combining a maximum
packing of H-D with P gives the required extension.

3. THE EXACT THREE-EDGE PROFILE.

THEOREM 4.
Let F be a three-edge subset of E(H), and for J subset F put
  H(J)=H-(F\J).
Assume:
 (a) tau(H)=2nu(H)=2k;
 (b) every edge of F belongs to no minimum cover of H;
 (c) nu(H(empty))=k-1;
 (d) Tuza holds for every H(J), including H(empty).
Then for all J subset F,
  tau(H(J))=2k-2+min(|J|,2),
and
  nu(H(J))=k-1 if J is empty, and k otherwise.

In particular the packing gains for nonempty J need not be separately
assumed: the other hypotheses force them. These are the complete
packing and cover profiles of the rich side required by the reviewed
round-two separator obstruction.

Proof. For every nonempty A subset F, any minimum cover of H-A combined
with A covers H and contains an edge of F. It cannot be a minimum
cover of H, by (b). Hence
  tau(H-A)+|A|>=2k+1.                 (*)

With A=F, this gives tau(H(empty))>=2k-2. Assumptions (c) and (d)
give the reverse inequality, so tau(H(empty))=2k-2.

If |J|=1, then |F\J|=2, and (*) gives tau(H(J))>=2k-1.
Adding the single edge of J to a minimum cover of H(empty) gives a
cover of H(J) of size 2k-1. Thus equality holds. If |J|=2, apply (*)
with the single deleted edge to obtain tau(H(J))>=2k; monotonicity
gives tau(H(J))<=tau(H)=2k. If J=F the value is already assumed.
This proves the stated cover profile.

For nonempty J, the cover number is at least 2k-1. Assumption (d)
and integrality therefore give nu(H(J))>=k, while H(J) being a
subgraph of H gives the opposite inequality. This proves the packing
profile and completes the theorem.

The eight values can be summarized by subset size:
  |J|       0         1         2         3
  nu       k-1        k         k         k
  tau      2k-2      2k-1       2k        2k.

Thus H-F and each graph retaining exactly two boundary edges are
Tuza-tight. Restoring one edge raises packing and cover by one; restoring
the second raises only the cover; restoring the third changes neither.

4. THE OBSTRUCTION CANNOT HAVE PACKING NUMBER ONE.

LEMMA 5.
If nu(H)=1 and tau(H)=2, then all triangles of H lie in one K4,
and every edge that belongs to a triangle belongs to some minimum
triangle cover of H. Consequently Theorem 4's hypotheses cannot hold
with k=1.

Proof. Any two triangles of H share an edge because nu(H)=1. Their
common intersection cannot contain an edge shared by every triangle,
since that edge alone would be a cover and tau(H) would be one.
Choose distinct triangles abc and abd. Since not all triangles contain
ab, some triangle T avoids ab and still meets both chosen triangles
in edges. The only possibilities are acd and bcd: choosing disjoint
edges from the two original triangles cannot produce a triangle, while
choosing incident edges forces one of those two triples. Either choice
makes all six edges on {a,b,c,d} present, so H contains a K4.

Every triangle of H must meet each of the four triangles of this K4
in an edge. One K4 edge lies in only two of its four triangles, so such
a triangle must contain at least two distinct K4 edges. Those two edges
meet and force all three of its vertices into the K4. Thus there are
no triangles outside this K4.

Given any edge of that K4, its union with the opposite edge is a
two-edge triangle cover of the K4 and therefore of H. Its size is
tau(H), so every triangle edge belongs to a minimum cover. Finally,
Theorem 4 forces each edge e in F to create a triangle-packing gain
when it alone is restored to H-F. Such an edge must belong to a triangle.
But (b) excludes every edge of F from every minimum cover, impossible
when k=1. The claimed obstruction therefore requires k>=2.

5. WHAT IS OPEN, AND WHAT A FINITE SEARCH CAN SAY.

The symbolic results do not establish whether any graph with the full
three-edge profile exists. For the unresolved sparse four-vertex cuts,
F would induce a star K_{1,3} or a path P4 on four vertices. A graph
realizing the rich profile is only one necessary side of the obstruction;
it is not itself a Tuza counterexample, nor is it sufficient to defeat
gluing without the second side and its different packing profile.

The staged numerical protocol asks first for a Tuza-tight graph with
an edge that lies in a triangle but in no minimum cover. Lemma 1 provides
the exact test tau(H-e)=tau(H). If such graphs occur, inspect induced
four-vertex stars and paths whose three edges all pass that test, and
test nu(H-F)=nu(H)-1. Under the boundary-subgraph Tuza hypothesis,
Theorem 4 then forces the full profile; explicit checks of all eight
states may serve as independent verification.
