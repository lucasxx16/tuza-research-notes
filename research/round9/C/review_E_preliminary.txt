ROUND 9 C — E JOINT LOW-NEIGHBOR CANDIDATES, PRELIMINARY AUDIT

Literature baseline: all claims below require G robust and irreducible. Let L=V5∪V6, v a vertex adjacent to low vertices, R=N(v)\L, and W_x=N(v)∩N(x) for each low neighbor x. Low vertices are independent by Puleo 2.7(b), hence W_x⊆R. Puleo 2.7(a) gives |W_x|≥d(x)−2; if v subsumes x, equality improves to d(x)−1. For distinct low neighbors x,y, v belongs to N(x)∩N(y) but not N(v), so |W_x∩W_y|≤codeg(x,y)−1. BFG 3.3 gives ≤2 for 5/5 or 5/6; if one 5-link is nonclique, 5/5 improves to ≤1. After D's reviewed round-nine exact-codegree-four reduction, every low pair has codegree≤3, giving ≤2 for 6/6 as well. Thus any two low neighbors have |W_x∪W_y|≥5; if one is subsumed, ≥6. The latter follows in 5/5 using the nonclique equality restriction and in mixed/6/6 using the direct overlap caps. Consequently every degree 7,8,9 vertex has at most d(v)−5 low neighbors, and a degree-eight/nine subsumer with another low neighbor has at most d(v)−6. In particular a degree-eight vertex subsumes at most one degree-six neighbor. For degree seven, Puleo forbids subsumption of 5/6; two degree-six neighbors would have two four-element W sets with overlap≤2 and union≥6, but R has size five. Hence degree seven has at most one degree-six neighbor.

For v of degree eight with exactly three low neighbors, |R|=5. Three 5-neighbors would give three 3-subsets of a 5-set pairwise meeting at most once, impossible. Two 6-neighbors would give two W sets of size at least four with union at least six, impossible. Thus two 5-neighbors x,y and one 6-neighbor t remain. Neither 5 is subsumed (Puleo 2.7(e)); the 6 cannot be subsumed since its size-five W and a 5-neighbor's size-three W with mixed overlap≤2 would union to six. Therefore Wx,Wy have size three, Wt size four. Nonclique 5-links force |Wx∩Wy|≤1, and R size five forces exactly one; label Wx={s,a,b}, Wy={s,c,d}. Mixed overlap≤2 forces Wt={a,b,c,d}.

If t is thin, its link missing matching restricts G[Wt] to C4 with missing cross pairs ac,bd (ab,cd are present from the 5-links); G[R]=K1(s)∨C4. The eight-vertex link G[N(v)] then has WKE witness Q={s,b,d,t}, M={xa,yc,sb,dt}: the only edges outside Q are xa,yc, and the matching has four edges. Thus irreducibility rules out thin t. For nonthin t, its unique link missing edge is v-ht, so Wt is K4 and G[R]=K5. Writing N(x)={v,s,a,b,hx}, N(y)={v,s,c,d,hy}, N(t)={v,a,b,c,d,ht}, all three h-vertices lie outside N(v). Equality hx=hy would give 5/5 codegree three with nonclique links, contradicting BFG equality; hx=ht or hy=ht would give 5/6 codegree four, also forbidden. Each z∈R has v, four R-neighbors, two low neighbors, and two distinct external h-neighbors, so d(z)≥9. This is a conditional structural profile, not a target-eight discharging theorem.
