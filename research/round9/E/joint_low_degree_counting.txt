JOINT LOW-DEGREE COUNTS AND THE DEGREE-EIGHT THREE-LOW PROFILE

Status: reviewed research proof; see research/round9/C/review_E_full.txt.
The graph is robust and irreducible throughout. Vertices of degrees
five and six are retained, and no classification of seven-links is
assumed. No enumeration or numerical calculation is a premise.

1. INPUTS, ATTRIBUTION, AND THE TARGET-EIGHT ACCOUNT

Robust means that every component of every induced neighborhood has
at least five vertices. Irreducible means that there is no nonempty
vertex set admitting an ambient triangle-packing/cover certificate
with cover size at most twice packing size and every packed edge
outside the deleted set included in the cover.

All conclusions here apply to a subgraph-minimal counterexample
within mad(G)<8. The class is subgraph-closed, so every proper
subgraph satisfies Tuza by minimality; the reduction bridge forbids
reducible sets, and Puleo Lemma 2.6 supplies robustness. Consequently
delta(G)>=5. This is not an assumption that delta(G)>=7.

The fixed literature baseline is research/known_results.txt.
We import all of Puleo Lemma 2.7(a)-(h), with its robust-irreducible
hypotheses: low links have matching complements of size other than
two; vertices of degree at most six are independent; seven-vertices
neither subsume six-vertices nor neighbor thin six-vertices; vertices
of degree at most eight do not subsume five-vertices; a nine-vertex
subsumes at most three low vertices, and if three then these are
all its low neighbors; a 10+ subsumer has at most d-6 low neighbors;
vertices of degrees 7,8,9 have at most d-4 low neighbors.

Here a vertex v subsumes x if N[x] is contained in N[v]. A six-
vertex is thin when its link complement is a perfect matching.
The possible low links are K5, K5-e, K6, K6-e, and thin K6-3K2.

We also import Botler--Fernandes--Gutierrez, arXiv:2002.07925v2,
Lemma 3.3: in an irreducible robust graph, d(x),d(y)<=6 and
|N(x) union N(y)|<=7 force both degrees to be five, codegree three,
and both links K5. Its already-known arithmetic consequences are
5/5 codegree <=3 (equality only for two K5 links), 5/6 codegree <=3,
and 6/6 codegree <=4. They are inputs, not results claimed here.

The additional explicit reduction used in this note is the complete
6/6 exact-codegree-four exclusion in
research/round9/D/six_six_codegree_four.txt, reviewed in
research/round9/C/review_D_full.txt. Combined with the BFG bound,
it gives the following working overlap bounds for distinct low
vertices x,y:

  |N(x) intersect N(y)|<=3 in all degree-5/6 cases;            (1)
  |N(x) intersect N(y)|<=2 for a 5/5 pair with a K5-e link.    (2)

Only the 6/6 strengthening in (1) uses the additional round-nine
construction. Bound (2) is the BFG equality restriction. No claim
of literature novelty is made for the deductions in this note.

Put L=V5 union V6 and H=V_{>=9}. At target eight, the exact initial
deficit and surplus totals are

  deficit = 3|V5|+2|V6|+|V7|,
  surplus = sum_{v in H}(d(v)-8).                           (3)

Every degree-eight vertex has zero initial surplus. This note
establishes structural and incidence constraints; it does not
authorize positive net outflow from such a vertex or claim that
the surplus in (3) meets the deficit.

2. A COMMON-NEIGHBOR INCIDENCE SYSTEM AT ANY NONLOW VERTEX

Fix v outside L and write

  L_v=N(v) intersect L, ell_v=|L_v|,
  R_v=N(v)\L, r_v=|R_v|=d(v)-ell_v,
  W_x=N(v) intersect N(x), for x in L_v.

The independence of L ensures W_x is contained in R_v. The low
link complement is a matching, so within N(x) the vertex v
misses at most one of the other d(x)-1 vertices. Thus

  |W_x|>=d(x)-2,
  |W_x|=d(x)-1 if v subsumes x.                            (4)

For distinct x,y in L_v, the vertex v itself is a common neighbor
of x,y but is not in W_x intersect W_y. Therefore (1)-(2) give

  |W_x intersect W_y|<=2;                                 (5)
  |W_x intersect W_y|<=1 for a 5/5 pair with a K5-e link.    (6)

These are simultaneous set constraints on one ground set R_v,
not independent upper bounds whose extremal cases may be freely
combined.

Lemma 1 (joint neighbor caps). The following hold:

  (a) ell_v<=d(v)-5 for every v outside L;
  (b) if v subsumes a low vertex, ell_v<=d(v)-6;
  (c) a seven-vertex has at most one degree-six neighbor;
  (d) an eight-vertex subsuming a six-vertex has at most two
      low neighbors, and any other low neighbor has degree five;
  (e) every degree-nine vertex has at most three low neighbors.

Proof. If there are two low neighbors, their W-sets have union
of size at least five. For a 5/6 pair, (4)-(5) give 3+4-2=5;
for a 6/6 pair they give 4+4-2=6. For a 5/5 pair with both links
K5, both W-sets have size four, giving union at least six. If
at least one link is K5-e, (6) gives at least 3+3-1=5. This
proves (a) when ell_v>=2; when ell_v<=1 it follows from d(v)>=7.

If one of the two neighbors is subsumed, its W-set has size four
or five. A subsumed five against a six gives 4+4-2=6; against
a five with at least one nonclique link gives 4+3-1=6; and
against a clique five gives at least 4+4-2=6. A subsumed six
against a five gives 5+3-2=6, and against a six gives 5+4-2=7.
Thus r_v>=6, proving (b) when ell_v>=2. With ell_v<=1 it is
automatic, since Puleo's restrictions put every such subsumer
at degree at least eight.

For (c), part (a) gives ell_v<=2. If two neighbors had degree
six, their W-sets would have union at least six, whereas
r_v=7-2=5. This is impossible. For (d), part (b) gives
ell_v<=2. If a second low neighbor had degree six, its W-set
together with the subsumed six's W-set would require at least
seven vertices, but r_v=8-2=6. This also is impossible.

For (e), part (a) already bounds ell_v by four. If ell_v=4,
then r_v=5 and part (b) says v subsumes none of its low
neighbors. There cannot be two six-neighbors, whose W-sets
would require at least six vertices. Thus at least three
neighbors have degree five. Since they are not subsumed,
their links are K5-e and their W-sets are three-subsets of
the five-set R_v. Any two intersect in at most one, hence
exactly one, and their union is R_v. Their two-element
complements in R_v are therefore pairwise disjoint, impossible
for three two-sets in a five-set. This proves (e). QED.

In particular, the degree-7/8/9 low-neighbor caps are now 2,3,3,
and a degree-eight vertex can subsume at most one low vertex.
That vertex, if present, has degree six. These are deductions
from the stated inputs, not reattributions of the original
Puleo or BFG statements.

Here is one global count that retains the joint subsumption
information. Let U be the set of nonlow vertices subsuming at
least one low vertex. Lemma 1(b),(e) provide one extra unit
of improvement over part (a) on U union V9. Since L is
independent, summing over the other endpoint of each
low-to-nonlow edge gives

  5|V5|+6|V6|
     <= sum_{v outside L}(d(v)-5) - |U union V9|.         (7)

No degree-eight surplus was used in deriving (7).

3. THE ONLY DEGREE-EIGHT PROFILE WITH THREE LOW NEIGHBORS

Theorem 2. Let d(v)=8 and ell_v=3. Then the following labeled
configuration is forced, up to relabeling:

  L_v={x,y,t}, with d(x)=d(y)=5 and d(t)=6;
  R_v={s,a,b,c,d} induces K5;
  W_x={s,a,b}, W_y={s,c,d}, W_t={a,b,c,d};                 (8)

the links of x,y,t are respectively K5-e, K5-e, K6-e. There
are three distinct vertices h_x,h_y,h_t outside N[v] such that

  N(x)={v,s,a,b,h_x}, missing-link edge v h_x;
  N(y)={v,s,c,d,h_y}, missing-link edge v h_y;
  N(t)={v,a,b,c,d,h_t}, missing-link edge v h_t.            (9)

Every vertex of R_v has degree at least nine. No claim fixes
the degrees or additional adjacencies of the three h-vertices.

Proof. Here |R_v|=5. There cannot be two six-neighbors: their
W-sets have size at least four and intersection at most two,
so their union would have size at least six.

There cannot be three five-neighbors either. Because d(v)=8,
Puleo's subsumption restriction makes each such neighbor have
a K5-e link with v at an endpoint of its missing edge. Each
W-set has size exactly three. Their pairwise intersections
are at most one by (6), so any two have intersection exactly
one and union all of R_v. Equivalently, their two-element
complements in R_v are pairwise disjoint. Three disjoint
two-sets cannot fit in a five-set. Thus there are exactly
two five-neighbors x,y and one six-neighbor t.

The W-sets of x,y have size three. The vertex v cannot subsume
t, by Lemma 1(b) and ell_v=3, so |W_t|=4. Its intersection
with either three-set is at most two. Let s be the unique
vertex of R_v outside W_t. Both three-sets must contain s;
their intersection is at most one by (6), hence it is exactly
{s}. Their remaining two-sets are disjoint and partition W_t.
Label them {a,b},{c,d}, obtaining (8).

Since v is the nonsubsuming endpoint in each of the K5-e
links, both W_x and W_y induce triangles. In particular s
is adjacent to a,b,c,d, and ab,cd are present.

Suppose t were thin. Its link has six vertices, with v missing
one of them; call that vertex h_t. The other four form W_t.
The matching complement on these four has two edges, so W_t
induces C4. As ab and cd are present, exchange c,d if needed
so its two nonedges are ac,bd. Consequently R_v induces
the join of s and this C4.

In the eight-vertex link F=G[N(v)], take

  Q={s,b,d,t}, M={xa,yc,sb,dt}.

The four matching edges exist and are pairwise disjoint. The
vertices outside Q are x,y,a,c; their only edges are xa,yc:
the low vertices x,y are independent, their W-sets are given
by (8), and ac is absent. Thus |Q|=|M|=4 and Q covers F-M,
so F is WKE.

For completeness, a WKE link gives an ambient singleton
reduction directly. For any matching M and Q as above, use
the triangles {vij:ij in M} and the edge set
M union {vq:q in Q}. These triangles are edge-disjoint,
the cover size is at most 2|M|, every triangle through v
meets a deleted rim or spoke, and every packed edge avoiding
v belongs to M. Hence the WKE conclusion contradicts
irreducibility; it is also Puleo's WKE singleton criterion.

Therefore t is non-thin. Its link is K6 or K6-e, but v does
not subsume t, so it is K6-e with missing edge v h_t.
It follows that W_t induces K4. Together with s adjacent
to every vertex of W_t, this makes R_v a K5.

The exact degrees and W-set sizes give the neighborhoods in
(9). For x and y their missing-edge mates are likewise the
unique other neighbors not in N(v). All h-vertices lie outside
N[v]: each is distinct from v and is nonadjacent to v.
They also lie outside L by low-vertex independence.

The mates are pairwise distinct. If h_x=h_y, the five-vertices
x,y have common neighbors v,s,h_x, contradicting the BFG
bound two when a five-link is K5-e. If h_x=h_t, then x,t
have common neighbors v,a,b,h_x, contradicting the BFG 5/6
bound three. The same argument applies to h_y=h_t.

Finally, s is adjacent to v, the other four vertices of R_v,
x,y, and h_x,h_y. These are nine distinct vertices. Each of
a,b is similarly adjacent to v, four other R_v-vertices,
x,t, and h_x,h_t. Each of c,d uses y,t,h_y,h_t instead.
These edges are guaranteed by the sole-missing-edge description
in (9). Thus every vertex of R_v has degree at least nine,
as claimed. QED.

4. A JOINT COUNT FOR SHARED HIGH-DEGREE NEIGHBORS

Let P denote the set of degree-eight vertices with exactly
three low neighbors. For h in H=V_{>=9}, define

  p_h=|N(h) intersect P|;
  ell_h=|N(h) intersect L|;
  f_h=number of degree-five neighbors of h with link K5-e;
  e_h=number of degree-six neighbors of h with link K6-e.

Theorem 3. The following incidence identity and bounds hold:

  5|P| = sum_{h in H} p_h,                                (10)

  p_h <= binom(f_h,2)+f_h e_h,                            (11)

  p_h <= max(0,d(h)-ell_h-4).                             (12)

If p_h>0, then ell_h>=2 and ell_h<=d(h)-7. In particular,
a degree-nine vertex adjacent to P has exactly two low
neighbors and at most one neighbor in P. Consequently

  5|P| <= sum_{h in H}
      min{binom(f_h,2)+f_h e_h,
          max(0,d(h)-ell_h-4)}.                           (13)

Proof. By Theorem 2, each vertex of P has exactly five nonlow
neighbors, all in H. This proves (10).

Fix an incidence v in P intersect N(h) and use Theorem 2's
labels around v. If h=s, assign the low pair {x,y} to this
incidence. If h is a or b, assign {x,t}; if h is c or d,
assign {y,t}. In each case these are two low neighbors of h
and are subsumed by h, because h belongs to the dominating
part of each low link K5-e or K6-e. The assigned pair has
type (5 with K5-e,5 with K5-e) or (5 with K5-e,6 with K6-e).

Distinct P-incidences at h receive distinct pairs. Indeed,
the exact neighborhoods (9) and the distinct mates show

  N(x) intersect N(y)={v,s},
  N(x) intersect N(t)={v,a,b},
  N(y) intersect N(t)={v,c,d}.                            (14)

Equivalently, these profiles saturate the BFG bounds two for
the nonclique 5/5 pair and three for the 5/6 pairs. Each set
in (14) contains exactly one degree-eight vertex, namely v;
the other listed vertices all lie in H. Any other vertex of
P using the same low pair would have to be another common
neighbor of that pair, which (14) excludes. The number of
available unordered pairs at h is at most the right side of
(11), proving that bound.

If p_h>0, choose one incident v in P. The other four vertices
of its K5 R_v are four additional H-neighbors of h. They are
distinct from all low neighbors of h and all neighbors of h
in P. Consequently p_h+ell_h+4<=d(h). If p_h=0, no such four
vertices are asserted; using max(0,...) yields (12) in both
cases.

The incidence also supplies two subsumed low neighbors. For
the assigned 5/5 pair their W-sets at h have size four each
and intersection at most one by (6). For the assigned 5/6
pair their W-sets have sizes four and five and intersection
at most two by (5). In both cases their union contains at
least seven vertices of N(h)\L. Hence ell_h<=d(h)-7.
The assigned pair gives ell_h>=2. For d(h)=9 this forces
ell_h=2, leaving only one possible assigned low pair, so
p_h<=1 by the already proved pair injection. Summing (11)
and (12) through (10) proves (13). QED.

5. WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH

Theorems 2 and 3 give a complete necessary profile and a shared-
neighbor count for the extremal degree-eight case ell_v=3 in
an arbitrary robust irreducible graph. The result uses the
full established BFG overlap baseline and the additional
reviewed 6/6 reduction, rather than retesting the earlier
round-six graph J. That graph violates the BFG baseline and
is not a barrier to the hypotheses used here.

The conclusions are structural constraints available in every
minimal mad<8 counterexample, without delta>=7 or a seven-link
family hypothesis. They do not yet prove that (3)'s surplus
dominates its deficit. In particular, the following remain
uncontrolled by this note: arbitrary degree-seven link types
and their unit deficits; degree-eight vertices with one or
two low neighbors; and simultaneous funding of all five/six
deficits by higher-degree vertices. Equations (7) and (13)
are necessary incidence bounds, not a completed discharging
rule or an existence claim for their equality cases.

No net charge is assigned to leave a degree-eight vertex.
No sparse counterexample to the full updated baseline is
claimed, and no Tuza counterexample is constructed.
