ROUND 9 / D
COMPLETE REDUCTION OF THE SIX/SIX EXACT-CODEGREE-FOUR PATCH

Status: reviewed research proof; see C/review_D_full.txt.
No claim of literature novelty is made. BFG's published codegree
bounds are explicitly separated from the construction proved here.
No numerical search or numerical candidate was used.

1. LOCAL THEOREM AND REDUCTION DEFINITION.

All graphs are finite and simple. Let nu(G) and tau(G) denote the
maximum size of an edge-disjoint triangle packing and minimum size
of a triangle edge cover. A nonempty vertex set Z is reducible if
there are a packing S and an edge set X such that
  |X|<=2|S|;
  X meets every triangle containing a vertex of Z; and
  X contains every S-edge with both endpoints outside Z.

THEOREM.
Let u,v be nonadjacent vertices of degree six in an arbitrary graph
G, with exactly four common neighbors. Suppose the complement of
each of G[N(u)] and G[N(v)] is a matching of size other than two.
Then {u,v} is reducible.

Only these two neighborhood hypotheses are imposed. Other degrees
and all other ambient edges are unrestricted. The matching sizes
allowed on a six-vertex link are zero, one, and three.

Write
  C=N(u) intersect N(v),
  A=N(u)\C={a1,a2}, B=N(v)\C={b1,b2},
  P={ua1,ua2,vb1,vb2}.
The four private vertices are distinct and outside C union {u,v}.
This follows from exact codegree four and nonadjacency; it is used
in verifying the packing edges. In particular the theorem does not
silently allow private vertices from opposite sides to coincide.

The complement of G[C] is a matching on four vertices, since it is
a subgraph of either link complement. Thus G[C] is exactly one of
  K4, K4 minus one edge (diamond), or C4.
These three cases are exhausted below.

COMMON AMBIENT COVER CHECK.
Each displayed cover contains P and all of E(G[C]). Every triangle
through u either uses one of its two private neighbors and meets P,
or has its other two vertices in C and meets E(G[C]). The same
argument holds for v. There is no triangle using both u and v,
since uv is absent. Therefore these covers meet every ambient
triangle through {u,v}, regardless of private cross edges, edges
from private/core vertices to other vertices, or other ambient
triangles. It remains in each case to verify the packing, size, and
external-packed-edge condition.

2. FOUR-CYCLE CORE: FOUR TRIANGLES, EIGHT COVER EDGES.

Label the core so that its edges are 12,23,34,41, writing i for
the vertex xi. The two missing edges are 13 and 24.

For clarity, the link restrictions force both links to be thin in
this case. Each link complement already has two disjoint core
edges. It cannot have exactly two edges, so its third matching edge
must join its two private vertices. Thus a1a2 and b1b2 are absent,
and all private-to-core edges are present. The certificate does not
need these additional conclusions.

Take
  S4={u12,u34,v23,v41},
  X4=E(G[C]) union P.
The four distinct core edges are used once each. Each center uses
its four core spokes once, so the four triangles are edge-disjoint.
The only packed edges outside {u,v} are the core edges, all in X4.
The common ambient check applies, and
  |X4|=4+4=8=2|S4|.
This proves reducibility in the four-cycle case.

3. DIAMOND CORE: FIVE TRIANGLES, TEN COVER EDGES.

Label the core so that the missing edge is 12; its edges are
  13,14,23,24,34.
Both private pairs a1a2 and b1b2 must be edges. To see this for the
v-side, suppose b1b2 were absent. The link complement would contain
the two disjoint edges 12 and b1b2. It is a matching and cannot have
exactly two edges, so its third edge would have to be 34. But 34 is
present in the core, a contradiction. The same proof applies at u.
This reasoning covers thin and non-thin links equally.

Take
  S5={u13,u24,v14,v23,vb1b2},
  X5=E(G[C]) union {b1b2} union P.
The first four triangles use the four distinct edges 13,24,14,23
of the core four-cycle. Each center uses its core spokes once.
The last triangle uses vb1,vb2,b1b2, none previously used. Thus the
five triangles are edge-disjoint. Their only edges outside {u,v}
are the four used core edges and b1b2, all in X5. The common ambient
check applies, including triangles that use the other private pair.
Finally,
  |X5|=5+1+4=10=2|S5|.
This proves reducibility in the diamond case without any restriction
on the four private cross edges A--B or on how a thin link's other
two missing edges join its private vertices to the core.

4. COMPLETE CORE: SEVEN TRIANGLES, FOURTEEN COVER EDGES.

Label the core arbitrarily as C={1,2,3,4}, inducing K4. Neither link
can be thin: K6 minus a perfect matching has clique number three,
since a clique contains at most one endpoint of each missing pair.
Thus the complement of each six-link has size zero or one.

We may label the two u-private vertices so that
  a1 3 and a2 4 are edges,
and label the two v-private vertices so that
  b1 2 and b2 4 are edges.
Indeed, for either side and its indicated pair of core targets,
the four possible private-to-target edges have at most one missing
edge. A K2,2 with at most one edge removed has a perfect matching.
Choose that matching to label the two private vertices. The choices
on the two sides are independent, since their private sets are
disjoint. This justifies the selected attachments without assuming
that all private vertices are complete to C.

Take
  S7={u12,u a1 3,u a2 4,
      v13,v b1 2,v b2 4,234},
  F={a1 3,a2 4,b1 2,b2 4},
  X7=E(G[C]) union F union P.

The three u-triangles use all six u-spokes once, and the three
v-triangles use all six v-spokes once. Their two core edges are 12
and 13. The internal triangle 234 uses 23,24,34, disjoint from
those two. The four private-to-core edges in F are distinct because
the private vertices are distinct; they also avoid all core edges
and hub spokes. Hence all seven triangles are edge-disjoint.

Every packed edge outside {u,v} lies in E(G[C]) union F, which is
contained in X7. The common ambient check applies, and the three
parts of X7 are pairwise disjoint. Therefore
  |X7|=6+4+4=14=2|S7|.
This proves reducibility in the complete-core case. The status of
a1a2, b1b2, and every A--B edge is irrelevant to this certificate.

Sections 2-4 exhaust the core possibilities, proving the theorem.

5. INDUCTION BRIDGE AND STRUCTURAL CONSEQUENCE.

For any of the three certificates, put G'=(G-X)-{u,v}. If G' obeys
Tuza, let T and Y be a maximum packing and a minimum cover of G'.
Every packed S-edge either touches {u,v} or belongs to X, so T and
S are edge-disjoint. Every triangle of G either meets X or survives
in G' (the ambient check already covers triangles through u,v).
Consequently
  tau(G)<=|X|+|Y|<=2|S|+2|T|<=2nu(G).
This is a local ambient-safe reduction, not just a verification of
Tuza on one induced ten-vertex patch.

COROLLARY.
In a robust graph with no reducible set, any two distinct vertices
u,v of degree six have at most three common neighbors.

Proof. Puleo's Lemma 2.7(a),(b) gives nonadjacency and the two link
conditions used in the theorem. The published BFG Lemma 3.3 implies
that their codegree is at most four: if it were at least five, then
  |N(u) union N(v)|=12-|N(u) intersect N(v)|<=7,
and that lemma would force both degrees to be five, a contradiction.
The present theorem excludes codegree exactly four. This proves
the bound three.

The BFG upper bound four is imported, not rediscovered or counted
as a new result here. The work in this file is the complete exact-
codegree-four reduction. No claim is made that this strengthening
is absent from the broader literature.

The corollary applies to any subgraph-minimal counterexample with
mad<8. All proper subgraphs of such a graph satisfy Tuza because
mad<8 is subgraph-closed, so the induction bridge forbids reducible
sets. Puleo's Lemma 2.6 gives robustness. There is no restriction
on degree-seven link types, and no pointwise degree cap is assumed
for common or private neighbors.
