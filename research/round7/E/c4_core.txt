COMPLETE HUMAN PROOF FOR THE ORIGINAL FOUR-CYCLE CORE

Status: reviewed census-free research proof; see
research/round7/C/review_E_full.txt.
No saved-record count, catalogue completeness claim, enumeration, or
numerical certificate is a premise.

1. SETUP AND THEOREM

Let u,v be adjacent degree-seven vertices in a finite simple graph.
Their common neighborhood is C={x,y,p,q}, inducing the four-cycle
H with bipartition {x,y},{p,q}. Thus

  E(H)={xp,xq,yp,yq}; xy and pq are absent.

Let A={a,b} and B={c,d} be their private pairs, so

  N(u)={v} union C union A,
  N(v)={u} union C union B.

Edges between A and B and edges leaving this neighborhood are
unrestricted. A graph F is WKE if there are a matching M and a
vertex set Q with |Q|<=|M| such that every edge of F with both
endpoints outside Q belongs to M. Assume:

  (A1) Both links G[N(u)] and G[N(v)] are connected and not WKE.
  (A2) For each t in C,
       deg_H(t)+|N(t) intersect A|+|N(t) intersect B|<=5.

Theorem. Under (A1) and (A2), the pair {u,v} is reducible. More
precisely, there are an edge-disjoint triangle packing S and an
edge set X satisfying |X|<=2|S|, such that X meets every triangle
containing u or v and contains every packed edge whose endpoints
both avoid {u,v}. One of the four recipes below supplies such
a certificate, with at most seven triangles.

All core neighborhoods of private vertices in this proof mean
N_C(t)=N(t) intersect C. A side-overlap set is

  D_A=N_C(a) intersect N_C(b),
  D_B=N_C(c) intersect N_C(d).

2. THE FULL-SUPPORT INPUT

We use Lemma S of research/round6/E/diamond_core.txt in its general
form: the four-core may be any graph with a perfect matching.
Here is the exact statement and proof for a self-contained argument.

Lemma S. Let F have vertex set {z} union C union {a,b}, where
|C|=4, F[C] has a perfect matching, z is adjacent to every vertex
of C, and z is adjacent to neither a nor b. If F is connected and
not WKE, every core vertex has a neighbor in {a,b}.

Proof. First, the attachment support has size at least three.
Otherwise contain it in a two-set T={s,t} and put Q={z,s,t}.
The only possible edges outside Q are e, the core edge on C\T,
and ab. A matching of size three containing those which exist is:

  if e,ab exist: {e,ab,zs};
  if e exists and ab does not: {e,sa,zt}, after relabeling the
      privates and T using a private-core edge from connectedness;
  if only ab exists: a core perfect matching together with ab;
  if neither exists: choose a private-core edge ra, a core
      perfect-matching edge f avoiding r, and the core vertex s'
      outside {r} union V(f); take {ra,f,zs'}.

In each case Q and the matching witness WKE, a contradiction.
This is also Lemma M of compression/round3/E/path_core.txt.

Now if t in C has no private neighbor, put Q=C\{t}. The only
edges outside Q are zt and possibly ab. If ab exists, take
M={zt,ab,e}, where e is the core perfect-matching edge avoiding t.
If ab does not exist, both privates have nonempty neighborhoods
in Q by connectedness. These admit a two-edge matching unless
both are the same singleton: if one neighborhood has two choices,
one differs from any fixed neighbor of the other. The singleton
case contradicts support size at least three. Add zt to this
two-edge matching. In either event |Q|=|M|=3 and M contains
every edge outside Q, again a contradiction. QED.

The four-cycle has a perfect matching, so Lemma S applies to
each link. Every core vertex is supported on each side. Since
deg_H(t)=2, (A2) now says exactly that

  D_A intersect D_B is empty.                              (1)

Indeed, a core vertex has one or two attachments on each side,
and (A2) forbids two on both sides simultaneously.

3. FOUR EXPLICIT UNIVERSAL RECIPES

These recipes use the setup of Section 1, but do not require
(A1) or (A2). Unspecified adjacencies are arbitrary. Each recipe
may be relabeled by a core automorphism, a permutation of either
private pair, and an exchange of the two hubs with their sides.
A triple denotes its triangle and a pair denotes its edge.

P. A private edge.

If cd is present, take

  S={uxp,uyq,vxq,vyp,vcd},
  X=E(H) union {cd,uv,ua,ub,vc,vd}.

The four core rims are distinct, their spoke pairs are disjoint
at each hub, and vcd uses the two other v-spokes. Thus S is an
edge-disjoint packing of five triangles and |X|=10. All external
packed edges are in E(H) union {cd}. A triangle through both
hubs meets uv; every other hub triangle either meets a private
spoke in X or has its rim in E(H). This proves coverage. If ab
is present instead, exchange sides.

A. An asymmetric attachment on an induced three-vertex path.

If xa and yc are present while ya and pa are absent, take

  S={uvq,uyp,uxa,vyc,vpx},
  E0={yp,xa,yc,px},
  X=E0 union {uv,uq,ub,vq,vc,vd}.

The four single-hub triangles use distinct rims and disjoint
spoke pairs at each hub, all avoiding q; hence S is edge-disjoint.
We have |S|=5 and |X|=10, with all external packed edges in E0.
The edge uv handles two-hub triangles. At u, deleting uq,ub
leaves rim vertices x,y,p,a: their only possible edges are
xp,yp,xa, all in E0. At v, deleting vq,vc,vd leaves x,y,p,
whose edges xp,yp are in E0. Thus all hub triangles are covered.

C. Crossed attachment matchings.

If xa,pb,yc,qd are present while pa,xb,qc are absent, take

  S={uxa,upb,uyq,vxp,vyc,vqd},
  E0={xa,pb,yq,xp,yc,qd},
  X=E0 union ({ab} intersect E(G)) union {uy,uq,vx,vp,vd}.

At u the rim pairs are {x,a},{p,b},{y,q}; at v they are
{x,p},{y,c},{q,d}. Each list is disjoint and the six rim edges
are distinct. Hence |S|=6, |X|<=12, and all external packed
edges belong to X. The core spokes uy,uq,vx,vp meet every
two-hub triangle. At u the remaining rim vertices x,p,a,b have
possible edges xp,xa,pb,ab, all in X, since pa,xb are absent.
At v the remaining rim vertices y,q,c have edges yq,yc in X
and qc absent. This proves coverage.

T. An exact opposite-pair private and an outside triangle.

Assume N_C(a)={p,q}, xb is present, and yc,yd,pd,qd are present.
Take

  S={uxb,uyq,upa,vxp,vyc,vqd,ypd},
  E0={xp,xb,py,yq,yc,yd,pa,pd,qd},
  X=E0 union {uv,uq,ub,vx,vc}.

At u the rim pairs are {x,b},{y,q},{p,a}; at v they are
{x,p},{y,c},{q,d}. Their six rims are distinct and the spoke
pairs at each hub are disjoint. The outside triangle ypd uses
the three new edges yp,yd,pd, so it is disjoint from those six
triangles. Thus |S|=7 and |X|=14; all external packed edges are
exactly E0. The edge uv covers two-hub triangles. At u, deleting
uq,ub leaves x,y,p,a: the possible edges xp,yp,pa are in E0,
while xy,xa,ya are absent. At v, deleting vx,vc leaves y,p,q,d:
the possible edges yp,yq,yd,pd,qd are all in E0 and pq is absent.
Therefore all hub triangles are covered.

Ambient safety for all four recipes. Every triangle through a
hub lies within that hub together with its specified neighborhood.
Consequently the link checks just given meet all ambient hub
triangles, including when there are arbitrary edges between A
and B or from this neighborhood to other vertices. The displayed
packing triangles remain present in the ambient graph, and all
their non-hub edges are explicitly in X. No ambient-degree bound
on core or private vertices is part of these sufficient recipes.

Provenance. P,A,C,T are direct specializations of O7,O4,O6,O8,
respectively, in compression/templates/optimal_patterns.txt. Their
validity is proved directly above. Neither the dictionary's
extraction from saved records nor its coverage is a premise.

4. NECESSARY ATTACHMENT STRUCTURE WHEN P AND A DO NOT APPLY

By recipe P, it remains to consider ab and cd both absent.
Connectedness of each link then makes every private vertex's
core neighborhood nonempty.

Suppose a private vertex sees exactly one vertex of an opposite
pair in the core, and misses a vertex of the other opposite pair.
Relabel the first pair x,y so it sees x and misses y, and call
the missed vertex of the other pair p. Lemma S supplies an
opposite-side neighbor c of y. These are exactly the conditions
of recipe A. Therefore, if A does not apply, the following holds:

  (*) Whenever a private neighborhood meets one opposite pair
      in exactly one vertex, it contains the other pair in full.

As a consequence, every private core neighborhood is one of:

  an opposite pair; a three-element set; or all four core vertices.
                                                               (2)

Indeed, a singleton or an adjacent two-set violates (*); the
neighborhood is nonempty, and the only remaining two-sets are
the opposite pairs. The larger sets in (2) satisfy (*).

Lemma D. Under the current assumptions, if a core vertex t has
both private vertices of a side as neighbors, one of those
privates is adjacent to t and to both cycle neighbors of t.

Proof. Write r,s for the cycle neighbors of t and t' for its
opposite. Suppose neither private sees both r,s. By (2), the
possible neighborhoods containing t and missing at least one
of r,s are {t,t'}, C\{r}, and C\{s}. Full support at r,s forces
the two private neighborhoods to be C\{r} and C\{s}; no other
pair of these choices covers both r,s. Name them a,b so a misses
r and b misses s. In this link, with opposite hub z, take

  Q={z,t,t'}, M={rb,sa,zt}.

The vertices outside Q are r,s,a,b. Their only edges are rb,sa:
rs and ab are absent, as are ra,sb. The three edges of M are
present and pairwise disjoint. Hence Q,M witness WKE, a
contradiction. QED.

We will also use the same WKE obstruction in the following form:

  If a side's two neighborhoods are distinct triples, their
  missing vertices cannot form an opposite pair.                (3)

For if the missing vertices are opposite r,s, the same Q,M just
displayed gives a WKE witness. This uses no numerical check.

Here is the resulting sufficient condition for recipe T. If one
side has a private neighborhood equal to one opposite pair Y,
and the other side doubles a vertex t of the complementary pair
C\Y, recipe T applies. To see this, call t=y, its opposite x,
and Y={p,q}; call the first private a. Full support supplies xb.
Lemma D supplies a private d on the other side adjacent to
y,p,q, and its other private c is adjacent to y because y is
doubled. This is precisely T's signed-edge hypothesis.

5. COMPLETE STRUCTURAL CASE SPLIT

Assume P and A did not already give the desired reduction.
Thus (1), (2), Lemma D, and (3) hold; both sides have full support.

Case I. At least one overlap set is empty.

Exchange sides so D_A is empty. Since the two nonempty A
neighborhoods cover C, are disjoint, and each has size at least
two by (2), they are complementary opposite pairs.

If D_B is nonempty, choose a doubled vertex t in D_B and choose
the A-private whose neighborhood is the opposite pair not
containing t. The sufficient condition for T at the end of
Section 4 applies.

If D_B is also empty, both sides partition C into opposite pairs.
Relabel privates so

  N_C(a)=N_C(c)={x,y}, N_C(b)=N_C(d)={p,q}.

Recipe C applies directly: xa,pb,yc,qd are present, while
pa,xb,qc are absent. This completes Case I.

Case II. Both overlap sets are nonempty and some private
neighborhood is an exact opposite pair.

Exchange sides and opposite pairs so N_C(a)={p,q}. Full support
makes b adjacent to x,y, and D_A is a nonempty subset of {p,q}.

If D_B meets {x,y}, apply the sufficient condition for T.
Otherwise D_B is a nonempty subset of {p,q}. By (1), the two
nonempty disjoint subsets D_A,D_B of this two-set must be its
two singletons. Relabel p,q so D_A={p} and D_B={q}. Then

  N_C(a)={p,q}, N_C(b)={x,y,p}.

On side B the union is C and the intersection is {q}, so the
neighborhood sizes sum to five. By (2) they are two and three;
the two-set is an opposite pair containing q, necessarily {p,q}.
Relabel c,d to obtain

  N_C(c)={p,q}, N_C(d)={x,y,q}.

Apply recipe C on first-side matching qa,xb and second-side
matching pc,yd. Its three crossed absences are xa,qb,yc, all
true. Explicitly its labels (x,p,y,q,a,b,c,d) are replaced by
(q,x,p,y,a,b,c,d); this is a core automorphism. Thus Case II
also gives a reduction.

Case III. Both overlap sets are nonempty and no private
neighborhood is an exact opposite pair.

Every private neighborhood has size at least three by (2).
On either side its two neighborhoods cover four vertices, so
their intersection has size at least two. Disjointness (1)
forces |D_A|=|D_B|=2 and D_B=C\D_A. The two neighborhood sizes
on each side therefore sum to six, and both must be three.
Their missing vertices are distinct, since otherwise full
support would fail.

Let r,s be the two missing vertices on side A. They are precisely
C\D_A=D_B and, by (3), are adjacent. The complementary two-set
{t,w}=D_A is also an edge of the four-cycle. Label r,s,t,w so
r-s-t-w-r is its cycle order. Each of r,s is supported by exactly
one A-private, and these are different privates: relabel them
so ra,sb are present and sa,rb absent.

Similarly, t,w are the two missing vertices on side B, hence
they have different unique B-neighbors. Relabel so tc,wd are
present and wc,td absent. Apply recipe C with

  (x,p,y,q,a,b,c,d)=(r,s,t,w,a,b,c,d).

This is a core automorphism, its required core edges are rs,tw,
and its crossed absences are sa,rb,wc. All hold. Case III is
therefore reduced.

These three cases exhaust the overlap sets and the neighborhood
types in (2). Together with the earlier P and A reductions they
prove the theorem.

6. SCOPE AND LOGICAL DEPENDENCIES

The entire four-cycle-core case is proved from (A1) and (A2).
Connected non-WKE links supply full support, nonempty private
neighborhoods after removing the private-edge case, and the
three-edge WKE obstruction of Lemma D and (3). The degree budget
is used only to make D_A,D_B disjoint. Four fixed recipes supply
all required ambient packing/cover certificates.

The argument does not use the saved C4 records as an exhaustive
list and does not infer a theorem from testing those records.
It does not settle another remaining core type, the degree-five
or degree-six sparse-counterexample branches, or mad(G)<8.
