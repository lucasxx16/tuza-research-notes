ROUND 7 / D
TWO AMBIENT-SAFE REDUCTIONS FOR A COMMON FOUR-CLIQUE

Status: reviewed research proof; see C/review_D_full.txt.
No novelty claim is made. The proof is symbolic and self-contained;
the final corollaries explicitly identify their imported hypotheses.

1. DEFINITIONS AND THE AMBIENT REDUCTION BRIDGE.

All graphs are finite and simple. A triangle packing is a collection
of pairwise edge-disjoint triangles. A triangle cover is a set of
edges meeting every triangle. Their maximum and minimum sizes are
nu(G) and tau(G), respectively.

A nonempty vertex set Z is reducible if there are a triangle packing
S and an edge set X such that
  (i) |X|<=2|S|;
  (ii) X meets every triangle containing a vertex of Z;
  (iii) X contains every edge of S with both endpoints outside Z.

To recall why this is sufficient, put G'=(G-X)-Z. If G' satisfies
Tuza's inequality, choose a maximum packing P and a minimum cover Y
there. No edge of P belongs to S: an S-edge either meets Z or belongs
to X. Hence P union S is an edge-disjoint packing in G. The set
Y union X covers G: triangles through Z meet X, while other triangles
either meet X or survive in G'. Therefore
  tau(G)<=|Y|+|X|<=2|P|+2|S|<=2nu(G).
No assumption on other ambient edges is used in this bridge.

2. THE SIX-TRIANGLE FAMILY.

THEOREM A.
Let u,v be distinct nonadjacent vertices of an arbitrary graph G.
Let C={c1,c2,c3,c4} be a chosen four-clique contained in
N(u) intersect N(v). It need not be the entire common neighborhood.
Suppose there is a vertex r in N(v)\C that is adjacent to every
vertex of C. If d(u)+d(v)<=11, then {u,v} is reducible.

In particular r is distinct from u,v, by nonadjacency. It is allowed
to be adjacent to u. There are no restrictions on other neighbors,
their edges, or any vertices outside these sets.

Proof. Define six triangles
  S6={u c1 c2, u c3 c4,
      v c1 c3, v c2 c4,
      r c1 c4, r c2 c3}.
All exist by the stated hypotheses. The six C-edges used by them are
the six distinct edges of the K4. The two u-triangles use disjoint
u-spokes, and likewise the two v-triangles. The last two triangles
use the four distinct r-to-C edges. Since u,v,r are distinct and
outside C, these spokes cannot coincide with each other or with
the six C-edges. Thus S6 is edge-disjoint.

Write
  R=E(G[C]) union {r c: c in C},
  Uout={u x: x in N(u)\C},
  Vout={v y: y in N(v)\(C union {r})},
  X6=R union Uout union Vout.
The set R consists of exactly ten edges, namely all edges of the
K5 on C union {r}. The three displayed edge sets are pairwise
disjoint: R avoids u,v, while Uout and Vout could share only uv,
which is absent. Consequently
  |X6|=10+(d(u)-4)+(d(v)-5)=d(u)+d(v)+1<=12=2|S6|.

Every triangle through u either uses a neighbor outside C and meets
Uout, or has both other vertices in C and meets E(G[C]). Every
triangle through v either uses a neighbor outside C union {r} and
meets Vout, or has its other two vertices in that K5 and meets R.
Thus X6 meets every triangle through u or v, including any affected
by arbitrary ambient edges among their remaining neighbors.

Finally, every packed edge not incident to u or v belongs to R:
these are the six C-edges and the four r-to-C edges. Conditions
(i)-(iii) all hold, proving reducibility.

3. THE SEVEN-TRIANGLE FAMILY AND THE EXTRA DEGREE UNIT.

THEOREM B.
Keep the structural hypotheses of Theorem A, and suppose in addition
that some q in N(v)\(C union {r}) is adjacent to r. Replace the degree
bound by d(u)+d(v)<=12. Then {u,v} is reducible.

Again q may also neighbor u; neither q nor r is required to be a
private neighbor. Nonadjacency ensures q is distinct from u,v.

Proof. Use the same six triangles and add vqr:
  S7=S6 union {vqr},
  X7=X6 union {qr}.
The edges vq and vr are not used by S6, whose v-triangles use only
v-to-C edges. The new external edge qr also avoids every S6-edge:
q is outside C union {r,u,v}. Hence S7 is edge-disjoint. The edge qr
is not already in X6, since it avoids u,v and is not internal to
C union {r}. Thus
  |X7|=d(u)+d(v)+2<=14=2|S7|.
The set X6 already covers every triangle through u or v. The new
triangle has just one edge outside {u,v}, namely qr, which was added
to X7. All three reduction conditions therefore hold.

The degree bounds give explicit useful cases. Theorem A covers a
degree-five/six pair. Theorem B also covers a degree-five/seven pair
or a degree-six/six pair under its extra qr condition. The additional
degree unit is handled by the outside-spoke cover, with no unexamined
ambient triangle case.

4. NECESSARY OVERLAP RESTRICTIONS IN AN IRREDUCIBLE ROBUST GRAPH.

The following corollaries use exactly these imported consequences
of Puleo's Lemma 2.7(a),(b): in a robust graph with no reducible set,
degree-at-most-six vertices are independent, and every such link
has complement of maximum degree at most one and not exactly two
edges. Thus a five-link is K5 or K5 minus one edge; a six-link is
K6, K6 minus one edge, or K6 minus a three-edge perfect matching.
The last type is thin. In particular it contains no four-clique.

COROLLARY 1 (FIVE/SIX OVERLAP).
In a robust graph with no reducible set, a degree-five vertex and a
degree-six vertex cannot have four common neighbors inducing K4.
The six-vertex is automatically non-thin if such a K4 is present.

Proof. Call the vertices u,v, of degrees five and six respectively,
and take such a common four-clique C. They are nonadjacent by the
imported independence conclusion. Since the six-link contains C,
it is not thin, and consequently has at most one missing edge.
There are two vertices outside C in N(v). At least one, call it r,
is complete to C: a failure for both would require two distinct
missing edges. Theorem A now makes {u,v} reducible, a contradiction.

In particular the two vertices cannot have five common neighbors:
these would be all five neighbors of u, whose K5 or K5-e link always
contains a K4. Hence their codegree is at most four, and if it is
four their common-neighbor graph cannot be K4. Using the same link
restriction, the latter graph must be K4 minus one edge.

COROLLARY 2 (SIX/SIX FOUR-CLIQUE OVERLAP).
In a robust graph with no reducible set, let degree-six vertices
u,v have a chosen common four-clique C. Each of the two pairs
  N(u)\C and N(v)\C
must be a nonedge. More precisely, the sole missing edge of each
six-link must join its two vertices outside C.

Proof. Both links contain K4, so they are non-thin and have at most
one missing edge. Write N(v)\C={q,r}. If qr were present, at least
one of q,r would be complete to C, for otherwise two distinct
missing edges would be required. Relabel it as r and apply Theorem B
to the degree-six/six pair, a contradiction. Therefore qr is absent.
As this link has at most one missing edge, it is exactly K6 minus qr.
Interchanging u,v proves the other assertion.

Both corollaries hold without any maximum-average-degree hypothesis.
In particular they apply to a subgraph-minimal counterexample with
mad<8: all proper subgraphs then satisfy Tuza because the mad bound
is subgraph-closed, so the reduction bridge forbids reducible sets;
Puleo's Lemma 2.6 gives robustness. Neither corollary claims that
its surviving configurations actually occur in a minimal counterexample.

5. THE ROUND-SIX OBSTRUCTION J IS LOCALLY EXCLUDED.

Recall the labeled J from research/round6/D/joint_caps_barrier.txt:
core {w,0,1,2,3,4,5,6} induces K8 minus 01 and 35, and independent
a,b,c,t have neighborhoods 03456,01256,01234,123456 respectively.

Apply Theorem A with
  u=b, v=t, (c1,c2,c3,c4)=(1,2,5,6), r=4.
The graph on C={1,2,5,6} is K4, and 4 is complete to C and adjacent
to t. The degrees are five and six. The explicit certificate is
  S6={12b,56b,15t,26t,146,245},
  X6={12,15,16,25,26,56,14,24,45,46,0b,3t}.
It has six triangles and twelve cover edges. It does not use any
edge from 0 to the core, or the edge 34, or any edges involving a,c,w.
All ambient triangles through b or t are covered by the general proof.

Since 34 is present, Theorem B also gives
  S7=S6 union {34t}, X7=X6 union {34},
with seven triangles and thirteen cover edges. This is the fixed-J
certificate returned by the bounded delegated search, expressed in
the universal construction. The spare edge of cover budget permits
one extra incident edge at either center while the stated structural
hypotheses are retained.

Thus J is excluded by a reduction on {b,t}, independently of whether
Tuza happens to hold on the whole graph. The result is strictly local
and also applies inside any larger graph satisfying the theorem's
degree and neighborhood hypotheses. It does not retract the round-six
logical barrier: those earlier numerical conditions alone permit J;
the additional overlap reduction now supplies a further restriction.
