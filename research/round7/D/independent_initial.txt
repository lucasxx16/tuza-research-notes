Here is the direct check, using the definition that H is WKE if some
matching M and vertex set Q satisfy |Q|<=|M| and H-M has vertex cover Q.
Necessarily H-Q has maximum degree at most one.

The low-degree links K5-e and K6-e cannot meet this condition: Q has
size at most two or three respectively, leaving at least three core
vertices with at least two edges. The w-link is K7 minus a matching
of size two; Q has size at most three, leaving at least four vertices
whose induced minimum degree is at least two.

For any degree-nine core vertex, its link consists of a K6-e core
and three independent low vertices. Since |Q|<=4, at least four core
vertices must lie in Q: any three remaining core vertices induce at
least two edges. Thus Q is four core vertices and leaves two core
vertices plus the three low vertices. If the remaining pair is an
edge, at least one endpoint has an outside neighbor, so its degree is
at least two. (The union of the three outside neighborhoods contains
every core vertex except w.) If it is the unique missing edge, some
outside vertex sees both endpoints: for core center 0 this vertex is
a, for center 1 it is t, for center 3 it is c, and for center 5 it is b.
That outside vertex then has degree two. Both possibilities fail.

For degree-ten centers 2,4,6, the link core is K7 minus the two-edge
matching {01,35}, with three independent low vertices. Since |Q|<=5,
at least five core vertices must be removed: any three remaining
core vertices induce at least two edges. The remaining pair cannot
be an edge, since the low neighborhoods cover every core vertex
except w. It cannot be 01 or 35 either: at each such center, one of
its low neighbors sees both ends of 01, and one sees both ends of 35.
Again H-Q has degree at least two somewhere. Hence all links fail WKE.

This is only a check of that specific old singleton criterion. It
does not assert that no other old reduction excludes J, nor that a
non-WKE link makes its center irreducible.
