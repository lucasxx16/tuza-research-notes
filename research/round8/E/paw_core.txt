COMPLETE HUMAN PROOF FOR THE ORIGINAL PAW CORE

Status: reviewed census-free research proof; see
research/round8/C/review_E_full.txt.
No saved-record count, enumeration, numerical witness, or catalogue
completeness claim is a premise.

1. SETUP AND THEOREM

Let u,v be adjacent degree-seven vertices in a finite simple graph.
Their common neighborhood is C={p,q,r,s}, inducing the paw H:

  E(H)={pq,pr,qr,ps}.

Thus pqr is a triangle and s is pendant at p; qs,rs are absent.
Let A={a,b}, B={c,d} be the private pairs, so

  N(u)={v} union C union A,
  N(v)={u} union C union B.

Edges between A and B, and edges leading outside this neighborhood,
are unrestricted. A graph F is WKE if a matching M and a vertex
set Q satisfy |Q|<=|M| and every edge of F with both endpoints
outside Q belongs to M. Assume:

  (A1) The induced links G[N(u)] and G[N(v)] are connected and
       not WKE.
  (A2) For every t in C,
       deg_H(t)+|N(t) intersect A|+|N(t) intersect B|<=5.

Theorem. Under (A1) and (A2), the pair {u,v} is reducible: there
are an edge-disjoint triangle packing S and an edge set X with
|X|<=2|S|, such that X meets every triangle containing u or v
and contains every packed edge whose endpoints avoid both hubs.
The proof gives a certificate with at most six triangles.

Write N_C(w)=N(w) intersect C for a private vertex's core
neighborhood. The argument first proves full support and a
unique attachment at p on each side, then reduces a complete
empty-or-three-set classification by four explicit recipes.

2. FULL SUPPORT AND THE UNIQUE ATTACHMENT AT p

We use the general form of Lemma S from
research/round6/E/diamond_core.txt, also reproduced in
research/round7/E/c4_core.txt. Its hypotheses and proof follow.

Lemma S. Suppose a seven-vertex link F has vertex set
{z} union C union {a,b}, where |C|=4, F[C] has a perfect
matching, and z is adjacent to all of C and to neither private
vertex. If F is connected and not WKE, every vertex of C has
a neighbor in {a,b}.

Proof. First the support has size at least three. If it lies
in a two-set T={x,y}, put Q={z,x,y}. Outside Q the only possible
edges are e, the edge on C\T, and ab. A three-edge matching
containing whichever of these edges are present is:

  if both exist: {e,ab,zx};
  if e exists but ab does not: {e,xa,zy}, after relabeling T
      and the privates using a private-core edge from connectedness;
  if only ab exists: a core perfect matching together with ab;
  if neither exists: take a private-core edge ta, a core perfect-
      matching edge f avoiding t, and the core vertex w outside
      {t} union V(f); use {ta,f,zw}.

Each choice witnesses WKE, proving the support-size claim. This
is the support-size Lemma M of compression/round3/E/path_core.txt.

Now suppose t in C is unsupported. Set Q=C\{t}. Its outside
edges are exactly zt and possibly ab. If ab exists, use
M={zt,ab,e}, where e is a core perfect-matching edge avoiding t.
If ab is absent, connectedness gives both privates nonempty
neighborhoods in Q. There is a two-edge attachment matching
unless both neighborhoods are the same singleton; the latter
contradicts support size at least three. Add zt to that two-edge
matching. In either case |M|=|Q|=3 and M contains every outside
edge, a contradiction. QED.

The paw has perfect matching {ps,qr}. Apply Lemma S on both
sides. Every core vertex has a private neighbor on each side.
Since deg_H(p)=3, (A2) forces p to have exactly one private
neighbor on each side. Relabel so these are a and c. Thus

  pa,pc are present; pb,pd are absent.                      (1)

3. FOUR UNIVERSAL PAW RECIPES

The following recipes require only the setup of Section 1 and
their stated signed-edge conditions; they do not require (A1)
or (A2). Unspecified adjacencies are arbitrary. Each is valid
after swapping the two hubs with their sides and relabeling
the private pairs as required. A triple denotes a triangle and
a pair an edge. The variable names t,w,h below specify an
orientation of a path, not necessarily a paw automorphism.

A. Asymmetry on an induced three-vertex path.

Let {t,w} be either {s,q} or {s,r}, and let h be the fourth
core vertex outside {t,p,w}. Thus tp,pw are present and tw is
absent. Suppose ta,wc are present and wa,pa are absent. Take

  S={uvh,uwp,uta,vwc,vpt},
  E0={wp,ta,wc,pt},
  X=E0 union {uv,uh,ub,vh,vc,vd}.

The four single-hub triangles have distinct rims and disjoint
spoke pairs at each hub, all avoiding h. They are edge-disjoint
from uvh, so |S|=5 and |X|=10. The external packed edges are
exactly E0. The edge uv covers two-hub triangles. At u, deleting
uh,ub leaves rim vertices t,w,p,a, whose possible edges are
tp,pw,ta, all in E0; tw,wa,pa are absent. At v, deleting vh,vc,vd
leaves t,w,p, whose only edges tp,pw are in E0. This proves
coverage of all hub triangles.

E. Empty unused private neighborhoods on both sides.

Suppose qa,sc are present, and neither b nor d has a neighbor
in {s,p,q}. Take

  S={uvr,usp,uqa,vsc,vqp},
  E0={sp,qa,sc,qp},
  X=E0 union {uv,ur,ua,vr,vc}.

The spoke pairs at each hub are disjoint, avoid r, and have
four distinct rims. Thus |S|=5 and |X|=9<=10. All external
packed edges lie in E0. The edge uv covers two-hub triangles.
At u the remaining rim vertices after deleting ur,ua are
s,p,q,b; at v, after deleting vr,vc, they are s,p,q,d.
Their only possible edges are sp,pq, both in E0. Thus X
covers every hub triangle. Private edges ab,cd are unrestricted
and are covered by the deleted spokes ua,vc.

M. One empty private neighborhood and one three-set.

Assume qa,ra,ab,sd,pc are present while qb,rb,pd are absent.
Take

  S={uvq,ups,uab,vsd,vpc,qra},
  E0={qr,qa,ra,ps,sd,pc,ab},
  X=E0 union {up,us,vq,vr,vc}.

The two u-only rim pairs are {p,s},{a,b}, and the two v-only
pairs are {s,d},{p,c}; all avoid q. Their four rims are distinct.
The three edges qr,qa,ra of the outside triangle are distinct
from those rims and use no hub edge. Consequently the six
triangles are edge-disjoint, |X|=12=2|S|, and every external
packed edge is in E0. The core spokes up,us,vq,vr cover every
two-hub triangle. At u the remaining rim vertices q,r,a,b
have possible edges qr,qa,ra,ab, all in E0; qb,rb are absent.
At v the remaining rim vertices p,s,d have possible edges
ps,sd, both in E0, and pd is absent. Coverage follows.

T. Dominant asymmetry against a matched core edge.

Let {t,w} be {s,q} or {s,r}, and let h be the other triangle
vertex, namely the core vertex outside {t,w,p}. Suppose ta,hd,pc
are present while wa,pd are absent. Take

  S={uvw,uph,uta,vhd,vpc},
  E0={ph,ta,hd,pc},
  X=E0 union {up,uh,ub,vt,vw,vc}.

The u-only rim pairs {p,h},{t,a} and the v-only pairs {h,d},{p,c}
are disjoint at their respective hubs and avoid w. Their four
rims are distinct, so the packing is edge-disjoint and |S|=5,
|X|=10. All external packed edges lie in E0. The four core
spokes up,uh,vt,vw cover all two-hub triangles. At u the
remaining rim vertices t,w,a have possible edge ta in E0;
tw and wa are absent. At v the remaining rim vertices p,h,d
have possible edges ph,hd in E0 and pd absent. This covers
every hub triangle.

For an explicit mapping into historical O1, set
(c0,c1,c2,c3)=(h,t,p,w), with its B-labels (b0,b1)=(d,c)
and its attached A-vertex a1=a. For the q,s pair this is
(r,q,p,s) when t=q, or (r,s,p,q) when t=s. The r,s pair
is obtained by exchanging q and r. In each orientation the
crucial absent B-attachment is p d, and the two other absences
are tw and wa.

Ambient safety. Every triangle through one of the hubs is
contained in that hub together with its specified neighborhood.
The residual-link checks above therefore cover all such triangles
in the ambient graph. Edges between A and B cannot create another
kind of hub triangle, and edges leaving the neighborhood cannot
do so either. Every displayed packed edge avoiding the hubs is
explicitly included in X. All four certificates therefore meet
the ambient reducibility definition, including the outside
triangle of recipe M.

Provenance. A,E,M,T specialize O4,O9,O5,O1, respectively, from
compression/templates/optimal_patterns.txt. The validity of
each recipe is proved directly here. Catalogue coverage is
not used to force any branch.

4. THE COMPLETE STRUCTURAL DISJUNCTION

Use the labels in (1), so a,c are the unique private neighbors
of p and b,d miss p.

If b has different adjacency on s and q, orient the induced
path s-p-q so its endpoint t is seen by b and endpoint w is
missed. Since b also misses p and the other side supports w,
recipe A applies with b as its attached first-side private.
The same holds for different adjacency on s and r. The identical
argument applies to d after exchanging sides.

Thus, unless recipe A already reduces the pair, each of b,d
has equal adjacency to s,q,r. By (1) this means

  N_C(b) is empty or {s,q,r},
  N_C(d) is empty or {s,q,r}.                               (2)

If one of these neighborhoods is empty, that private vertex
has no core neighbor and no edge to the opposite hub. The
connectedness of its seven-vertex link therefore forces its
private edge. Full support then makes the other private of
that side adjacent to every core vertex. In particular:

  N_C(b) empty implies ab present and N_C(a)=C;
  N_C(d) empty implies cd present and N_C(c)=C.              (3)

Case I. Both neighborhoods in (2) are empty.

By (3), qa and sc are present, and b,d both miss {s,p,q}.
Recipe E applies directly.

Case II. Exactly one neighborhood in (2) is empty.

Exchange sides so N_C(b) is empty and N_C(d)={s,q,r}.
Then qa,ra,ab are present by (3), and qb,rb are absent.
Also sd is present, while pc is present and pd absent by (1).
These are exactly the hypotheses of recipe M. This completes
the mixed case without any restriction on cd or other attachments.

Case III. Both neighborhoods in (2) are {s,q,r}.

Suppose a has different adjacency on s and q, or on s and r.
Choose t to be the seen endpoint and w the missed endpoint
of that pair, and let h be the remaining triangle vertex.
Since d sees both q and r, hd is present. Moreover pc is
present and pd absent by (1). Recipe T applies. The same
argument applies to c after exchanging sides, using b at
the remaining triangle vertex.

If neither application of T is available, adjacency of a to
s,q,r is constant, and the same is true for c. Each of their
core neighborhoods is therefore either {p} or all of C.

The alternative N_C(a)={p} makes its link WKE. To see this,
write z for the opposite hub in that link and take

  Q={z,p,b}, M={qr,pa,zs}.

The outside vertices are q,r,s,a. Their only edge is qr:
qs,rs are absent in the paw, and a has no neighbor among q,r,s.
The three edges of M exist and are disjoint, so this is a WKE
witness regardless of whether ab is present. This contradicts
(A1). The symmetric witness rules out N_C(c)={p}.

Consequently both a and c see every core vertex. But q then
has all four privates as neighbors, since b and d also see q.
Its budget expression in (A2) is 2+2+2=6>5, a contradiction.
Thus Case III must already have been reduced by T.

The cases exhaust (2), and failure of (2) was reduced by A.
Each branch has a checked packing and cover, proving the theorem.

5. SCOPE AND DEPENDENCIES

This proves the entire original paw-core case under (A1) and
(A2). Full support uses the paw's perfect matching. The degree
budget is used to force the unique attachment at p on each
side and to exclude four private attachments at q in the final
branch. Connectedness supplies the forced private edge in (3),
and non-WKE excludes the displayed {p}-only dominant link.

No inspection or classification of the saved paw records is
part of the argument. This document does not claim another
core type or any further step toward the full mad(G)<8 result.
