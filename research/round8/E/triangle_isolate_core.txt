COMPLETE HUMAN PROOF FOR THE TRIANGLE-PLUS-ISOLATE CORE

Status: reviewed census-free research proof; see
research/round8/C/review_tri_full.txt.
The core has no perfect matching. No perfect-matching support lemma,
saved-record count, enumeration, or numerical result is a premise.

1. SETUP AND THEOREM

Let u,v be adjacent degree-seven vertices in a finite simple graph.
Their common neighborhood is C=T union {s}, where T={p,q,r}
induces a triangle and s is isolated in the core H=G[C]. Thus

  E(H)={pq,pr,qr}.

Let A={a,b} and B={c,d} be their private pairs, so

  N(u)={v} union C union A,
  N(v)={u} union C union B.

Edges between A and B and edges leaving this neighborhood are
unrestricted. A graph F is WKE if a matching M and a vertex
set Q satisfy |Q|<=|M| and every edge of F with both endpoints
outside Q belongs to M. Assume:

  (A1) Both induced links G[N(u)] and G[N(v)] are connected
       and not WKE.
  (A2) For each t in C,
       deg_H(t)+|N(t) intersect A|+|N(t) intersect B|<=5.

Theorem. Under (A1) and (A2), the pair {u,v} is reducible by
five edge-disjoint triangles and a ten-edge set X meeting all
triangles through u or v and containing every packed edge
whose endpoints both avoid {u,v}. More specifically, the
hypotheses force an instance of the explicit O1 recipe in
Section 4.

The proof first derives two link necessities by explicit WKE
witnesses. In particular, support at s is proved directly;
it is not inferred from support at the triangle vertices.

2. EVERY PRIVATE VERTEX SEES TWO TRIANGLE VERTICES

Work in one link F, with vertex set {z} union C union {a,b}.
The opposite hub z is adjacent to every vertex of C and to
neither a nor b. The only other possible edges are the three
triangle edges, private-core attachments, and the private edge ab.

Lemma 1. If F is connected and not WKE, each private vertex
has at least two neighbors in T.

Proof. Suppose b misses two triangle vertices, relabeled p,q.
Let r be the third triangle vertex and set

  Q={z,a,r}.

The vertices outside Q are p,q,s,b. Their only possible edges
are pq and sb: p,q miss b by assumption; s has no core edges.
We exhibit a three-edge matching containing those edges that
are present, in every case.

  Case 1: sb is present.
    M={pq,sb,zr}.

  Case 2: sb is absent and rb is present.
    M={pq,rb,zs}.

  Case 3: sb and rb are both absent.
    The vertex b has no core neighbor. It also misses z, so
    connectedness forces ab. Take M={pq,ab,zs}.

All three matchings have disjoint edges and contain every edge
outside Q; their sizes equal |Q|=3. The edge ab may be either
present or absent in Cases 1 and 2 because a belongs to Q.
In Case 3 its absence is ruled out by connectedness. Thus all
private-edge possibilities have been covered. Each case proves
F WKE, a contradiction. Exchange the private labels to obtain
the same conclusion for a. QED.

3. FULL SUPPORT, INCLUDING THE ISOLATED CORE VERTEX

Lemma 2. Under the hypotheses of Lemma 1, every vertex of C
has a private neighbor.

Proof. Suppose t in C is adjacent to neither a nor b. Put

  Q=C\{t}.

The vertices outside Q are z,t,a,b, whose only edges are zt
and possibly ab. There are two cases.

If ab is present, choose a triangle edge e avoiding t and
take M={zt,ab,e}. Such an edge e always exists: if t is a
triangle vertex, use the edge on the other two; if t=s,
use any triangle edge. This is a three-edge matching containing
both outside edges.

If ab is absent, Lemma 1 gives each private at least two
triangle neighbors. All these neighbors avoid t because t
is unsupported. Choose distinct triangle vertices x,y with
ax,by present: after any choice of x for a, b has a choice
different from x. Take M={zt,ax,by}. These edges are disjoint
and contain the sole outside edge zt.

In both cases |M|=|Q|=3, contradicting non-WKE. This argument
also applies verbatim when t=s, proving isolated-vertex
support rather than assuming it. QED.

4. ONE UNIVERSAL FIVE-TRIANGLE RECIPE

Partition the four core vertices into the two unordered pairs
{j,k} and {t,w}, where j,k are triangle vertices, {t,w} consists
of the remaining triangle vertex and s, and the order within
either pair can be chosen. In particular, jk is present and
tw absent.

Use private labels A={alpha,beta}, B={gamma,delta}. Suppose

  t alpha, j gamma, k delta are present,
  w alpha, k gamma are absent.                            (4)

No restriction on either private edge or any other attachment
is imposed. Set

  S={uvw,ujk,ut alpha,vj gamma,vk delta},
  E0={jk,t alpha,j gamma,k delta},
  X=E0 union {uj,uk,u beta,vt,vw,v delta}.

Here spacing merely separates a Greek-label vertex: for example
ut alpha is the triangle with vertices u,t,alpha.

All triangles exist. At u the two single-hub triangles use
the disjoint rim pairs {j,k},{t,alpha}; at v the pairs are
{j,gamma},{k,delta}. Both lists avoid w, so their hub spokes
are disjoint from the two spokes of uvw. Their four rim edges
are also distinct. Thus S is an edge-disjoint packing of five
triangles. The four edges of E0 avoid the hubs, and the six
additional hub edges are distinct, so |X|=10=2|S|. The external
packed edges are exactly E0, all contained in X.

For coverage, first consider triangles through both hubs.
The spokes uj,uk,vt,vw cover uv together with each of the
four core vertices. For triangles through u alone, deletion
of uj,uk,u beta leaves the rim vertices t,w,alpha. The edge
tw is absent and w alpha is absent by (4); the only possible
remaining edge t alpha lies in E0. At v, deletion of vt,vw,
v delta leaves j,k,gamma. Its possible edges jk,j gamma lie
in E0, while k gamma is absent by (4). Thus every hub triangle
meets X.

These are ambient checks: every triangle containing a hub is
formed from that hub and an edge of its specified link. Edges
between A and B or leading outside the local neighborhood
cannot create a different kind of hub triangle. Hence all
reducibility requirements hold in the ambient graph.

This is O1 from compression/templates/optimal_patterns.txt,
with its core labels (c0,c1,c2,c3)=(j,t,k,w), attached first-side
private a1=alpha, unused first-side private a0=beta, and second-
side labels (b0,b1)=(gamma,delta). Its three absent edges are
tw,w alpha,k gamma. The direct verification above is independent
of the dictionary's source or coverage.

The recipe remains valid after exchanging the two hubs with
their private sides; this exchange is essential in the forcing
argument below.

5. FORCING THE RECIPE

Apply Lemmas 1 and 2 to both links. For each triangle vertex
t define its two attachment columns

  A(t)=N(t) intersect A, B(t)=N(t) intersect B,

and define A(s),B(s) in the same way at the isolate. All of
these sets are nonempty by Lemma 2. Put

  D_A={t in T:A(t)=A}, D_B={t in T:B(t)=B}.

Lemma 1 implies D_A and D_B are nonempty: on either side
each of two privates has at least two neighbors in a three-set,
so their triangle neighborhoods intersect. Since each triangle
vertex has core degree two, (A2) forbids it from having four
private attachments. Therefore

  D_A intersect D_B is empty.                             (5)

Two nonempty disjoint subsets of the three-set T cannot both
have size at least two. Exchange sides so |D_A|=1. Each A-private
has at least two triangle neighbors; their union is T and their
intersection has size one. Consequently both have exactly two
triangle neighbors and their missing triangle vertices differ.
Thus the three columns on T are exactly

  {a}, {b}, {a,b},                                        (6)

in some order.

Fix any i in T and let j,k be the other two triangle vertices.
Their A-columns are distinct nonempty subsets of the two-set A.
Among these two columns one is a singleton. Order j,k and label
the A-privates gamma,delta so

  A(k)={delta}, gamma belongs to A(j).

This is possible because the two columns differ. It gives
the matched core edge and crossed absence

  j gamma, k delta present; k gamma absent.                (7)

Suppose B(i) differs from B(s). Some B-private alpha then sees
exactly one of i,s. Let t be the seen vertex, w the missed one,
and beta the other B-private. The pair {t,w}={i,s} is a nonedge
of the core. Apply the recipe of Section 4 after the following
explicit side exchange:

  recipe first hub = original v;
  recipe second hub = original u;
  recipe first private pair {alpha,beta} = original B;
  recipe second private pair {gamma,delta} = original A.

The first-side attachments t alpha and absent w alpha come
from the chosen difference between B(i),B(s); the second-side
matching and crossed absence are (7). This supplies a valid
five-triangle reduction.

It remains to consider the possibility that B(i)=B(s) for
every i in T. The three B-columns are then one constant
nonempty subset of B. If it is a singleton, the other B-private
has no triangle neighbor, contradicting Lemma 1. If it is B
itself, every triangle vertex belongs to D_B, contradicting
(5) because D_A is nonempty. These exhaust the nonempty
subsets of a two-set. Thus some i has B(i) different from B(s),
and the required recipe always exists. This proves the theorem.

6. SCOPE

The entire triangle-plus-isolate core is proved reducible under
(A1) and (A2), using only explicit WKE obstructions, a three-column
attachment argument, and one universally checked packing/cover
recipe. In particular the isolated core vertex's support is
proved separately in Lemma 2 without a core perfect matching.

This theorem forces O1 for this core type only. It makes no
global assertion that the same nine catalogue templates are
forced in all core types, no global template-minimality claim,
and no claim to settle the separate mad(G)<8 problem.
