ROUND 8 / A — primary-source hypothesis audit, five/six common-diamond case
Target: u deg 5, v deg 6, nonadjacent, codegree exactly 4, common C={a,b,c,d}
inducing diamond K4-e with sole missing edge ab. N(u)=C u {p}, N(v)=C u {q,r}.
Primary source: Puleo, arXiv:1308.2211v3 (definitions and Lemma 2.7 in the primary HTML).
No re-survey; only the import statements below were checked.

SOURCE-STATED FACTS USED
- Lemma 2.7(a): in a robust graph with no reducible set, every 6^- vertex v has
  Delta(overline G[N(v)]) <= 1 and |E(overline G[N(v)])| != 2.
- Lemma 2.7(b) (from Prop 5.2): the 6^- vertices form an independent set.
- Def 2.1 reducible set, Def 2.3 robust, Def 2.5 (and 7.2) thin 6-vertex;
  Lemma 2.7(c)/7.6 concerns 7-vertex subsumption of a 6-vertex, and
  Lemma 2.7(d)/7.7 concerns adjacency to a thin 6-vertex (scope only).

VERIFICATION OF THE BUNDLED HYPOTHESES
1. u,v nonadjacent: FORCED (source-stated). Both are 6^-; Lemma 2.7(b) makes
   6^- vertices pairwise nonadjacent. (No edge pair theorem is needed.)
2. Common C={a,b,c,d} with exactly four common neighbours; N(u)=C u {p} with
   unique private p; N(v)=C u {q,r} with the two privates q,r: FORCED by the
   degree counts (d(u)=5, d(v)=6) once codegree 4 is given. Not a named Puleo
   theorem; it is counting.
3. "C induces diamond, missing ab": given hypothesis (codegree 4, diamond).
   This labels a,b as the diamond's sole non-edge; c,d are adjacent (edge cd).
4. Five-link / u's private p complete to C: Lemma 2.7(a) on the 5-vertex
   neighborhood forces overline G[N(u)] in {empty, single edge}, i.e.
   G[N(u)] in {K5, K5-e} -- a SYMBOLIC CONSEQUENCE of (a), not a named theorem.
   Since a,b are a non-edge (diamond), G[N(u)] != K5; the unique missing edge
   is ab, so p is complete to {a,b,c,d}. FORCED (symbolic consequence of (a)
   plus the diamond setup).
5. Six-link / v: Lemma 2.7(a) on the 6-vertex neighborhood forces
   overline G[N(v)] in {empty, single edge, perfect 3-matching}, i.e.
   G[N(v)] in {K6, K6-e, K6-3K2} (symbolic). Since ab is a non-edge, G[N(v)]
   != K6. Two subcases, both with qr present:
   - K6-e: sole non-edge is ab, so q,r complete to C and qr in E. FORCED.
   - K6-3K2: complement is a perfect matching on N(v) holding ab plus a
     2-matching on {c,d,q,r}. Because cd is an edge (diamond), cd not in the
     complement; the 2-matching is {cq,dr} or {cr,dq}, i.e. after relabeling
     the missing edges are {ab,cq,dr}, and qr not in the complement so
     qr in E. FORCED.
   Hence "qr present and (both complete to C, OR missing cq,dr after
   relabeling)" holds in all cases. The alternative {ab,cd,qr} is impossible
   because cd is an edge.
6. Thin-6 scope: in the K6-3K2 subcase v is thin. Lemma 2.7(c)
   forbids a 7-vertex subsuming a 6-vertex, while 2.7(d) forbids a
   7-vertex adjacent to a thin 6-vertex. Neither statement restricts
   this nonadjacent 5/6 pair or alters the p/q/r conclusions. This
   observation concerns only the cited statements, not all possible
   reducibility results.

CONCLUSION
Given the codegree-4 diamond setup, the bundled hypotheses are all forced.
Nonadjacency is source-stated (Lemma 2.7(b)); every link-structure claim
(five-link K5/K5-e; six-link K6/K6-e/K6-3K2; p complete to C; qr present with
the two stated alternatives) is a SYMBOLIC CONSEQUENCE of Lemma 2.7(a) together
with the diamond setup (cd edge), not a theorem stated by Puleo. Reading-scope
statement only; no exhaustiveness or priority inference.
