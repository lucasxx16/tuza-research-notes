ROUND 8 / D
A COMMON-DIAMOND REDUCTION AND A FIVE/SIX CODEGREE BOUND

Status: reviewed research proof; see C/review_D_full.txt.
No novelty claim is made. This is a symbolic proof for arbitrary
ambient graphs; no numerical search or numerical candidate was used.

1. REDUCIBILITY AND THE AMBIENT INDUCTION BRIDGE.

All graphs are finite and simple. Let nu(G) be the largest number of
edge-disjoint triangles and tau(G) the smallest size of an edge set
meeting every triangle. A nonempty vertex set Z is reducible if there
are an edge-disjoint triangle collection S and an edge set X satisfying
  (i) |X|<=2|S|;
  (ii) X meets every triangle containing a vertex of Z;
  (iii) every S-edge with both endpoints outside Z belongs to X.

Put G'=(G-X)-Z. If tau(G')<=2nu(G'), take a maximum packing P and a
minimum cover Y of G'. By (iii), P is edge-disjoint from S. Also
X union Y covers G: a triangle through Z meets X by (ii), and a
triangle avoiding Z either meets X or survives in G'. Therefore
  tau(G)<=|X|+|Y|<=2|S|+2|P|<=2nu(G).
This explains the required ambient safety. It is stronger than merely
checking Tuza's inequality on the displayed local patch.

2. A UNIVERSAL SELECTED-DIAMOND FAMILY.

THEOREM.
Let u,v be distinct nonadjacent vertices of an arbitrary graph G.
Let C={a,b,c,d} be a chosen subset of N(u) intersect N(v), inducing
the diamond K4-ab. The set C need not be the entire common
neighborhood. Suppose
  p belongs to N(u)\C and pc,pd are edges;
  q,r are distinct vertices of N(v)\C and qr is an edge.
If d(u)+d(v)<=12, then {u,v} is reducible.

The vertex p is permitted to equal q or r. Otherwise all of p,q,r
are distinct. None belongs to C union {u,v}, by the stated membership
and nonadjacency. No hypothesis requires them to be private neighbors,
and there are no restrictions on other common neighbors, remaining
neighbor edges, or the rest of G.

Proof. Take the six triangles
  S={uac,ubd,vad,vbc,vqr,pcd}.
All their edges exist. The first four use the four distinct edges
ac,bd,ad,bc of the common four-cycle and distinct spokes at u or v.
The last triangle uses the unused common edge cd and pc,pd. The
triangle vqr uses vq,vr outside the four common spokes, and qr.
The edges pc,pd,qr are distinct: p,q,r lie outside C and q!=r.
Even if p=q or p=r, qr has both endpoints outside C, whereas pc,pd
each have one endpoint in C. None of these edges touches u or v.
Thus all six triangles are edge-disjoint, including the permitted
coincidences among p,q,r.

Define
  R=E(G[C]) union {pc,pd,qr},
  Uout={ux: x in N(u)\C},
  Vout={vy: y in N(v)\C},
  X=R union Uout union Vout.
The eight edges of R all avoid u,v. The outside-spoke sets Uout and
Vout are disjoint because their only possible common edge would be
uv, which is absent. Hence
  |X|=8+(d(u)-4)+(d(v)-4)=d(u)+d(v)<=12=2|S|.

Every triangle through u either has a neighbor outside C and meets
Uout, or has its two other vertices in C and meets E(G[C]). The
same dichotomy at v uses Vout or E(G[C]). This accounts for all
ambient triangles, including any created by extra edges among outside
neighbors or by additional common neighbors.

Finally, every S-edge outside {u,v} belongs to R: these are exactly
the five diamond edges and pc,pd,qr. The three reduction conditions
hold, proving the theorem.

The degree sum condition includes five/six, five/seven, and six/six
degree pairs when the displayed structural hypotheses hold. No new
classification of the latter two pairs is asserted here. The single
unit of slack for degrees five and six can absorb one additional
incident edge through the outside-spoke cover.

3. THE EXACT FIVE/SIX PATCHES: NON-THIN AND THIN.

Assume now that G is robust and has no reducible set. Here robust
means that every component of every induced neighborhood has at
least five vertices. We import precisely Puleo's Lemma 2.7(a),(b):
degree-at-most-six vertices are independent, and each of their link
complements is a matching whose size is not two. Therefore a five-link
is K5 or K5-e, while a six-link is K6, K6-e, or K6 minus a perfect
matching of size three. A six-vertex of the last type is thin.

Suppose d(u)=5, d(v)=6, and their entire common neighborhood is
C={a,b,c,d}, inducing K4-ab. They are nonadjacent by the imported
independence condition. Write
  N(u)=C union {p},
  N(v)=C union {q,r}.
In this exact-codegree-four application, p,q,r are distinct, p is
not a neighbor of v, and q,r are not neighbors of u. The u-link
already lacks ab, so its only missing edge is ab. In particular p
is complete to C and pc,pd exist.

There are exactly two possible types for the v-link.

Non-thin case: the v-link is K6-ab, because ab is already missing
and at most one edge may be missing. Thus qr exists.

Thin case: its missing edges form a perfect matching of size three.
One is ab. The other two form a perfect matching on {c,d,q,r}.
They cannot be {cd,qr}, since cd is present in the common diamond.
The other two possibilities are {cq,dr} and {cr,dq}. After
interchanging q,r if necessary, its missing edges are precisely
  ab,cq,dr.
Again qr exists. The argument does not discard the thin case or
silently assume that q,r are complete to C.

Thus both cases satisfy the universal theorem, and they have the
same explicit certificate
  S={uac,ubd,vad,vbc,vqr,pcd},
  X=E(G[C]) union {pc,pd,qr,up,vq,vr}.
It has six triangles and eleven edges. The edges pq,pr are arbitrary
and unused. This contradicts the assumption of no reducible set.

Consequently a degree-five/six pair cannot have an entire common
neighborhood inducing a four-vertex diamond in any robust graph
with no reducible set.

4. CLOSING THE DEGREE-FIVE/SIX CODEGREE-FOUR CASE.

For completeness, the short common-K4 argument from the reviewed
round-seven proof is reproduced here with its exact certificate.

LEMMA (COMMON-K4 EXCLUSION).
In a robust graph with no reducible set, a degree-five vertex u and
a degree-six vertex v cannot have a chosen common four-clique
Q={z1,z2,z3,z4}.

Proof. They are nonadjacent. A thin six-link, K6 minus a perfect
matching, has clique number three, so the presence of Q means the
v-link has at most one missing edge. Among the two neighbors of v
outside Q, one, say s, is complete to Q: otherwise two distinct
missing edges would be needed. Use
  T={u z1 z2, u z3 z4, v z1 z3, v z2 z4,
     s z1 z4, s z2 z3}.
Its six triangles are edge-disjoint: their Q-edges are the six
distinct clique edges, with four distinct spokes at each of u,v,s.
Take as cover all ten edges of the K5 on Q union {s}, all u-spokes
leaving Q, and all v-spokes leaving Q union {s}. There are
  10+(5-4)+(6-5)=12
edges. All packed edges outside {u,v} lie in that K5; every triangle
through either center uses a deleted outside spoke or a K5 edge.
This is a reduction, a contradiction. The vertex s may also neighbor
u; the count and the proof still hold.

COROLLARY (FIVE/SIX CODEGREE AT MOST THREE).
In every robust graph with no reducible set, vertices u,v of degrees
five and six satisfy
  |N(u) intersect N(v)|<=3.

Proof. They are nonadjacent. If they had five common neighbors, those
would be all of N(u). Its K5 or K5-e graph contains a four-clique,
contradicting the preceding lemma. More than five common neighbors
is impossible because d(u)=5. If they have exactly four common
neighbors, their induced graph is K4 or K4-e, by the five-link
restriction. The lemma excludes K4, and Section 3 excludes K4-e.
These exhaust all possibilities of codegree at least four.

This corollary uses no average-degree hypothesis. In particular it
is a necessary condition for any subgraph-minimal counterexample to
Tuza with mad<8. Such a graph has all proper subgraphs satisfying
Tuza, since mad<8 is subgraph-closed; Section 1 therefore forbids any
reducible set. Puleo's Lemma 2.6 gives robustness. The argument applies
to arbitrary such minimal counterexamples, not only graphs whose
degree-seven links belong to a previously classified family.
