TARGET AND EXACT TWO PATCHES.
Let u,v be nonadjacent vertices of degrees five and six, with
N(u) intersect N(v)=C={a,b,c,d}, inducing diamond K4-ab.
Write N(u)\C={p}, N(v)\C={q,r}.

In a robust graph with no reducible set, Puleo 2.7(a) says each
low-degree link complement is a matching and cannot have two edges.
Since the u-link already misses ab, it is exactly K5-ab, so p is
complete to C.

The v-link is either K6-ab (non-thin), or K6 minus a perfect matching
of size three (thin). In the thin case, one missing edge is ab; the
other two form a perfect matching on {c,d,q,r}. The option {cd,qr}
is impossible because cd belongs to the common diamond. After
interchanging q,r, the missing edges therefore are ab,cq,dr.
In particular qr exists in BOTH cases. The private cross edges pq,pr
are arbitrary and will not be used.

UNIFIED SIX-TRIANGLE CERTIFICATE.
Take
  S={uac,ubd,vad,vbc,vqr,pcd},
  X=E(G[C]) union {pc,pd,qr,up,vq,vr}.
The two u-triangles use one perfect matching of the four-cycle
a-c-b-d-a, and the two v-triangles use its other perfect matching.
The triangle pcd uses the remaining common edge cd and two p-spokes.
The triangle vqr uses the two remaining v-spokes and qr.
Thus all six triangles are edge-disjoint. The set X has eleven edges.

All packed edges with endpoints outside {u,v} belong to X. Every
triangle through u either contains p and meets up, or uses a common
edge in E(G[C]). Every triangle through v either contains q or r and
meets vq or vr, or uses a common edge. Thus X covers all ambient
triangles through either center and |X|=11<=12=2|S|.

This is a genuine reduction of {u,v}, valid for arbitrary ambient
edges outside their fixed neighborhoods; it does not just verify
Tuza on the nine-vertex patch. It treats thin and non-thin together.

POSSIBLE SYMBOLIC FAMILY, TO BE STATED PRECISELY IN THE FULL PROOF.
The same S works when C is a chosen common diamond, p is any neighbor
of u outside C adjacent to c,d, and q,r are adjacent neighbors of v
outside C. Cover ALL center edges leaving C, together with
E(G[C]) and pc,pd,qr. For nonadjacent u,v the cover size is exactly
d(u)+d(v), so degree sum at most twelve suffices. The vertices p,q,r
need not be pairwise private; p may equal q or r without causing an
edge collision. For a clean first theorem they may be required
distinct, since the intended codegree-four corollary has that property.

Combining the exact degree-five/six diamond exclusion with the reviewed
round-seven common-K4 exclusion should imply that, in any robust graph
with no reducible set, a degree-five vertex and a degree-six vertex
have at most three common neighbors. Full hypotheses and the ambient
induction bridge remain to be written and adversarially checked.
