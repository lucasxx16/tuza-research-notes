COMPLETE HUMAN PROOF FOR THE ORIGINAL DIAMOND CORE

Status: reviewed census-free research proof; see C/review_E_full.txt.
The hypotheses are connected
non-WKE links and the original common-vertex degree budget. Neither
saved-record coverage nor any numerical search is a premise.

1. SETUP AND RESULT

Let u,v be adjacent degree-seven vertices in a finite simple graph.
Their common neighborhood is C={x,y,p,q}; their private pairs are
A={a,b} at u and B={c,d} at v. Thus

  N(u)={v} union C union A,
  N(v)={u} union C union B.

The induced core H=G[C] is the diamond K4-xy: x,y are its degree-two
vertices, and p,q its degree-three vertices. Edges between A and B,
and edges leading away from this neighborhood, are unrestricted.

A graph F is WKE if a matching M and a vertex set Q satisfy
|Q|<=|M| and every edge of F with both endpoints outside Q belongs
to M. Assume:

  (A1) Both induced links G[N(u)] and G[N(v)] are connected and
       not WKE.
  (A2) For each t in C,
       deg_H(t)+|N(t) intersect A|+|N(t) intersect B|<=5.

Theorem. Under (A1) and (A2), the pair {u,v} is reducible: there
exist an edge-disjoint triangle packing S and an edge set X such
that |X|<=2|S|, X meets every triangle containing u or v, and X
contains every packed edge whose endpoints both avoid {u,v}.

The proof gives five explicit sufficient recipes, then derives an
exhaustive disjunction of their hypotheses from (A1) and (A2).
No bound on a private vertex's ambient degree is used.

2. CONNECTED NON-WKE LINKS FORCE FULL CORE SUPPORT

In this section the core C is any four-set inducing a graph with
a perfect matching. The seven-vertex link F has vertex set
{z} union C union {a,b}; z is adjacent to all of C and to neither
a nor b. The only other edges are core edges, private-core edges,
and the optional edge ab. The support is the set of core vertices
with a neighbor in {a,b}.

Lemma S. If F is connected and not WKE, its support is all of C.

Proof. First recall the support-size lemma M from
compression/round3/E/path_core.txt: under precisely these hypotheses,
the support has size at least three. Here is its short proof, to
make the present argument self-contained. If support lies in a
two-set T={s,t}, put Q={z,s,t}. Outside Q the only possible edges
are e, the core edge on C\T, and ab. A three-edge matching containing
every such edge that exists can be chosen as follows:

  e and ab present: {e,ab,zs};
  e present, ab absent: {e,sa,zt}, after relabeling T and the
      privates using a private-core edge supplied by connectivity;
  e absent, ab present: a perfect matching of H together with ab;
  both absent: choose a private-core edge ra, a perfect-matching
      edge f avoiding r, and let s' be the core vertex outside
      {r} union V(f); use {ra,f,zs'}.

These are WKE witnesses, proving support size at least three.

Now suppose t in C is unsupported, and set Q=C\{t}, of size three.
Outside Q the only edges are zt and possibly ab. If ab is present,
take M={zt,ab,e}, where e is the edge of a core perfect matching
avoiding t. This is a three-edge matching containing every edge
outside Q.

If ab is absent, connectivity gives each private vertex a nonempty
core neighborhood, necessarily in Q. These neighborhoods admit a
matching saturating {a,b} unless both are the same singleton:
indeed, if one has two choices, choose one distinct from any fixed
neighbor of the other. The singleton alternative contradicts the
support-size lemma. Take such a two-edge attachment matching and
add zt. Again this is a three-edge matching containing all edges
outside Q. Thus F is WKE in either case, a contradiction. QED.

Apply Lemma S to each of the two links. The diamond has perfect
matching {xp,yq}. Consequently every core vertex has at least one
attachment on each side. By (A2):

  (i) Each of p and q has exactly one A-neighbor and exactly one
      B-neighbor.
  (ii) Each of x and y has at least one neighbor on each side,
       but cannot have two A-neighbors and two B-neighbors.

These are the only consequences of (A2) used below.

3. FIVE UNIVERSAL DIAMOND RECIPES

Throughout this section the setup of Section 1 holds, but (A1) and
(A2) are not needed. A triple denotes its triangle; a pair denotes
its edge. Unspecified adjacencies are arbitrary. Every recipe is
valid after exchanging u,A with v,B, permuting each private pair,
exchanging x,y, or exchanging p,q.

For coverage it is enough to check the two induced link graphs:
every triangle through one hub consists of that hub and an edge
of its link. A triangle through both hubs is uv together with a
core vertex. Thus the checks below cover every ambient hub triangle.

R1. Asymmetric leaf attachment (five triangles, ten edges).

Assume xa and yc are present, while ya and pa are absent. Set

  S={uvq,uyp,uxa,vyc,vpx},
  R={yp,xa,yc,px},
  X=R union {uv,uq,ub,vq,vc,vd}.

The single-hub triangles use disjoint spoke pairs at each hub and
four distinct rims, and neither uses a spoke to q. Hence S is an
edge-disjoint packing, |S|=5, and |X|=10. All its external edges
are in R. The edge uv covers all two-hub triangles. At u, after
deleting spokes uq,ub, the remaining rim vertices are x,y,p,a.
Their possible edges are xp,yp,xa, all in R; xy,ya,pa are absent.
At v, deleting vq,vc,vd leaves core vertices x,y,p, whose only
edges xp,yp are in R. This proves every required condition.

R2. Crossed attachment matchings (six triangles, at most twelve edges).

Assume xa,pb,yc,qd are present, while pa,xb,qc are absent. Set

  S={uxa,upb,uyq,vxp,vyc,vqd},
  R={xa,pb,yq,xp,yc,qd},
  X=R union ({ab} intersect E(G)) union {uy,uq,vx,vp,vd}.

At u the rim pairs are {x,a},{p,b},{y,q}; at v they are
{x,p},{y,c},{q,d}. Each list consists of disjoint pairs, and all
six rims are distinct. Hence |S|=6, |X|<=12, and every external
packed edge lies in X. The deleted core spokes uy,uq,vx,vp cover
all two-hub triangles. At u the remaining rim vertices are
x,p,a,b: the possible edges xp,xa,pb,ab are in X, and pa,xb
are absent. At v they are y,q,c: yq,yc are in X, and qc is absent.
Thus every hub triangle is covered.

R3. Two unused privates (five triangles, nine edges).

Assume ya and xc are present, and b and d have no neighbors in
{x,y,p}. Set

  S={uvq,uxp,uya,vxc,vyp},
  R={xp,ya,xc,yp},
  X=R union {uv,uq,ua,vq,vc}.

As in R1, the hub spoke pairs are disjoint, no single-hub triangle
uses q, and the four rims are distinct. Thus |S|=5 and |X|=9<=10;
all external packed edges are in R. The edge uv covers two-hub
triangles. At u the remaining rim vertices after deleting uq,ua
are x,y,p,b, and at v they are x,y,p,d. Their only possible edges
are xp,yp, both in R. This proves coverage.

R4. A private edge and an outside triangle (six triangles, twelve edges).

Assume xa,pa,ab,yc,qd are present, while xb,pb,qc are absent.
Set

  S={uvx,uyq,uab,vyc,vqd,xpa},
  R={xp,xa,pa,yq,yc,qd,ab},
  X=R union {uy,uq,vx,vp,vd}.

The u-only rim pairs {y,q},{a,b} avoid x; the v-only pairs
{y,c},{q,d} avoid x. Their rims are distinct. The three edges
xp,xa,pa of the outside triangle are distinct from these four
rims, and this triangle uses no hub edge. Hence |S|=6 and the
packing is edge-disjoint. We have |X|=12, and all external packed
edges lie in R. As in R2, the four core spokes cover every two-hub
triangle. At u the remaining rim vertices are x,p,a,b, whose
possible edges xp,xa,pa,ab lie in R because xb,pb are absent.
At v the remaining rim vertices are y,q,c, with edges yq,yc
in R and qc absent. This proves coverage.

R5. Two private edges and one private attached to both centers
    (seven triangles, fourteen edges).

Assume ab,cd,dp,dq are present. Set

  S={uxq,uyp,uab,vxp,vyq,vcd,dpq},
  R=E(H) union {ab,cd,dp,dq},
  X=R union {uv,ua,ub,vc,vd}.

At each hub the three rim pairs are disjoint. Their four core
rims xq,yp,xp,yq are distinct; dpq uses the fifth core edge pq
and the two distinct attachments dp,dq. The private rims ab,cd
are each used once. Thus |S|=7 and all triangles are edge-disjoint.
The set R has nine edges, so |X|=14; it contains all external
packed edges. A two-hub triangle meets uv. Every other hub triangle
either meets a deleted private spoke or has its rim in E(H).
This completes the check.

Provenance of the recipes. R1,R2,R3,R4 are direct specializations
of O4,O6,O9,O5, respectively, in
compression/templates/optimal_patterns.txt. They have been verified
explicitly here, so catalogue coverage is not an input. R5 is
Lemma K of mad8/round5/E/p3_plus_edge_complements.txt: its seven-
triangle pattern was discovered in that round's bounded search,
and its universal validity has a direct proof reproduced above.

4. THE STRUCTURAL DISJUNCTION

By Section 2, each center has a unique attachment on each side.
Call a side paired if the same private vertex is adjacent to p
and q, and split if different private vertices are adjacent to
these centers. In a paired side call the vertex adjacent to both
centers the dominant vertex and the other the weak vertex.
The weak vertex is adjacent to neither center.

First, if a private vertex is adjacent to exactly one of x,y and
misses at least one of p,q, apply R1. Indeed, relabel its adjacent
leaf x, its missed leaf y, and its missed center p. The opposite
side has a neighbor c of y by Lemma S, supplying the other R1
attachment. Exchange sides if necessary.

It remains to work under the following symmetry condition:

  (*) Every private vertex missing at least one center is adjacent
      to both leaves or to neither leaf.

On a split side, both private vertices miss a center. By (*) and
leaf support, either exactly one of them sees both leaves, or both
see both leaves. Call these sparse split and dense split, respectively.
On a paired side, its weak vertex sees both leaves or neither;
no such restriction has yet been imposed on its dominant vertex.

Case I. There is a sparse split side.

Exchange sides and relabel centers and privates so this side is A,
with pa,qb present, qa,pb absent, a adjacent to both leaves, and
b adjacent to neither. Let c be the unique B-neighbor of p, and
let d be the other B-vertex, so pd is absent.

If yd is present, apply R2 with the substitutions

  (R2 x,p,y,q,a,b,c,d)=(x,q,y,p,a,b,d,c).

Its required edges are xa,qb,yd,pc, and its absent crossed edges
are qa,xb,pd, all supplied above.

If yd is absent, d misses p and condition (*) forces xd absent.
Both unused privates b,d therefore miss the triple {x,y,p}.
The vertex a sees y, and c sees x because d does not and x is
supported on side B. Apply R3. This completes Case I regardless
of whether side B is paired or split and regardless of either
private edge.

Case II. No side is sparse split, but at least one side is split.

Exchange sides so A is dense split: pa,qb are present, qa,pb
absent, and both a,b see both leaves. By (ii), each leaf has
exactly one B-neighbor. Thus B cannot be dense split, and it is
not sparse split by the case assumption. It is paired. Write c
for its dominant vertex and d for its weak vertex.

If d sees both leaves, the unique B-neighbor condition forces
c to see neither leaf. Apply R2 after exchanging sides, using
on the first side the matching xd,pc and on the second side
the matching ya,qb. Its three crossed absences are pd,xc,qa.
Explicitly, R2's labels (u,v,x,p,y,q,a,b,c,d) are replaced by
(v,u,x,p,y,q,d,c,a,b). Thus the two crossed absences required
on its first side belong to paired B; only qa is required on
the dense split side A.

If d sees neither leaf, it has no core neighbor. Since it has
no edge to the opposite hub, connectedness of the link forces
cd present. Leaf support makes c adjacent to both leaves as
well as to both centers. Apply R4 after exchanging sides, with
outside triangle xpc, private edge cd, and opposite matching
ya,qb. The required absences xd,pd,qa hold. This completes Case II.

Case III. Both sides are paired.

Write a,c for the respective dominant vertices and b,d for their
weak partners. Thus a,c see p,q and b,d miss p,q. By (*), each
weak vertex sees both leaves or neither.

If neither weak vertex sees a leaf, each has no core neighbor;
connectedness forces ab and cd. Since c sees both centers,
apply R5 with its B-labels c,d exchanged.

If exactly one weak vertex sees neither leaf, exchange sides
so this vertex is b. Connectedness forces ab, and full support
makes a adjacent to every core vertex. The other weak vertex d
sees both leaves, while c sees both centers. Apply R4 with
outside triangle xpa, private edge ab, and opposite matching
yd,qc: its absent edges are xb,pb,qd. Equivalently exchange the
two B-labels in R4.

Finally, suppose b and d both see both leaves. By (ii), a and c
cannot both be adjacent to x. Exchange sides if necessary so
xa is absent. Apply R2 with first-side matching xb,pa and
second-side matching yd,qc. Its three absent crossed attachments
are pb,xa,qd. This completes Case III.

The cases exhaust all possibilities for the two sides after (*),
and the failure of (*) was covered by R1. Every branch supplies
one of the universally valid recipes from Section 3. This proves
the theorem.

5. SCOPE

This closes the entire diamond-core structural subproblem under
(A1) and (A2). It does not merely verify a subset of saved examples,
and its validity is independent of the number or completeness of
those examples. The degree budget is used only for center uniqueness
and to forbid simultaneous double support at a leaf. The non-WKE
hypothesis is used in Lemma S; connectedness is used there and when
a private vertex with no core neighbor must have a private partner.

The argument does not prove the remaining original core types and
does not settle mad(G)<8. In particular it makes no inference about
degree-five or degree-six vertices in a sparse minimal counterexample.
