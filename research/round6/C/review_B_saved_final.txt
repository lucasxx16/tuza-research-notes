Final bounded saved-record audit, 3 October 2026.

The first B_saved checker version used three invalid conditions (recorded in review_B_saved_initial.txt). I corrected it to require S triangles in the decoded graph, edge-disjointness, X subset E, all hub triangles hit, all S-edges avoiding both hubs in X, and |X|<=2|S|. It now treats 14=2*7 as tight and does not demand coverage of unrelated graph edges. It exits nonzero if expected counts or any literal check fail.

I wrote an independent standard-library verifier, C/check_diamond_transfer_literal.py. It reconstructs the graph directly from the published core/side bit orders and numeric vertex labels, separately tests the diamond degrees and Lemma-K attachment condition, compares the exact eligible record indices with B's export, and checks all seven certificate conditions for every exported witness. Three damaged-certificate controls per record test external-edge safety, graph membership, and packing disjointness. The run found 139 diamond records; 42 satisfy Lemma K up to roles; 84 lack at least one private-pair internal edge; 13 have both internal edges but no private vertex adjacent to both centers. All 42 exported 7/14 certificates passed, and all 126 negative controls were rejected. No original census/finder or new graph search ran.

These data validate the 42-record transfer and its literal certificates, not complete human coverage of the diamond core. The remaining 97 saved records are outside Lemma K's sufficient hypotheses. The 42 are a subset of the already counted 139 diamond records; combined with the previously settled five core types, this gives 574+42=616 saved records covered by those specified human recipes, not six wholly settled core types.

Subsequently E/diamond_core.txt gave a separate complete human proof of the entire diamond core using four additional recipes and an exhaustive structural disjunction. Its review is C/review_E_full.txt; the 42-record transfer check is supplementary, not its premise.
