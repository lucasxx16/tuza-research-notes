Botler--Fernandes--Gutiérrez Lemma 3.3 (arXiv:2002.07925v2): its degree-five

ROUND 6 / D
A TWELVE-VERTEX BARRIER TO THE EXISTING JOINT DEGREE CAPS

Status: reviewed research proof; see C/review_D_joint_caps.txt.
No novelty claim is made. No numerical search or computation is used.

PURPOSE AND SCOPE.
The conclusions of Puleo's Lemma 2.7(a)-(h), robustness, and independence
of degree-seven vertices do not by themselves force average degree at
least eight. This remains true when the only degree-seven link has
complement a matching, one of the previously treated link families.
The example below simultaneously has vertices of degrees five, six,
and seven, so it is an obstruction to combining these numerical caps
alone into a target-eight discharging proof.

This does NOT assert that the example has no reducible set. Puleo's
lemma assumes robustness and no reducible set and gives necessary
conclusions; satisfying its conclusions does not recover that premise.
The example is not a counterexample to Tuza's conjecture. A new joint
argument may exclude it by a reduction or use a stronger structural
condition. The statement is precisely about the insufficiency of the
listed necessary conditions.

1. LABELED GRAPH.

Let U={w,0,1,2,3,4,5,6}. On U take K8 minus edges 01 and 35.
Add four mutually nonadjacent vertices a,b,c,t, with neighborhoods
  N(a)={0,3,4,5,6},
  N(b)={0,1,2,5,6},
  N(c)={0,1,2,3,4},
  N(t)={1,2,3,4,5,6}.
There are no other edges. Denote this graph by J.

Its degree classes are
  degree 5: a,b,c;
  degree 6: t;
  degree 7: w;
  degree 9: 0,1,3,5;
  degree 10: 2,4,6.
In particular there are no degree-eight vertices. The degree sum is
  3*5 + 6 + 7 + 4*9 + 3*10 = 94,
so J has 47 edges and average degree 47/6<8.

2. MAXIMUM AVERAGE DEGREE IS STRICTLY BELOW EIGHT.

It suffices to check induced subgraphs, since removing edges cannot
increase average degree at fixed vertex set. Consider a nonempty
vertex set Z. Put s=|Z intersect U|, let r be the number of a,b,c in Z,
and let q be 1 if t lies in Z and 0 otherwise. Thus r<=3 and q<=1.

If s=0, J[Z] has no edges. If s=1, it has at most r+q edges, and
  8|Z|-2|E(J[Z])| >= 8+6(r+q)>0.

If 2<=s<=7, the crude bound
  |E(J[Z])| <= s(s-1)/2 + 5r + 6q
gives
  8|Z|-2|E(J[Z])| >= s(9-s)-2r-4q >=14-10>0.

If s=8, both omitted core edges are absent and every selected outside
vertex contributes its entire degree. Therefore
  8|Z|-2|E(J[Z])| = 12-2r-4q >=2>0.

Every nonempty subgraph consequently has average degree below eight,
and J is finite. Thus mad(J)<8.

3. ROBUSTNESS AND THE DEGREE-FIVE/SIX LINKS.

The four low-degree vertices form an independent set. Their induced
neighborhoods are
  J[N(a)] = K5 minus edge 35,
  J[N(b)] = K5 minus edge 01,
  J[N(c)] = K5 minus edge 01,
  J[N(t)] = K6 minus edge 35.
Each is connected and has at least five vertices. In particular all
low-link complements have maximum degree one and exactly one edge.
The degree-six vertex t is non-thin: its link complement does not
contain a matching of size three.

For any core vertex v in U, its core neighbors induce a connected
graph: this is K7 minus at most two edges when v=w,2,4,6, and a K6
minus at most one edge for v=0,1,3,5. Every outside vertex adjacent
to v has at least three neighbors in that core-neighbor set. Indeed,
its link is K5 or K6 minus one edge, and deleting v leaves at least
three of its other neighbors adjacent to v. Consequently J[N(v)] is
connected. It has at least seven vertices.

Thus J is robust: every component of every neighborhood has at least
five vertices. The degree-seven set consists of the single vertex w
and is independent. Moreover J[N(w)] is K7 minus 01 and 35, so its
complement is 2K2 plus three isolated vertices.

4. EXACT SUBSUMPTION AND DONOR COUNTS.

A vertex v subsumes x when N[v] contains N[x]. For the four low-degree
vertices, their subsuming neighbors are exactly
  a: {0,4,6};
  b: {2,5,6};
  c: {2,3,4};
  t: {1,2,4,6}.
These lists follow directly by taking the dominating vertices of
each low-degree link displayed in Section 3.

The degree-seven vertex w has no low-degree neighbor. No vertex of
degree at most eight subsumes a degree-five vertex; every listed
subsumer of a,b,c has degree nine or ten. No degree-seven vertex
subsumes a degree-six vertex, and there is no thin degree-six vertex.

Here is the complete low-neighbor and low-subsumer table for degrees
nine and ten. Letters in the third column are those subsumed.

  vertex  degree  low neighbors  low subsumed
     0       9      a,b,c           a
     1       9      b,c,t           t
     3       9      a,c,t           c
     5       9      a,b,t           b
     2      10      b,c,t           b,c,t
     4      10      a,c,t           a,c,t
     6      10      a,b,t           a,b,t

Each degree-nine vertex thus subsumes just one low-degree vertex,
strictly below the cap three; the implication concerning a vertex
subsuming three is vacuous. Each degree-ten vertex has three
low-degree neighbors, at most 10-6. The degree-seven vertex has zero
low-degree neighbors, and degree-nine vertices have three, at most
9-4. There are no degree-eight vertices to check.

Together with Section 3, these facts verify every conclusion of
Puleo's Lemma 2.7(a)-(h), not just its coarse degree bounds.

5. THE EARLIER CONNECTIVITY CONDITIONS ALSO HOLD.

The graph J is five-connected. Deleting at most four vertices leaves
at least four core vertices. They induce a complete graph minus at
most the matching {01,35}, which is connected. Every surviving outside
vertex retains at least one core neighbor, since its degree is at
least five. The entire remaining graph is therefore connected.

Moreover, every complete bipartition cut with at least two vertices
on each shore has at least ten edges. To see this, let one shore
contain s core vertices. If 2<=s<=6, the core alone contributes at least
  s(8-s)-2>=10
cut edges. If s=0, the shore contains at least two outside vertices;
they are independent and their degrees sum to at least ten.
If s=1, write its core vertex as v and its nonempty outside set as R.
The cut size is exactly
  d(v) + sum_{x in R}(d(x)-2*1[vx is an edge]) >=7+3=10.
The cases s=7 and s=8 follow by exchanging shores. Thus J meets the
previous necessary four-connectivity and nontrivial-small-cut bounds.
These checks do not assert that J is irreducible.

6. THE TARGET-EIGHT DEFICIT.

The total initial deficit below eight is
  3|V5|+2|V6|+|V7| = 9+2+1 = 12.
The total surplus at degrees at least nine is
  sum_{d>=9}(d-8)|Vd| = 4+6 = 10.
No conservative redistribution can make all vertices finish with
charge at least eight, since the total deficit exceeds the surplus
by two. This is consistent with the exact degree sum in Section 1.

This graph proves the logical barrier even without degree-eight
vertices. In a general graph they must still contribute zero, not
positive surplus, at target eight. Treating them correctly does not
by itself repair the insufficiency exposed here.

Consequently an argument for arbitrary minimal mad<8 counterexamples
must use some input beyond robustness, the exact conclusions of
Lemma 2.7(a)-(h), degree-seven independence, the previous connectivity
and small-cut bounds, and the condition that degree-seven link
complements are matchings. For example, it could
exploit additional restrictions coupling low-degree neighborhoods to
the selected degree-seven incidences, or exclude this overlap pattern
by a local reduction. This note does not claim that all previously
proved local reductions survive on J.
