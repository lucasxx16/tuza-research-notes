ROUND 11 / E
A MIXED COVER WITH ARBITRARY RED-BOOK PAGE COUNTS

Status: reviewed research proof; see
research/round11/C/review_E_mixed_cover_page_tails.txt.
No universal coefficient below 165/59 is proved here. The result is
an unconditional cover inequality under the exact RB premises, plus
explicit necessary page-tail conditions on any near-two example.
No enumeration or numerical output is used in the proof.

1. SCOPE AND NOTATION.

Let H be a finite simple red-blue graph in which EVERY triangle has
exactly one red edge. Let Q be a maximum triangle packing, |Q|=p>0,
and assume every red edge of a Q triangle is globally private: it
belongs to exactly one H triangle. Other red edges may have arbitrarily
many pages. The quantity tau(H) is an EDGE triangle transversal.

Write R_Q for the p packed red edges, R for all participating red
edges, r=|R|, and K=B(Q) for the 2p packed blue edges. For e in R let
  w(e)=the number of H triangles containing e.
Thus w(e)=1 for e in R_Q. All triangles on a fixed red edge form its
book, and w(e) is the page count.

The RB budget is r<=3p. The main cover theorem below does not require
that budget; its tail consequences state it explicitly. Selecting a
red edge into a cover costs one, regardless of its number of pages.
That whole-color option is kept throughout the proof. In particular
we never replace the maximum triangle packing by the ordinary matching
number of the blue-edge auxiliary graph.

For any chosen set D contained in R minus R_Q, put
  q=|D|,
  B_D=sum_{e in R minus (R_Q union D)} w(e).
The letter B_D denotes this number of pages, not a set of blue edges.

2. TWO ELEMENTARY COVER FACTS.

LEMMA 1.
K meets every triangle of H. For each Q triangle, at least one of its
two blue edges belongs to no triangle meeting K in exactly one edge.

Proof. A triangle meeting a packed red edge must be its unique Q page,
and hence meets K. Any other triangle missing K would be edge-disjoint
from Q and could augment it. Therefore K covers all triangles.

For the second claim, write a Q triangle as uvx with uv red. Suppose
T1=uxy and T2=vxz each meet K in exactly their displayed blue edge.
They use no packed red edge, by privacy. If T1 and T2 are edge-disjoint,
replace uvx by both, increasing Q. Otherwise their common edge forces
y=z=w and their shared edge is xw; the alternative identifications
would make one triangle equal to uvx, which meets K twice. If xw is
red, the edges uw,vw are blue and uvw is a second uv page. If xw is
blue, uw,vw are red and uvw is all red. Both contradict the assumptions.
This proves the claim. It is the single-contact lemma previously
reviewed in research/round10/E/red_budget_reduction.txt, Section 4.

LEMMA 2.
After deleting any D contained in R minus R_Q, Q remains a maximum
packing of H-D, and all its red edges remain private. The number of
remaining Q-external triangles is exactly B_D.

Proof. A Q triangle has only its own red edge, which is not deleted.
Thus every Q triangle survives. Deletion cannot increase nu, so Q
remains maximum. Its privacy is preserved. Deleting a red edge only
deletes its own pages, because every triangle has exactly one red edge.
This proves the page-count identity and also shows that the one-red
condition is preserved. In particular, no maximum-packing hypothesis
is lost when applying Lemma 1 to H-D.

3. THE MIXED-COVER THEOREM.

THEOREM 3.
With the assumptions and notation above, for every D contained in
R minus R_Q,
  tau(H)<=2p+|D|-p^2/(p+2B_D).                    (1)
Consequently the minimum of these explicit bounds over choices of D
is also a valid upper bound. Arbitrary page counts are allowed.

Proof. Work first in H-D, where Lemma 2 permits reuse of Q and K.
From each Q triangle select one blue edge having no single-contact
triangle, as supplied by Lemma 1. Let Z be the p selected blue edges.
The triangles of Q are edge-disjoint, so these p choices are distinct.

Form a simple graph F on the vertex set Z. Join two members of Z when
they are the two blue edges of a triangle of H-D. Two specified blue
edges can form at most one triangle in a simple graph, so there is no
multiplicity in F. A Q triangle contributes no F edge because only one
of its two blue edges was chosen into Z. Every F edge therefore comes
from a Q-external triangle, giving
  |E(F)|<=B_D.

There is an independent set I in F with
  |I|>=sum_{z in Z}1/(d_F(z)+1)
       >=p^2/(p+2|E(F)|)
       >=p^2/(p+2B_D).                            (2)
For completeness, order Z uniformly at random and keep each vertex
preceding all its neighbors. The resulting set is independent and
has the displayed expected size. Cauchy-Schwarz gives the middle
inequality, hence some order attains the stated bound.

The blue set K minus I covers every triangle of H-D. To check this
completely, every such triangle meets K by Lemma 1. If it meets K
once, that contact is outside Z by the way Z was chosen, and cannot
be removed. If it meets K twice, deleting both contacts would put
an F edge inside I, contrary to independence. A triangle has exactly
two blue edges, so these cases exhaust its possible contacts with K.
Thus K minus I is a cover of size at most 2p-p^2/(p+2B_D).
Add the q red edges of D to cover their deleted pages. This proves (1).

The choices of Z and I are made AFTER deleting D; no claim is made
that the same safe blue choices work for every D. The resulting cover
uses actual edges of H and meets every H triangle.

COROLLARY 4 (AN ADDITIVE GAP, NOT A UNIFORM FACTOR).
Every finite graph under these private-maximum-packing assumptions
satisfies
  tau(H)<=2p-1.                                  (3)

Proof. Use D empty in (1). Since p>0 and B_empty is finite, the saving
p^2/(p+2B_empty) is positive. Tau is an integer, giving (3).

This does not prove RB(epsilon) for any fixed epsilon. In a sequence
with p tending to infinity, the factor in (3) tends to two. It does
show that searching these exact premises for a finite tau=2p example
can never refute RB; a negative answer would need ratios tending to
two to rule out EVERY fixed positive epsilon, rather than a finite
equality example. A proposed particular epsilon can still have a
finite strict counterexample, subject to the next size restriction.

There is also an exact boundary for the specific candidate RB(1/100).
A strict counterexample would require
  199p/100<tau(H)<=2p-1,
so necessarily p>100. For example every graph on at most twelve
vertices has p<=floor(binomial(12,2)/3)=22, and therefore cannot be
such a strict counterexample under these premises. A negative result
from a small-graph search is thus not evidence for RB(1/100): in this
range a counterexample is already ruled out symbolically.

4. WHOLE-BOOK TRUNCATION AND NECESSARY TAILS.

Assume now r<=3p. Fix any integer L>=1 and define
  D_L={e in R minus R_Q: w(e)>L},
  f_L=|D_L|.
The page counts of these removed books have no upper bound.
All remaining Q-external red edges have at most L pages, so
  B_(D_L)<=L(r-p-f_L)<=L(2p-f_L).
Theorem 3 yields the sharper and weaker convenient forms
  tau(H)<=2p+f_L-p^2/[p+2L(r-p-f_L)]
         <=2p+f_L-p^2/[p+2L(2p-f_L)]             (4)
         <=(2-1/(4L+1))p+f_L.                    (5)
All denominators are positive: r-p-f_L is the number of remaining
Q-external participating red edges and is nonnegative.

Thus if, for some fixed L, a graph satisfies
  f_L<=p/[2(4L+1)],
then it has the explicit proportional saving
  tau(H)<=(2-1/[2(4L+1)])p.                      (6)
This allows an arbitrary number of pages on the f_L exceptional books.
It also allows all of the other Q-external red edges to be nonprivate;
the argument is not restricted to a small TOTAL number of nonprivate
red edges as in the earlier round-ten bound.

The corresponding necessary obstruction must be stated conditionally.
If this H additionally satisfies
  tau(H)>=(2-delta)p
for a specified delta>=0, then (5) forces, for EVERY integer L>=1,
  f_L/p>=1/(4L+1)-delta.                         (7)
A negative right side is simply vacuous. The sharper (4), with
x=f_L/p in [0,2], requires
  x+delta>=1/(1+4L-2Lx).                         (8)
These are necessary page-tail restrictions on a near-two example;
they are not unconditional lower bounds on f_L for arbitrary H.

At the formal old critical cover value tau(H)=2p, (8) reads
  x(1+4L-2Lx)>=1,
which requires
  x>=[4L+1-sqrt((4L+1)^2-8L)]/(4L).             (9)
The larger root is greater than two, so (9) is the relevant root on
the actual domain [0,2]. For L=1 it is exactly
  (5-sqrt(17))/4,
recovering the previously reviewed nonprivate-red threshold. The
new statements for L>=2 distinguish books of different sizes rather
than merely recording whether they are private.

For finite H, condition tau(H)=2p is already impossible by (3). The
point of (7)-(9) is quantitative and asymptotic: any sequence under
the RB budget whose ratios tend to two must retain a positive density
of books above EVERY fixed page threshold L. For example (7) gives
liminf f_L/p>=1/(4L+1) for each fixed L along such a sequence. No upper
bound contradicting this requirement is proved here.

5. A SORTED-PAGE FORM AND THE PRECISE REMAINING QUESTION.

List the page counts outside Q in nonincreasing order as
  w_1>=w_2>=...>=w_(r-p).
For q between zero and r-p, choose D to contain the first q red edges.
Theorem 3 then gives
  tau(H)<=2p+q-p^2/[p+2 sum_{i>q} w_i].           (10)
If tau(H)>=(2-delta)p, rearranging (10) gives, whenever q+delta*p>0,
  sum_{i>q} w_i >= [p^2/(q+delta*p)-p]/2.         (11)
When q+delta*p=0 the asserted near-two lower bound is impossible,
as the positive saving in (1) shows. Negative lower bounds in (11)
are allowed but carry no information.

This is an explicit mixed-cover choice for every red-book length
distribution, not a substitution of ordinary matching for rainbow
matching. Its effectiveness depends on the page-count tail. The
glued family in multipage_matching_gap.txt illustrates why very long
books should sometimes be selected directly into the cover; the
cost is the number of such books, not their number of pages.

The earlier implication
  RB(epsilon) => tau(G)<=(165/59-epsilon/10000)nu(G)
for all finite simple G is already proved in
research/round10/E/red_budget_reduction.txt, Section 5. This file
does not establish its premise. A remaining concrete route is to
prove, from H-realizability and maximum packing, a uniform tail
restriction strong enough for (4) or (10) to save a fixed fraction.
The red-edge count r<=3p alone has not been shown to supply that
restriction. We do not assume one without proof.

EXACT CONDITIONAL UNIVERSAL COEFFICIENT.
Suppose that there exists an integer L>=1, independent of the graph,
such that EVERY graph satisfying the RB premises has
  f_L<=p/[2(4L+1)].
Then (6) proves RB(epsilon) with epsilon=1/[2(4L+1)]. The reviewed
round-ten bridge consequently gives, for every finite simple G,
  tau(G)<=[165/59-1/(20000(4L+1))]nu(G).          (12)
The tail hypothesis in this paragraph is not proved here. A single
H satisfying it gives only the local cover (6), not the universal
conclusion (12). For L>=1, the counted heavy red edges cannot belong
to any all-private Q, so f_L does not depend on which such Q is chosen.

6. A DIRECT GLOBAL CONSEQUENCE: WANG'S EXACT RATIO IS NOT ATTAINED.

THEOREM 5.
Every finite simple graph G with n=nu(G)>0 satisfies
  59 tau(G)<=165n-1.                             (13)
This is an additive strictness statement, not a uniform improvement
of the coefficient 165/59.

Proof. Use Wang's packings and notation a,b,c,d,h,e as recorded in
research/round10/E/red_budget_reduction.txt, Section 1. Its residual
H has exactly one red edge in every triangle, and its maximum packing
Q has size c with h private red edges. The imported covers W1-W5 and
their exact retained-slack consequence give
  59 tau(G)<=165n-93e-d-3(c-h-6d)_+.
Assume for contradiction that 59 tau(G)=165n. All quantities subtracted
on the right are nonnegative, so e=d=0 and c=h. Let g_i>=0 denote
the right side of W_i minus tau(G), for i=1,2,3,4. The established
weighted identity gives
  20g1+8g2+19g3+12g4=165n-59tau(G)-93e-d=0.
Every g_i is therefore zero. Equality in W3 gives
  c=3n-tau(G)=12n/59>0.

The residual maximum packing has h=c, so all its red edges are private.
Corollary 4 applies without any bound on r and yields
  tau(H)<=2c-1.
The direct outer cover and equality in W4 now give
  tau(G)<=3(n-e)-b+tau(H)
         <=3n-b+2c-1
         =tau(G)-1,
a contradiction. Thus 59tau(G)<165n; the two sides are integers,
which proves (13).

Equivalently tau(G)<=floor((165nu(G)-1)/59) for nu(G)>0. The ordinary
integrality rounding already makes Wang's ratio strict unless n is a
multiple of 59; the argument above also rules out equality in that
remaining congruence class. It does not give a fixed delta>0 with
tau(G)<=(165/59-delta)nu(G). Taking many disjoint copies of a graph
does not turn an additive one into such a uniform delta, since the
normalized saving in (13) tends to zero as n grows.

No current independent D-route material was read in developing this
argument. The only imported lemma is the already reviewed round-ten
single-contact exchange, reproved above for completeness, together
with the explicitly cited Wang construction and retained-slack
identity in the global corollaries.
