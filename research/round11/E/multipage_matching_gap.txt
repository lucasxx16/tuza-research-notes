ROUND 11 / E
ONE MULTIPAGE RED EDGE CAN CAUSE AN UNBOUNDED ORDINARY-MATCHING GAP

Status: reviewed research proof; see
research/round11/C/review_E_multipage_matching_gap.txt.
This is a counterexample to a proposed intermediate simplification,
not a counterexample to RB(epsilon), and not an improved universal
Tuza constant. No numerical result is a premise of the proof.

1. THE INTERMEDIATE CLAIM BEING TESTED.

Let H be a finite simple red-blue graph in which every triangle has
exactly one red edge. Its blue-edge auxiliary graph J has the blue
edges of H as vertices, with one J edge for every H triangle joining
its two blue edges. Color that J edge by the red edge of its triangle.

J is triangle-free and each color class is a matching. Triangle
packings in H correspond exactly to rainbow matchings in J. A triangle
cover in H corresponds to a mixed cover of J: one may select a J vertex
(a blue H edge) or an entire color class (a red H edge). A red edge is
private precisely when its auxiliary color is a singleton.

The RB hypothesis asserts that p=nu(H)>0, the number r of participating
red edges satisfies r<=3p, and there EXISTS a maximum packing whose
red edges are all globally private. It does not say that every maximum
packing has this property.

A suggested simplification was that these H-realizable hypotheses
might force mu(J)=p, allowing ordinary matching arguments directly.
The family below disproves this. More strongly, if s is the number
of globally nonprivate participating red edges, there is no fixed
constant C for which those hypotheses force mu(J)<=p+C*s.

2. THE GRAPH FAMILY.

Fix integers k>=4 and t>=1. Start with t blocks. Block j has vertices
  x(j,0), x(j,1), ..., x(j,2k-1), y(j).
Identify all x(j,0) as one vertex u and all x(j,2) as one vertex v.
Apart from these two common vertices, all block vertices are distinct.

In each block put in the following BLUE edges:
  x(j,0)x(j,1), x(j,1)x(j,2), ...,
  x(j,2k-2)x(j,2k-1), x(j,2k-1)x(j,0),
  x(j,0)y(j), y(j)x(j,2).
Thus its blue graph is a cycle C_(2k) with an additional length-two
path from u to v. The two-edge path through x(j,1) is already on the
cycle. There are no further blue edges.

In each block put in the following RED edges:
  x(j,0)x(j,2), x(j,2)x(j,4), ...,
  x(j,2k-4)x(j,2k-2), x(j,2k-2)x(j,0),
and
  x(j,1)x(j,3), x(j,3)x(j,5), ...,
  x(j,2k-3)x(j,2k-1), x(j,2k-1)y(j).
The first row is a k-cycle on its even-indexed vertices. The second
is a path on its odd-indexed vertices followed by y(j).
The edge uv=x(j,0)x(j,2) is the same single red edge in every block.
There are no further red edges. Call the resulting graph H_(k,t).
Its blue graph, and hence H_(k,t), is connected: each block's blue
graph is connected and all blocks contain u and v. Its order is
2+(2k-1)*t.

3. ALL TRIANGLES AND THE AUXILIARY GRAPH.

LEMMA 1.
Every triangle of H_(k,t) has exactly one red edge. Its auxiliary graph
J is the disjoint union of t paths, each on 2k+2 vertices. In each path
the first and last edges have the common color uv; every other auxiliary
edge has its own singleton color, distinct also between blocks.

Proof. Partition the H vertices into the even-indexed vertices, and
the odd-indexed vertices together with all y(j). Blue edges go between
the parts, and red edges remain within a part. Thus an all-blue triangle
and a triangle with two red edges are impossible.

The red graph on the second part is a disjoint union of paths. On the
first part it consists of k-cycles sharing just the edge uv. A red
triangle contained in a block would be a triangle of a cycle of length
k>=4, which is impossible. A triangle meeting different blocks would
need an edge between their distinct internal vertices, and no such
edge exists. There is therefore no all-red triangle either.

It remains to identify the full set of one-red triangles, not an
arbitrarily chosen subfamily. For red uv the common blue neighbors
are exactly
  x(j,1), y(j), for j=1,...,t.
For every other red edge on a block's even k-cycle, its common blue
neighbor is exactly the odd-indexed vertex between its endpoints on
the original blue cycle. For a red edge x(j,2i+1)x(j,2i+3) on the odd
path, its unique common blue neighbor is x(j,2i+2). Finally the red
edge x(j,2k-1)y(j) has exactly one common blue neighbor, u.
These assertions follow immediately from the listed blue neighborhoods;
identifying u and v does not give a block's internal vertex any new
blue neighbor outside that block. In particular every red edge except
uv is globally private, and uv has exactly 2t pages.

For an explicit auxiliary path ordering, label the blue edges in block j
as
  b(j,i)=x(j,i)x(j,i+1), 0<=i<=2k-2;
  b(j,2k-1)=x(j,2k-1)u;
  b(j,2k)=uy(j);
  b(j,2k+1)=y(j)v.
All these blue edges are distinct, also across different blocks.
The triangles listed above give exactly the auxiliary edges
  b(j,i)b(j,i+1), 0<=i<=2k.
The edges with indices i=0 and i=2k have color uv. Every other edge
has a distinct private red color. There are no auxiliary edges between
blocks, since the full list of H triangles contains no triangle whose
two blue edges come from different blocks. This proves the lemma.

4. EXACT PACKING, COVER, AND EDGE PARAMETERS.

THEOREM 2.
For H=H_(k,t), with k>=4 and t>=1,
  p=nu(H)=k*t,
  tau(H)=k*t+1,
  mu(J)=(k+1)*t,
  r=1+(2k-1)*t<=3p,
  s=1.
There is a maximum triangle packing of size p whose red edges are all
globally private. In particular H satisfies every stated premise of RB.

Proof of packing assertions. In each auxiliary path, take the edges
with odd indices
  i=1,3,...,2k-1.
They form a matching of size k and all have singleton colors. Their
union over blocks is a rainbow matching of size k*t, corresponding
to a private-red H packing Q.

A path on 2k+2 vertices has matching number k+1 and a unique perfect
matching, namely its edges with indices 0,2,...,2k. Uniqueness follows
by taking the only edge incident to the first endpoint and proceeding
inductively down the path. This perfect matching includes both its
first and last edges, whose common color is uv. Thus no rainbow
matching uses k+1 edges within any component. Every rainbow matching
has at most k edges per component and at most k*t edges in total.
This proves nu(H)=k*t, while ordinary matchings give mu(J)=(k+1)*t.

Proof of red-edge counts. Each block has 2k red edges. Only uv is shared
between blocks, so r=1+(2k-1)*t. Every listed red edge participates in
the triangles in Lemma 1. Only uv is nonprivate, giving s=1. Moreover
  3p-r=(k+1)*t-1>=0.

Proof of cover assertions. Select the single red edge uv. This covers
the two end triangles of every auxiliary path. The remaining triangles
form, in each block, the auxiliary path on vertices
  b(j,1),...,b(j,2k).
It has a vertex cover of size k, obtained by taking alternating path
vertices. These k*t blue edges, together with uv, give an H triangle
cover of size k*t+1.

Conversely let X be any H triangle cover. Nonparticipating edges can be
discarded. Replace every selected red edge other than uv by either blue
edge of its unique triangle. This does not increase the size and keeps
a cover. If uv is not selected, the resulting all-blue cover is a vertex
cover of J, and has size at least (k+1)*t, by its disjoint ordinary
matching of that size. Since (k+1)*t>=k*t+1, this is sufficient.

If uv is selected, the remaining selected blue edges must cover every
triangle with auxiliary index 1,...,2k-1 in every block. Those remaining
paths contain the k*t disjoint auxiliary edges of Q. Hence at least
k*t blue edges are required in addition to uv. The total size is again
at least k*t+1. This proves tau(H)=k*t+1.

5. THE ROUTE KILLED, AND WHAT REMAINS OPEN.

For fixed k=4, the graphs have
  p=4t, mu(J)=5t, r=7t+1<=12t, s=1, tau(H)=4t+1.
Thus mu(J)-p=t is unbounded even though there is just one nonprivate
red edge and a maximum all-private packing exists. No inequality
  mu(J)<=p+C*s
with fixed C can hold under the RB premises. Even the relative gap
mu(J)/p=5/4 remains separated from one.

The single-block case k=4,t=1 has nine vertices, p=4, mu(J)=5, r=8,
and tau(H)=5. The construction and its complete triangle list supply
a literal small H-realizable witness; this is not an abstract colored-J
example whose graph realization is unknown.

The mechanism is exact: every ordinary augmenting component requires
the same red spine twice. Rainbow matching cannot perform that
augmentation, while selecting the spine as one cover edge resolves
all those components at once. Any further argument using J must keep
track of whole-color cover choices, not simply substitute its ordinary
matching number for the triangle packing number.

This family does NOT refute RB(epsilon) for a useful small epsilon:
its cover/packing ratio is 1+1/(k*t), at most 5/4. It also does not
prove RB for other graphs, and no smaller universal Tuza coefficient
is obtained in this file. Its contribution is the exact negative
verdict on the ordinary-matching simplification, including any bounded
error proportional only to the number of nonprivate red edges.

The active RB statement and its previously reviewed conditional
implication for a universal coefficient are recorded in
  research/round10/E/red_budget_reduction.txt, Section 5.
That conditional implication remains valid; its hypothesis remains
unproved. No current independent D-route material was read in deriving
this family.
