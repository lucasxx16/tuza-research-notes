Verdict: accepted as a counterexample to the ordinary-matching shortcut, not to RB(epsilon).

The two parity parts make blue edges cross and red edges internal; k>=4 prevents a red triangle. Listing the common blue neighbors of each red edge gives exactly t disjoint auxiliary paths P_(2k+2). The shared red uv colors precisely the two terminal edges of every path; all other colors are singleton. The odd-index auxiliary edges give a globally private rainbow matching of size kt. A component matching of k+1 is its unique perfect matching and uses uv twice, so no rainbow matching exceeds k per component. Thus nu(H)=kt while mu(J)=(k+1)t. The mixed-cover proof correctly gives tau(H)=kt+1, and r=1+(2k-1)t<=3kt with exactly one nonprivate red edge. Identifying the two vertices across blocks introduces no cross-block triangle. Consequently mu(J)-nu(H)=t with s=1, disproving every fixed additive Cs shortcut. The ratio tau/nu=1+1/(kt) leaves RB(epsilon) open.
