1. SETUP AND THE REPLACEMENT HALL CONDITION.

Use Wang's construction in an arbitrary finite simple graph G.
Let P be a maximum triangle packing, n=|P|, and O=E(P). Edges in O
are old, all others new. Each old edge has the unique label of the
triangle of P containing it. A triangle's type is its number of old
edges. Let A be a maximum type-one packing, a=|A|, and G'=G-E(A).
Choose P' in G' by maximizing first its number b of type-two members,
and then its total size m. Write B* for its type-two members, and
T=P'\B* for its type-three members.

The family S consists of type-two triangles remaining after deletion
of all old edges of P'. Let Q be a maximum packing of S, c=|Q|.
Each member of Q shares its unique new edge with exactly one member
of B*. These paired triangles form two-page books with that new edge
as spine; the other B* members are one-page books. Write N for the
b new edges of B*. The old edges of Q avoid all edges of P' and A.

LEMMA 1 (REPLACEMENT HALL).
Let R be any edge-disjoint triangle packing in G. Join each triangle
of R to every triangle of P with which it shares an edge. For every
subset R0 of R,
  |R0| <= number of its P-neighbors.
Consequently this bipartite incidence graph has a matching assigning
distinct P-triangles to all members of R.

Proof. If R0 had fewer P-neighbors than members, remove those
P-neighbors from P and insert all of R0. The inserted triangles are
pairwise edge-disjoint, and have no edge in any retained P-triangle
by definition of the neighbor set. This enlarges P, a contradiction.
The final assertion is Hall's marriage theorem applied to these
inequalities. The inequalities themselves suffice for every use below.

2. A MARKED PSEUDOFOREST FOR ANY TYPE-TWO PACKING.

Every type-two triangle uses old edges with two different labels.
Indeed, two edges belonging to the same original packed triangle
would force their third closing edge to belong to that same old
triangle, contradicting type two.

The old-edge labels of A are distinct. If two members of A had the
same label, they could replace the corresponding member of P and
enlarge P: their other edges are new and A is edge-disjoint.

Let B be any edge-disjoint type-two packing avoiding E(A). Define
a loopless multigraph L_B on the n members of P. A member of B
gives an edge between its two old-edge labels. Mark the a vertices
that supply the old edges of A. Multiple edges are allowed; marks
are distinct.

LEMMA 2 (MARKED COMPONENT BUDGET).
For every connected component K of L_B,
  |E(K)| + number of marked vertices in K <= |V(K)|.
Hence K is either a tree with at most one mark or an unmarked
unicyclic multigraph. The statement includes isolated vertices.

Proof. Take all B-triangles represented by edges of K and all
A-triangles whose old-edge labels lie in K. This is a packing:
B and A avoid each other's edges. Its old edges have labels in K,
and its new edges avoid P. Replacing the |V(K)| original P-triangles
by this collection proves the displayed inequality. A connected
loopless multigraph has at least |V(K)|-1 edges. The inequality
therefore leaves exactly the asserted two possibilities.

Since the graph has n vertices and |B| edges, it has exactly n-|B|
tree components. Precisely a of them contain a mark. Thus the number
of unmarked tree components is exactly n-a-|B|. For B=B*, Candidate
in Section 4 is equivalently the assertion that c does not exceed
this number. The actual witness in actual_counterexample.txt has
zero unmarked trees and c=1, so this charging fails for legal choices.

For Wang's B*/Q books, this lemma applies to EVERY selection of
one page per book. Their supports are edge-disjoint, and each page
avoids A. Also, the fixed type-three packing T may be retained:
the chosen pages avoid its old edges, while their new edges belong
to N and cannot belong to T. Thus A, T, and all selected pages form
a packing. Lemma 1 applies to every such choice, with T included.

In particular, if a+m=n, each such selection together with A and T
has a perfect assignment to all n original P-triangles. This is a
necessary structural condition, not an additional packing beyond n.

3. BOOKS THAT ACTUALLY PAY THE OPPOSITE-EDGE COST.

Color new edges red and old edges blue. Let h be the number of
Q-members whose red edge is private in S, as in Wang. Define k to
be the number of paired B*/Q books whose opposite edge exists in
G' and is a new edge outside N.

LEMMA 3 (ACTUAL BOOK COUNT).
One has k<=h, and Wang's random-bipartition cover satisfies the
sharpened parameter estimate
  tau(G)<=3n/2+5a/2+3b/2-c+3k/8.

Proof. A counted book has pages uvx in B* and uvy in Q, spine uv
new, and opposite edge xy new outside N. Wang's exchange Lemma 2.1
applies because Q's old edges avoid P', hence avoid the old edges
of B*. It makes uv private in the blue-deleted residual family of
B*, and therefore in its subfamily S. Thus that Q-member is counted
by h, proving k<=h.

In Wang's random-bipartition construction, every added new edge
outside N must be the opposite edge of such a paired book. If a book
has no new opposite edge outside N, it contributes no such edge.
For each counted book the addition event requires x,y to have the
same bit and at least one of u,v the other bit, a probability 3/8.
The union bound therefore uses k rather than h. All the other terms
of that construction are unchanged, giving the displayed estimate.
Repeated opposite edges can only reduce the union-bound cost; no
distinctness assumption on them is needed for this inequality.

Consequently the retained-slack identity from round ten sharpens to
  59 tau(G) <= 165n - 93(n-a-m) - d
              - 3(c-h-6d)_+ - 3(h-k),
where d is the maximum residual packing size after also deleting the
old edges of Q. This follows from the same proof and weights because
the random-cover bound has coefficient eight, and 8*(3/8)=3.
This is additional explicit slack, not a new uniform constant.

LEMMA 4 (FOUR DISTINCT ORIGINAL LABELS).
The four old edges of every counted book have four different labels
in P.

Proof. Its induced four-vertex graph has old cycle edges ux,vx,uy,vy
and new diagonals uv,xy. Adjacent pairs of those old cycle edges
have their third closing edge equal to one of the new diagonals,
so they cannot belong to a common original P-triangle. Opposite
cycle edges are vertex-disjoint and also cannot lie in one triangle.
These exhaust all pairs of cycle edges.

If the opposite edges of some collection of counted books are
pairwise distinct, their complete K4 supports are pairwise
edge-disjoint. Their old cycle supports already are edge-disjoint
by the book construction; their distinct spines lie in N; their
distinct opposite edges lie outside N and are new. These supports
also avoid A, T, and the members of P' outside their own books.
Each of the four faces of one such K4 is type two. Therefore every
selection of ANY one of its four faces, independently in all such
books and with the other P' members retained, remains an admissible
packing together with A. Lemmas 1 and 2 apply to all these choices.

4. WHY LABEL CONDITIONS ALONE DO NOT COUNT ALL ALTERNATE PAGES.

A tempting additional inequality is
  a+b+c<=n.                                              (Candidate)
The assertion that it holds for every legal choice is false, as
proved without computed optima in actual_counterexample.txt. Whether a favorable
choice is always available remains open here. The following exact
abstract system, recorded before that graph witness, also shows
that the marked-pseudoforest conditions and old-edge slot counts
alone are insufficient to derive it, even allowing four choices in
a book. This is NOT asserted to come from an actual simple graph.

Take four original labels {1,2,3,4}, no marks, and three fixed
type-two label edges
  23,34,41.
They form a tree. Add one book whose main and alternate label edges
are 12 and 34. Allow its other two faces to have label edges 13 and
24; these four alternatives form the cycle 1-2-4-3-1. Any selection
of one alternative, together with the fixed three edges, is a
connected four-vertex graph with four edges, hence unicyclic.
This includes the choice 34 parallel to the fixed edge 34.
There are no marks, so every component budget in Lemma 2 holds.

The complete book uses one old-edge slot at each original label.
The fixed three label edges use additional slots of multiplicities
(1,1,2,2). Thus the total old-slot usage is
  (2,2,3,3),
which does not exceed the three edges available in each original
triangle. The book's main and alternate pages have disjoint label
pairs, as required by Lemma 4. Nevertheless its formal counts are
  n=4, a=0, b=4, c=1,
and a+b+c=5>4.

The full original graph has additional geometric consistency:
its labels represent actual triangles sharing actual vertices, and
old edges determine the closing edge of a triangle. That information
is absent from this abstract system. It cannot be discarded in a
proof using the label route. The corrected delegated search later
supplied an ACTUAL graph with a fully legal Wang packing sequence
violating Candidate. Its hand proof is in actual_counterexample.txt;
the graph has (n,a,b,m,c)=(4,1,3,3,1).

5. EXACT CONDITIONAL PAYOFF, NOT A UNIVERSAL CLAIM.

Suppose Candidate could be established for a choice of Wang's
packings available in every graph. Weight Wang's four final cover
inequalities by
  116,48,102,68,
respectively. Add four times 0<=n-a-b-c and once 0<=c-h. The a,b,c,d,h
coefficients cancel. Explicitly they are respectively
  -116+120-4=0,
  72-68-4=0,
  -48-102+153-4+1=0,
  -102+102=0,
  18-17-1=0.
The weights sum to 334 and the n-coefficient is
  348+72+306+204+4=934.
Thus the conditional conclusion would be
  tau(G)<=(467/167)nu(G),
and 165/59-467/167=2/9853>0.

The actual seven-vertex counterexample refutes the all-choices
version of Candidate. It does not refute the version allowing
optimized choices, which is the premise of the calculation above.
There is no unconditional improvement of 165/59 in this file.
