ROUND 2 / E: TWO MATCHINGS, CORE SUBSETS, AND TRIANGLE ABSORPTION
Status: rigorous reusable sufficient theorem and witness normalization;
catalogue coverage statistics are recorded separately when verified.

1. SCOPE AND WHAT A TEMPLATE MEANS

The target is the source's codegree-four reducible-pair statement, not
the equality example settled in round 1. Write u,v for the hubs,
C=N(u) intersect N(v), A=N(u) minus ({v} union C), and
B=N(v) minus ({u} union C). At degree seven and codegree four,
|C|=4 and |A|=|B|=2. Let L be the ambient induced graph on
{u,v} union C union A union B with A--B edges omitted. Put
H=L[C], J_u=L[C union A], J_v=L[C union B], and J=L[C union A union B].
Every ambient triangle through u or v is in L.

The source's admissible local graphs also have connected non-WKE links
and satisfy the common-vertex degree budget. Our sufficient theorems do
not require those extra hypotheses. Proving that all admissible local
graphs satisfy a sufficient condition is separate from proving that
condition sufficient, and separate from scanning the 1144 saved records.

A template here is a fixed algebraic recipe whose free data are two
matchings, an optional distinguished core vertex, a prescribed bounded
number of hub-free triangle motifs, and optionally a subset of the six
core edges. Its cover is derived from at most sixteen subsets of the
four-element core by an explicit formula. It may not accept a cover X
as input or look up an orbit key. Restricted subfamilies specify permitted
numbers/types of hub-free motifs and permitted extra core deletions.
Examples are the subfamilies with no hub-free motif or at most one.

The theorem below proves sufficiency for this grammar. We do not claim
its number of recipe names is globally minimal: arbitrary unions can
be renamed as one, making unrestricted minimality vacuous. A genuine
minimality claim requires a fixed allowed family and a lower bound.

2. THE RECIPE

Choose:
(a) epsilon in {0,1}, and x in C when epsilon=1;
(b) a matching M_u in J_u and a matching M_v in J_v, with disjoint edge
    sets; neither may use x when epsilon=1;
(c) a set T of t pairwise edge-disjoint triangles in J, with edges
    disjoint from M_u union M_v;
(d) a set D of r core edges disjoint from M_u union M_v union E(T).

For restricted catalogue templates we bound t and prescribe its motifs.
Write k=|M_u|+|M_v| and R=M_u union M_v union E(T) union D.
Then |R|=k+3t+r. Set H'=H-R, J'_u=J_u-R, and J'_v=J_v-R.

For a vertex cover U subset C of H', put
P_u(U)={a in A : ac is an edge of J'_u for some c in C minus U}.
Writing A={a_0,a_1}, let e_u(U)=1 if a_0a_1 survives in J'_u and
P_u(U) is empty, and e_u(U)=0 otherwise. Define
f_u(U)=|U|+|P_u(U)|+e_u(U).
Define f_v(V) analogously. Minima below range over core vertex covers
of H'; this collection is nonempty because C is one such cover.

Define
gamma_1(R)=1+min_U f_u(U)+min_V f_v(V),
gamma_0(R)=min_{U union V=C} (f_u(U)+f_v(V)),
gamma(R)=min(gamma_1(R),gamma_0(R)).
The subscripts indicate whether uv is deleted. There are at most
sixteen possible U and sixteen possible V.

Claim 1 (two-matchings sufficient theorem).
If the data satisfy

    gamma(R)+t+r <= k+2 epsilon,                       (1)

then {u,v} is reducible in the ambient graph. The recipe gives an
edge-disjoint packing S of k+t+epsilon triangles and an explicit X with
|X|=k+3t+r+gamma(R)<=2|S|, meeting all triangles through either hub and
containing every S-edge with both endpoints outside {u,v}.

Claim 2 (side cover formula).
For fixed U covering H', f_u(U) is the minimum size of a vertex cover
Q of J'_u with Q intersect C=U, and it constructs such a Q.

Proof. U covers the core edges. If c is outside U and ac is a residual
edge, Q must contain a, so P_u(U) is forced. Conversely U union P_u(U)
covers every core-A edge and every core edge. Only a_0a_1 can remain.
If absent, no addition is needed. If present and P_u(U) nonempty, it
is already covered. If present and P_u(U) empty, exactly one exclusive
vertex must be added, and either endpoint suffices. This proves the
formula and construction. End of proof.

Claim 3 (exact completion by hub edges).
Among X=R union Z with Z consisting of spokes and possibly uv,
gamma(R) is exactly the minimum |Z| that covers all hub triangles.

Proof. If uv is in Z, it covers every uvc. A remaining hub triangle
uyz is covered by R when yz is in R. Otherwise at least one of uy,uz
must be chosen, so the endpoints of chosen u-spokes must cover J'_u.
This is necessary and sufficient, and the analogous independent
condition holds at v. The two spoke sets and {uv} are disjoint.
Claim 2 gives the exact cost gamma_1(R).

If uv is absent, the same two rim-cover conditions hold, together
with the requirement that at least one of uc,vc be chosen for every
c in C. On the core portions U,V of the two spoke covers this is
U union V=C. Claim 2 gives exactly gamma_0(R). These exhaust all
hub triangles. End of proof.

Proof of Claim 1. Take S to be all uyz for yz in M_u, all vyz for
yz in M_v, all triangles of T, and uvx when epsilon=1.
The matching condition makes spokes at the same hub distinct. Spokes
at different hubs are distinct. Disjointness of the two matchings
makes their rim edges distinct; these are disjoint from E(T).
The triangles of T are edge-disjoint. When uvx is included, excluding
x from both matchings prevents reuse of ux,vx, and no other selected
triangle uses uv. Thus S is edge-disjoint and |S|=k+t+epsilon.

Choose a completion Z of size gamma(R) by Claim 3 and set X=R union Z.
Its parts are disjoint, so |X|=k+3t+r+gamma(R)<=2(k+t+epsilon) by (1).
Claim 3 proves hub-triangle coverage within L. The external S-edges
are exactly M_u union M_v union E(T), all contained in R subset X.

For ambient safety, a triangle through u has its other vertices in
{v} union C union A, so it cannot use an A--B edge or a vertex outside
L. All of its edges are retained in L. The same holds for v.
Therefore all ambient hub triangles are already covered. All members
of S are ambient triangles because L is a subgraph. This proves all
three reducibility conditions. End of proof.

3. NORMALIZATION TO CORE-ONLY EXTRA DELETIONS

Claim 4.
Any local witness (S,X) supported in L can be normalized, keeping S
and not increasing |X|, so that its external cover edges are
M_u union M_v union E(T) union D with D consisting only of extra
core edges. The resulting parameters satisfy (1).

Proof. Decompose S uniquely into its possible triangle uvx, its
u-only triangles, its v-only triangles, and its hub-free triangles T.
The u-only and v-only rim edges form matchings M_u and M_v: a shared
endpoint would reuse a spoke. Their edges are disjoint, and avoid x
if uvx is present. They are also disjoint from E(T).

Let F=M_u union M_v union E(T). External-edge safety forces F subset X.
Consider an external edge e in X minus F which is not a core edge.
Since L has no A--B edges, it belongs to one side, say J_u, with an
endpoint a in A. Replace e by ua, adding ua only if absent. Every
hub triangle using e contains u and a, since a is not adjacent to v.
It therefore meets ua. This preserves coverage without increasing
size, and preserves external-edge safety because e is not in F.
Repeat on both sides until all such edges are replaced.

The remaining external edges are F union D for a core subset D
disjoint from F. The other cover edges are spokes and possibly uv.
Claim 3 replaces them by a minimum completion of no greater size.
The inequality |F union D|+gamma(R)<=2|S| is exactly (1).
End of proof.

This is a normal-form completeness theorem, not an existence proof:
it starts with a witness. Claim 4 alone must not be described as a
human proof that every admissible graph has a witness. The genuinely
restricted assertions concern small T-motif families and restricted D.
Claim 1 gives their independent sufficiency and explicit cover synthesis.

4. ABSORBING A HUB-FREE TRIANGLE

Claim 5 (triangle absorption).
Let (S,X) be a local witness and xyz a hub-free triangle of S.
If h is a hub adjacent to x,y and hx,hy are unused by S, then xyz
can be replaced by hxy while preserving |S| and not increasing |X|.
The number of hub-free triangles decreases by one.

Proof. The edge xy was used only by xyz, and hx,hy were unused.
Hence S'=(S minus {xyz}) union {hxy} is edge-disjoint and |S'|=|S|.
Both xz and yz belong to X by external-edge safety. Put
Z_z={uz : uz is an edge} union {vz : vz is an edge},
X'=(X minus {xz,yz}) union Z_z.
Two distinct external edges are removed and at most two hub edges
are added, so |X'|<=|X|<=2|S'|.

A hub triangle that could become uncovered must use xz or yz. If its
hub is q, it also uses qz, and that edge is in Z_z. Thus coverage is
preserved. The removed edges are no longer used by S' and, by packing
disjointness, were not used by any other packing triangle. The new
triangle's sole external edge xy remains in X'. All other required
external edges remain. These arguments work in the ambient graph by
the local-completeness argument of Claim 1. End of proof.

If z is exclusive, at most one edge is added, so |X'|<=|X|-1. If z
is common, at most two are added. This can create slack even when
the original certificate is tight. The hub h need not be adjacent
to z; it only needs the two free spokes hx,hy.

Claim 6 (necessary saturation after absorption).
Call a neighbor w of h free when hw is unused by S. At termination
of Claim 5, no remaining hub-free triangle contains an edge whose
endpoints are both free neighbors of the same hub. Consequently:
(a) a triangle CCC has at most one free vertex at each hub;
(b) a triangle CCA has at most one free vertex at u, and its two
    common vertices are not both free at v;
(c) a triangle CAA has at most one free vertex at u;
and the symmetric statements hold with B and v.

Proof. Each forbidden edge would enable Claim 5. The pairwise edges
of each listed triangle exist, giving the individual conclusions.
Every absorption decreases the number of hub-free triangles, so the
process terminates. End of proof.

5. VERIFICATION BOUNDARY

The original census and certificate finder are not used. Only saved
records, limited witness transformations, and residual-cover arithmetic
are considered. Coverage of the saved 1144 records is not a new proof
that those records exhaust all admissible local graph orbits.

For restricted coverage, record for each input key the matchings,
optional uvx, T, D, minimizing core subsets, and inequality values.
These reconstruct S,X and permit independent literal validation.
Counts alone do not serve as proof objects.

The mathematical claims above do not depend on computational statistics.
Restricted-family coverage, absorption outcomes, and any lower bound
within a fixed recipe grammar are reported separately after verification.

6. WHY ONLY A SHARED CORE EDGE CAN BUY EXTRA BUDGET

Claim 7 (sharp saving bound for an extra rim deletion).
For an available core edge e not in R,
0 <= gamma(R)-gamma(R union {e}) <= 2.
For an available side rim edge e not in R, the corresponding upper
bound is one. Thus paying for an extra core edge can reduce the total
cover cost by at most one, while paying for an extra side edge can
never reduce that cost.

Proof. Deleting an additional rim edge removes requirements and never
adds any, so gamma cannot increase. Given an optimal hub completion
for R union {e}, restore the requirement at e. If e=xy is a core
edge, add ux and vx to the completion. Every hub triangle based on
e uses one of these two spokes, so the augmented completion works
for R and uses at most two more edges. If e has an exclusive endpoint
a, all hub triangles based on e use the single spoke to a at its
side's hub; adding this spoke suffices. The bounds follow. The extra
rim edge itself costs one, giving the claimed net savings. End of proof.

Consequently, if a fixed packing's forced-rim-only cover exceeds its
allowed budget by d>0, then any cover completion repairing it needs
at least d extra core edges after normalization. This follows by
applying Claim 7 successively, since each such edge gains at most one.
In particular, finding one extra core edge for a deficit-one packing
is sharp for the minimum number of extra core deletions for that
fixed packing; it says nothing about alternative packings in the graph.

B's separate saved-record calculation reports forced-rim-only feasibility
for 1027 records; the other 117 each have deficit exactly one, and
each is repaired by one extra core edge. The positive witnesses and
lower bounds are entirely consistent with Claim 7. These are claims
about completions of the stored packings, not about all packings.
The associated per-record evidence is in round2/B/B_cover_normalform.json.
