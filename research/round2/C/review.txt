Direct proof check. For each edge xy of matching M_u in J_u=L[C∪A], uxy is a triangle; the matching property prevents two such triangles sharing a u-spoke. Likewise M_v gives edge-disjoint v-triangles. The matchings must have disjoint edge sets so uxy and vxy do not share the rim xy. If epsilon=1, uvx is compatible when x lies in C and is not an endpoint of either matching. The hub-free triangles T may share vertices with hub triangles but, if pairwise edge-disjoint and with edges avoiding both matchings, they share no edges. Hence the natural S is an edge-disjoint packing with size |M_u|+|M_v|+|T|+epsilon.

Let R=M_u∪M_v∪E(T). Every S-edge avoiding both hubs is in R. X={uv}∪R∪uQ_u∪vQ_v is a set of L-edges. It meets each uvx via uv. A triangle uxy with x,y in C∪A has rim xy in J_u; if xy∈R it is hit directly, and otherwise Q_u vertex-covers J_u−R, so its u-spoke is hit. The v case is symmetric. Thus all three conditions of the paper's local reducibility criterion hold whenever the displayed inequality holds. The terms in X are disjoint by endpoint type, so |X|=1+|R|+|Q_u|+|Q_v|. If the matchings and T obey the stated edge-disjointness, |R|=|M_u|+|M_v|+3|T|, and the inequality equivalently reads 1+vc(J_u−R)+vc(J_v−R)+|T| <= |M_u|+|M_v|+2epsilon. This reformulation may clarify what each hub-free triangle must save in vertex-cover cost.

E exchange lemma (later candidate relayed by parent): ACCEPT as a conditional transformation of a valid local certificate. Let T=xyz be hub-free, choose h∈{u,v} adjacent to x,y and require hx,hy unused by every triangle of S\{T}. Replace T by hxy. The new triangle shares xy with the removed T, but no edge with any retained triangle: xy was unavailable to them because S was a packing, while hx and hy are free by assumption. Thus |S| is unchanged.

Since T is hub-free, old condition (iii) forces xy,xz,yz∈X. Keep xy; remove xz,yz; add hz for EACH hub h∈{u,v} adjacent to z. The two removed edges are distinct and no other retained packing triangle uses them. Every new packing edge avoiding hubs is xy, which remains in X, so condition (iii) persists. A triangle through a hub that loses its old cover through xz or yz contains z and that hub, hence contains the newly added hub-z spoke. All other previously covered hub triangles remain covered. Consequently condition (ii) persists and |X'|≤|X|−2+2≤2|S'|. Duplicate newly added spokes only lower |X'|. All edges used are L-edges; ambient A--B edges remain irrelevant by local-model completeness.

The exchange applies to CCA and CAA triangles (or their B-side analogues) with at most one new spoke at z if z is exclusive; to CCC triangles it may add both uz and vz. More generally z can have zero, one, or two hub neighbours, and the same proof works. The substantive condition is the existence of a hub h and a pair x,y of T with two free h-spokes. This has NOT been established for every hub-free triangle in every saved witness. The lemma alone proves neither that all hub-free triangles can be removed nor that every admissible graph has an all-hub packing certificate.

E sharpened cover-cost lemma (relayed by parent): ACCEPT as an exact optimization for the stated FIXED packed S and forced rim support, with the normalization described below. No counterexample found.

Normalization. Let F=M_u∪M_v∪E(T), the forced non-hub S-edges. Every admissible X contains F by condition (iii). An extra X-edge of type C--A or A--A participates in hub triangles only through u, since no A vertex is adjacent to v. Replace it by one u-spoke at either endpoint; that spoke meets every u-triangle that the old rim edge met. Likewise C--B/B--B extras can be replaced by one v-spoke. This changes no packing edge and weakly decreases |X| (duplicates can only help). The only extra rim edges worth retaining are core C--C edges, which can cover triangles through both hubs; call their set D⊂E(C)\F. This proves existence of a normalized cover whenever any X exists for this S. It does NOT say the actual stored X already has this form.

Exact spoke cost. Fix R=F∪D. For u, select core spokes at U⊂C. The residual core edges H−R require U to be a vertex cover. Every residual A--C edge with its core endpoint outside U forces its A endpoint into the side-spoke set, giving P_u(U). The single A--A edge, if residual, is covered by any member of P_u(U), since A has exactly two vertices; if P_u(U)=∅ it forces one additional A spoke. Thus f_u(U)=|U|+|P_u(U)|+1_{A--A residual and P_u(U)=∅} is EXACT for fixed U; the v side is symmetric. If uv is selected, all uvx triangles are hit at cost 1 and the two sides optimize independently. If uv is not selected, every c∈C must have uc or vc selected, so U∪V=C. These are exhaustive, giving the proposed gamma(R).

Since F's constituent edges are disjoint, |R|=k+3t+r, while |S|=k+t+epsilon. A normalized X of minimum size has |X|=|R|+gamma(R). Therefore it satisfies |X|≤2|S| exactly when gamma(R)+t+r≤k+2epsilon. This is an if-and-only-if statement for the restricted packed-S/rim-R witness class, not for arbitrary local certificates unless S is first decomposed into M_u,M_v,T and optional uvx and D is allowed to vary. Any pairwise edge-disjoint packing S in L does admit that decomposition, so minimizing over such data can represent existence of a local certificate. The existence of a SHORT structural proof that such data work for all admissible graphs remains wholly separate.

Potential presentation guard: define H=G[C] and residual H−R as deletion of R∩E(C); f_u and f_v should be taken over U,V that cover this residual core graph.
