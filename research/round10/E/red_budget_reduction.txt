ROUND 10 / E
RED-BUDGET COVERS, A NECESSARY MULTIPAGE OBSTRUCTION,
AND A CONDITIONAL TRANSFER TO A UNIVERSAL CONSTANT

Status: reviewed research proof; see research/round10/C/review_E_full.txt.
The requested universal constant below 165/59 is NOT proved here.
All graphs are finite and simple. No maximum-average-degree hypothesis,
bounded-degree hypothesis, numerical enumeration, or saved graph census
is used in any proof below. No literature novelty claim is made.

The proved results are an auxiliary-graph representation, a 9/5 cover
bound for a stronger all-red-private subclass, a general residual cover
bound involving the number of nonprivate red edges, and a local exchange
restriction. A separate red-budget conjecture remains open here. Its
conditional implication for a smaller exact universal constant is proved
explicitly, so it is not confused with an unconditional improvement.

1. WANG'S RESIDUAL AND ITS EXACT SCOPE.

We use the notation of Wang, arXiv:2609.13831v1, Lemma 2.3 and Section 3:
  n=nu(G), P an original maximum triangle packing, O=E(P);
  A a maximum packing of triangles with exactly one old (O) edge;
  a=|A|, G'=G-E(A);
  P' a packing in G' maximizing first its number b of type-2 triangles
     (two old edges), then its total size m;
  H=G'-B(P'), where B(P') consists of all old edges in P';
  S the type-2 triangle family of H;
  Q a maximum packing of S, c=|Q|;
  h the number of Q triangles whose new edge is private in S;
  d the maximum packing size after deleting the two old edges of
     each triangle of Q;
  e=n-a-m>=0.
Color new edges red and old edges blue. Private means contained in exactly
one triangle of the specified family, not merely used once by a packing.

In fact EVERY triangle of H has exactly one red edge:
  * An all-red triangle could augment the original packing P.
  * A two-red triangle in G' could augment A.
  * An all-blue triangle in H avoids every edge of P': all its edges
    are old, and B(P') has been deleted. Adding it preserves the type-2
    count and increases the total size, contrary to the choice of P'.
Thus S is the entire triangle family of H. This last fact is necessary
for the auxiliary graph argument below; a claim only about a selected
one-red subfamily would not suffice.

Every participating red edge of H belongs to a type-2 triangle of P'.
Otherwise a triangle of H using that red edge avoids all edges of P'
and could increase its type-2 count. Consequently the number r of red
edges participating in triangles of H satisfies r<=b.

We import the following valid covers, retaining a+m rather than replacing
it by n:
  (W1) tau(G)<=3n-a;
  (W2) tau(G)<=3n/2+5a/2+3b/2-c+3h/8;
  (W3) tau(G)<=3(n-e)-c-d;
  (W4) tau(G)<=3(n-e)-b+9c/4+3d/2-h/4;
  (W5) tau(G)<=3(n-e)-b+2c+3d.
W1-W4 and their weights 20,8,19,12 are Wang's existing construction.
W5 is its intermediate colored cover, not a new inequality discovered
here. More generally that outer construction gives
  tau(G)<=3(n-e)-b+tau(H).                         (1)
Indeed E(A) together with B(P') has 3a+3m-b edges, and deleting those
edges leaves exactly H; add any triangle cover of H.
The exact retained-slack consequence, proved in the D file cited below,
is
  59 tau(G)<=165n-93e-d-3(c-h-6d)_+.              (2)
Here x_+=max(x,0). We do not count reweighting W1-W4 as progress.

2. AN AUXILIARY GRAPH WHEN ALL PARTICIPATING RED EDGES ARE PRIVATE.

THEOREM A.
Let H be a red-blue graph in which every triangle has exactly one red
edge. Suppose every red edge participating in a triangle is private.
Write p=nu(H)>0 and r for the number of participating red edges. Then
  tau(H)<=2p-p^2/(2r-p).                          (3)
In particular r<=3p implies tau(H)<=9p/5.

Proof. Form an ordinary simple graph J whose vertices are the blue
edges of H. Each triangle of H gives a J edge joining its two blue
edges. Two specified blue edges can lie in at most one triangle, so
J is simple. Global privacy of every participating red edge gives
  |E(J)|=r.
Two H triangles can share a blue edge exactly when their J edges share
a vertex. They cannot share a red edge. Thus
  mu(J)=nu(H)=p.
A vertex cover of J gives a blue-edge triangle cover of H. Conversely,
in any triangle cover of H, replace a chosen red edge by either blue
edge of its unique triangle. This never increases the cover size and
proves
  beta(J)=tau(H),
where beta is the ordinary vertex-cover number.

J is triangle-free. Three pairwise intersecting distinct edges in a
simple graph form either a three-edge star or a triangle. If three
vertices of J formed a triangle, their corresponding blue edges in H
would have one of these forms. A blue triangle violates the assumption
on every H triangle. In a blue star, each pair of leaves is joined by
the red edge of the corresponding H triangle; the three leaves would
therefore form an all-red triangle, also impossible.

Apply the elementary triangle-free matching/cover estimate
  beta(J)<=2p-p^2/(2|E(J)|-p).                    (4)
For completeness, fix a maximum matching M of size p, with unmatched
vertex set U. U is independent. On each matching edge xy, at least one
endpoint has no neighbor in U: distinct unmatched neighbors at the two
ends augment M, and a common unmatched neighbor forms a triangle.
Choose one such endpoint on every matching edge to form Z, |Z|=p.
There are no edges between Z and U, and J[Z] has at most |E(J)|-p edges.
Ordering Z uniformly at random and keeping vertices preceding all
their neighbors gives an independent set I of size at least
  sum_{z in Z} 1/(d_{J[Z]}(z)+1)
  >= p^2/(2|E(J[Z])|+p)
  >= p^2/(2|E(J)|-p).
Remove I from the cover consisting of all 2p matched endpoints.
The result remains a cover, because U and I are independent and there
are no edges between them. This proves (4), hence (3).

The matching/cover estimate (4) is the elementary estimate proved in
research/round10/D/colored_slack_and_private_red_barrier.txt, Section 4.
The additional representation above applies it to every H satisfying
Theorem A, not only to the single-apex cone construction in that file.

3. A GENERAL COVER WITH AN EXPLICIT NONPRIVATE-RED PARAMETER.

THEOREM B.
Let H have exactly one red edge in every triangle. Let Q be a maximum
triangle packing of size c, of which h>0 have private red edges. Let r
be the number of participating red edges, let s be the number of these
that are nonprivate, and let b>=r. Then
  tau(H)<=2c+s-h^2/(2(r-s)-h)
         <=2c+s-h^2/(2(b-s)-h).                  (5)
Both denominators are positive. For h=0 the separately valid crude
statement is tau(H)<=2c+s; no expression 0/0 is used.

Proof. Delete the s nonprivate red edges. Every remaining participating
red edge is private; its unique original triangle survives because that
triangle has no other red edge. The resulting graph H0 thus has exactly
t=r-s participating red edges, all private. Its maximum packing size k
satisfies h<=k<=c: the h original private Q triangles remain, and deletion
cannot increase the maximum packing number. In particular t>=k>=h>0.
Theorem A and adding back the deleted red edges as cover edges give
  tau(H)<=s+2k-k^2/(2t-k).
The function x^2/(2t-x) is increasing on 0<x<=t (its derivative is
x(4t-x)/(2t-x)^2). Hence this is at most
  s+2c-h^2/(2t-h).
Using t<=b-s proves (5). If h=0 and k>0, simply drop the nonnegative
saving from Theorem A; if k=0, the deleted red edges alone cover H.

For Wang's residual, (1) and (5) give the valid additional estimate
  tau(G)<=3(n-e)-b+2c+s-h^2/(2(b-s)-h),           (6)
when h>0. This introduces genuine additional structural information s;
it does not supply a bound on s by itself.

EXACT CRITICAL-PROFILE CONSEQUENCE.
The already enhanced algebraic system W1-W5 still admits
  a=c=h=12n/59, b=36n/59=3c, m=47n/59, d=e=0.
This is only an algebraic parameter point, not an asserted graph.
At this point, for (5) not to exclude tau(H)=2c, it is necessary that
  s >= c^2/(5c-2s).
Since c>0 and s<=b-h=2c, the denominator is positive. Writing x=s/c,
  x(5-2x)>=1,
and therefore
  s/c >= (5-sqrt(17))/4.                         (7)
This is the necessary condition imposed by the NEW cover together with
the old critical parameters. It does not claim the existence of a
residual attaining tau(H)=2c, nor exclude that value when (7) holds.

A convenient rational subcase is: if h=c>0, r<=3c, and s<=c/6, apply
(5) with b=3c and weaken its denominator to 2b-h=5c to obtain
  tau(H)<=2c+c/6-c/5=59c/30.                     (8)
The whole desired class allows s as large as 2c; no proof here reduces
that range to the one in (8).

4. A LOCAL EXCHANGE RESTRICTION FOR THE LARGER CLASS.

LEMMA C (SINGLE CONTACT).
Suppose every triangle of H has exactly one red edge, and Q is a maximum
packing all of whose red edges are private. Put K=B(Q). For a fixed
Q triangle uvx with red edge uv, its two blue edges ux and vx cannot
both belong to triangles meeting K in exactly one edge.

Proof. Suppose T1=uxy and T2=vxz are such triangles. Neither can use any
red edge of Q, by privacy, and each avoids the other blue edges of Q.
If T1,T2 are edge-disjoint, replace uvx by both to augment Q. If they
share an edge, y=z=w and their shared edge is xw (the alternatives
would make one of them uvx, which has two edges in K). If xw is red,
uw and vw are blue, so uvw is a second triangle on private red edge uv.
If xw is blue, uw and vw are red, so uvw is all red. Both cases are
impossible.

This is only a local restriction. Triangles meeting two blue edges of
Q can obstruct choosing one blue edge per Q triangle as a cover. No
global saving is inferred solely from Lemma C.

5. THE REMAINING BUDGETED CLAIM IS A CONJECTURE HERE.

For 0<epsilon<=1, let RB(epsilon) be the following statement:

  If every triangle of a finite simple red-blue graph H has exactly
  one red edge, p=nu(H)>0, some maximum packing has all its red edges
  private, and the number of participating red edges is at most 3p,
  then tau(H)<=(2-epsilon)p.

The quantifier is existence of an all-private maximum packing, not a
claim that every maximum packing has that property. The numerical
diagnostic requested through the parent tests RB(1/100) only on a small
bounded collection. No diagnostic result is used in this proof file.

RB(1/100) IS NOT PROVED HERE. Theorem A proves a stronger-privacy
subclass; Theorem B also allows a small number of other nonprivate red
edges. Neither statement makes the unproved extension automatic.

The following bridge explains exactly why a proof of RB would matter.

LEMMA D (CONDITIONAL DELETION BRIDGE).
Assume RB(epsilon). Let H have one red edge in every triangle, let Q
be maximum of size c with h private red edges, and suppose at most b
red edges participate. Then
  tau(H)<=3(c-h)+(2-epsilon)h+(b-c-2h)_+.         (9)

Proof for h>0. Delete all three edges of each of the c-h nonprivate
Q triangles. The surviving h private Q triangles form a maximum
packing: a larger residual packing, together with the deleted Q
triangles, would exceed c. All h red edges remain private. There are
at most b-(c-h) participating red edges. If more than 3h remain,
delete the excess red edges, choosing them outside the h surviving
Q triangles. There are enough such edges, and this costs at most
(b-c-2h)_+. The h-packing remains maximum, its red edges remain private,
and RB(epsilon) applies. Adding the deleted edges gives (9).
For h=0, using all participating red edges covers H at cost at most b;
the right side 3c+(b-c)_+ is at least b. This handles the empty-packing
edge case without applying RB to p=0.

THEOREM E (EXACT CONDITIONAL UNIVERSAL CONSTANT).
If RB(epsilon) holds for some 0<epsilon<=1, then every finite simple
graph G satisfies
  tau(G)<=(165/59-epsilon/10000)nu(G).            (10)
In particular RB(1/100) would imply the exact universal coefficient
  165/59-1/1000000.
This is a conditional implication, NOT the asserted conclusion of
round ten.

Proof. If n=nu(G)=0, then tau(G)=0. Otherwise normalize every count by
n and write T=tau(G)/n, K0=165/59, Delta=K0-T>=0. In the rest of this
proof all lower-case packing parameters are normalized, so n=1.
Put c0=3-K0=12/59, l=c-h>=0, and let g_i be the right side of W_i
minus T, for i=1,2,3,4. Every g_i is nonnegative. The established
weighted identity is
  20g1+8g2+19g3+12g4=59Delta-93e-d.
Together with (2), it implies
  e<=59Delta/93<=Delta, d<=59Delta,
  g3<=59Delta/19<=4Delta, g4<=59Delta/12<=5Delta,
  d+3(l-6d)_+<=59Delta.                          (11)

The last inequality gives l<=354Delta: if l<=6d use d<=59Delta;
otherwise 3l<=59Delta+17d<=18*59Delta. Solving W3 for c gives
  c=c0+Delta-3e-d-g3,
hence
  c0-65Delta<=c<=c0+Delta,
  h=c-l>=c0-419Delta.                            (12)
Solving W4 for b gives
  b-3c=c0-c+Delta+l/4+3d/2-3e-g4
       <=(65+1+354/4+3*59/2)Delta
       =243Delta.                               (13)

The outer cover (1) and Lemma D give a further valid upper bound
  Wnew=3(1-e)-b+2c+l-epsilon*h+(b-3c+2l)_+.
Subtracting the right side of W4 yields
  Wnew-W4=3l/4-epsilon*h+(b-3c+2l)_+-3d/2.
Since T<=Wnew and W4=T+g4, (11)-(13) imply
  epsilon*h
  <=g4+3l/4+(b-3c+2l)_+-3d/2
  <=(5+3*354/4+243+2*354)Delta
  <=1222Delta.
Together with (12), this gives
  epsilon*c0<=(1222+419epsilon)Delta<=1641Delta,
so
  Delta>=12epsilon/(59*1641)>epsilon/10000.
The last comparison is exact: 120000>59*1641=96819.
This proves the weaker non-strict inequality in (10), including any
parameter boundary such as h=0.

6. A FIXED COUNTEREXAMPLE TO A DIFFERENT PROPOSED INPUT.

The claim that d+h>=eta*c for a uniform eta>0 in every complete
one-red triangle family is false, independently of RB. Take
H=K_{1,2,2} with parts {u},{v,z},{x,y}, red edges uv,uz, and all other
edges blue. Its complete triangle family is
  uvx, uvy, uzx, uzy.
The packing Q={uvx,uzy} has c=2 and is maximum, since every triangle
uses one of the two red edges. Neither packed red edge is private,
so h=0. Deleting B(Q)={ux,vx,uy,zy} removes every triangle, so d=0.
The two red edges form a cover and Q is a packing, hence tau=nu=2.
This example rejects that proposed positive lower bound; it does not
claim to realize all of Wang's preceding global choices or equalities.
