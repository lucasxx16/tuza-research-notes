PRECISE OBSTRUCTION TO A PRIVATE-RED BONUS.
Let R be an arbitrary triangle-free graph on a vertex set V. Add one
new apex z, color all edges of R red, and color every edge zx blue.
The family F of all one-red triangles is exactly
  {zxy: xy in E(R)}.
All red edges are globally private in F. Its packing number equals
the matching number mu(R), because two such triangles share an edge
exactly when their base edges share an endpoint. Its transversal
number equals the vertex-cover number beta(R): apex edges of a
vertex cover suffice, and any transversal choosing red base edges
can replace each chosen red edge by one endpoint's apex edge without
increasing its size.

There are triangle-free graphs with independence number o(|V|).
Then beta(R)=|V|-alpha(R), mu(R)<=|V|/2, so
  tau(F)/nu(F) >= 2-2alpha(R)/|V| ->2.
Consequently no fixed positive bonus delta*u can improve the bound
tau(F)<=2p when all p packed red edges are private, even with the
red graph triangle-free. Here every maximum packing has u=p and
deleting its blue edges leaves no family triangle, so q=0 exactly.

I will give a self-contained probabilistic existence proof for the
triangle-free bases, or cite a verified primary theorem. This barrier
does not rule out a global improvement using how S is embedded in
the preceding old-edge packings. It identifies why a private-red
colored-family bonus alone cannot eliminate the strengthened
critical profile. No numerical computation is needed.
