ROUND 10 C — WANG BASELINE AND ADVERSARIAL SCOPE (2026-10-03)

Primary text read narrowly: Sichen Wang, arXiv:2609.13831v1,
Sections 2–3, https://arxiv.org/html/2609.13831v1 . The established
universal theorem is tau(G)<=165/59 nu(G) for every finite simple graph.
The new target is one fixed rational or real c STRICTLY less than 165/59
valid for every such graph; 2.797 is only a rounded decimal and is not
the comparison threshold.

Colored-family quantifier. Section 2 defines F(H) as ALL triangles of
the red/blue graph H with exactly one red edge. Lemma 2.1 uses that
completeness: if xy is red and ux,uy are blue, then uxy must belong
to F(H), enabling the replacement of one packed triangle by two.
The lemma need not hold for an arbitrary chosen subfamily. Literal
counterexample: take red uv,xy and blue ux,vx,uy,vy,uz,vz; choose
the subfamily {uvx,uvy,uvz}, omitting uxy. Its maximum packing
P={uvx} has uvy surviving deletion of B(P), xy outside R(P), yet
the red edge uv lies in both surviving uvy and uvz. This attacks a
subfamily extension of the lemma, not Wang's complete-family claim.

Maximum versus maximal. In the SAME full F(H), P={uvx} is maximal:
all uv-pages meet uv, uxy meets ux, and vxy meets vx. It is not
maximum, since {uxy,uvz} is edge-disjoint. The same uvy/uvz remain
after deleting B(P), so Lemma 2.1 also fails if "maximum" is weakened
to "maximal." Section 3 deliberately uses a maximum type-1 packing A,
a lexicographically selected P' maximizing first type-2 count and then
total size, and maximum residual packings Q,Q1; the all-pages-maximal
condition of Lemma 2.2 is a different, precisely stated hypothesis.

Global versus sparse. Wang's theorem has no mad or degree hypothesis.
Any proof only for mad(G)<8, bounded degree, or a red/blue subclass
does not meet this round's universal goal without a valid reduction
covering the remaining graphs. An additive estimate tau<=c nu+K with
one constant K uniform over ALL finite graphs, or an estimate tau<=c nu
for ALL graphs with nu above one fixed threshold, DOES imply the
universal coefficient by amplification: apply it to the disjoint
union of t copies of any fixed G, use additivity of tau and nu,
divide by t, and let t grow. A graph-dependent loss that grows
linearly under copying is not removed this way. Mere asymptotics on
one subclass remain insufficient.

Fixed-system barrier. Section 3 produces four bounds (3)–(6) and
combines them with nonnegative weights 20:8:19:12, yielding
59 tau<=165 n-d<=165 nu. The paper's Remark gives the feasible
parameter point a=c=12n/59, b=m=39n/59, d=h=0 at which all four
right sides equal 165n/59. Under ONLY the listed constraints
a+m<=n, d<=c<=b<=m, h<=c, no reweighting or LP solve of those same
four inequalities can improve the universal constant. A genuine route
must strengthen an inequality, add a valid structural constraint that
excludes the extremal tuple, or introduce a different global argument.
The tuple is an abstract feasible point, not a claimed realizable graph.

Do not mistake the particular displayed m=b=39n/59 for an additional
restriction. If one keeps the pre-relaxation third transversal
tau<=3a+3m-c-d instead of replacing a+m by n, choose the equally
feasible abstract tuple a=c=12n/59, b=39n/59, m=47n/59=n-a,
d=h=0. It satisfies b<=m and a+m=n; the first, second, fourth,
and now also the unrelaxed third right-hand sides all remain
165n/59. Thus this single unrelaxation alone cannot beat the bound.

Audit rule for candidates: verify every triangle-family closure needed
for exchanges; distinguish maximum from maximal; check the exact source
of each inequality and any new constraint; derive a uniform coefficient
strictly below 165/59, with disjoint-union amplification when its
hypotheses permit, rather than merely a sparse-class improvement.
Numerical LP status alone is not a mathematical proof.
